# Chapter 15

## The Steward and Time

> **“So teach us to number our days, that we may apply our hearts unto wisdom.”**
> — Psalm 90:12

Time is one of the greatest treasures the King places into the hands of a steward.

Every morning, we receive the same gift: another day.

We may have different possessions, different opportunities, different responsibilities, and different abilities, but every person receives time. The question is not whether we have time. The question is:

**What are we doing with the time the King has entrusted to us?**

A steward does not own time.

He manages it.

And because time cannot be stored, recovered, or purchased back, how we use it reveals something about what we truly value.

---

## The Gift We Cannot Store

Money can be saved.

Resources can be stored.

Possessions can be preserved.

But time cannot.

Once a moment has passed, it becomes part of yesterday.

This is why Paul instructed the believers:

> **“Make the most of every opportunity.”**
> — Ephesians 5:16

Every opportunity carries a moment.

Every assignment has a season.

Every relationship has moments that may never come again.

The wise steward understands that time is not simply something to spend. It is something to **steward faithfully**.

---

## The King Gives Seasons

Ecclesiastes 3:1 says:

> **“For everything there is a season, a time for every activity under heaven.”**

There are seasons of preparation.

Seasons of waiting.

Seasons of building.

Seasons of serving.

Seasons of harvest.

And sometimes, seasons of letting go.

A steward must learn to recognize the season he is in.

One of the mistakes we can make is trying to force tomorrow while neglecting today.

But the King does not ask us to live tomorrow today.

He asks us to be faithful with **today**.

If today is a season of preparation, prepare faithfully.

If today is a season of serving, serve faithfully.

If today is a season of learning, learn faithfully.

If today is a season of waiting, wait faithfully.

The value of a season is not determined by how impressive it looks.

It is determined by how faithfully it is stewarded.

---

## Time Reveals Priorities

We often say that something is important to us.

But our use of time can reveal what we actually prioritize.

Jesus said:

> **“For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”**
> — Matthew 6:21

Although Jesus was speaking about treasure, the principle also challenges the way we use our time.

What receives our attention?

What receives our energy?

What consistently receives our hours?

These questions can reveal what occupies our hearts.

A steward should regularly ask:

**“Am I giving the King my leftovers, or am I giving Him my first and best?”**

God deserves more than whatever remains after everything else has been given our attention.

---

## The Danger of Wasted Time

Not every moment must be productive.

Rest is necessary.

Sleep is necessary.

Celebration is necessary.

Relationship is necessary.

Even Jesus withdrew to quiet places.

The issue is not whether we rest.

The issue is whether we are **wasting what God entrusted to us**.

There is a difference between rest and avoidance.

There is a difference between recreation and distraction.

There is a difference between waiting on God and simply refusing to act.

A steward learns the difference.

Because time is precious, we should not allow temporary distractions to consume the hours that could have been invested in eternal things.

---

## Time and Preparation

Many people want the results of a harvest without embracing the preparation of the season before it.

But preparation requires time.

David spent years being prepared before becoming king.

Joseph spent years in difficult circumstances before stepping into leadership.

Moses spent years in the wilderness before being sent back to Egypt.

Their preparation was not wasted time.

It was part of the King's process.

Sometimes we look at our current season and think:

**“Nothing is happening.”**

But heaven may see something very different.

Perhaps God is developing character.

Perhaps He is teaching patience.

Perhaps He is building wisdom.

Perhaps He is preparing us for responsibilities we are not yet ready to carry.

The steward does not despise preparation.

He understands that **faithfulness in preparation is part of faithfulness to the King.**

---

## The Steward Does Not Live Carelessly

Ephesians 5:15 says:

> **“So be careful how you live. Don’t live like fools, but like those who are wise.”**

Wisdom includes knowing that our days are limited.

We do not know how many days we have been given.

Therefore, we should not live as though time is unlimited.

This does not mean living in fear.

It means living with purpose.

Say what needs to be said.

Do what God has assigned you to do.

Love the people God has placed around you.

Serve while you have the opportunity.

Learn while you have the opportunity.

Give while you have the opportunity.

Build while you have the opportunity.

And most importantly, seek the King while you have the opportunity.

---

## When the Steward Looks Back

One day, every steward will look back at the life he was given.

The question will not simply be:

**“How much did you accomplish?”**

The deeper question is:

**“Were you faithful with what I placed in your hands?”**

The King is not asking us to live someone else's life.

He is asking us to faithfully steward **our assignment**.

We may not have the same amount of time as another person.

We may not have the same opportunities.

We may not have the same resources.

But we are responsible for what has been entrusted to us.

The faithful steward does not compare his assignment with another person's assignment.

He simply asks:

**“Lord, what do You want me to do with the time You have given me?”**

---

## Time and Eternity

Psalm 39:4 gives us a sobering prayer:

> **“Lord, remind me how brief my time on earth will be. Remind me that my days are numbered.”**

This is not a message of hopelessness.

It is a call to wisdom.

Our earthly time is temporary.

But what we do with that time can have eternal significance.

A conversation can encourage someone.

A prayer can strengthen someone.

A lesson can shape a life.

A business can provide for a family.

A ministry can point people toward Christ.

A book can carry truth to someone we may never meet.

A simple act of faithfulness can become part of something much bigger than we can see.

The steward therefore does not ask only:

**“What can I get from this day?”**

He asks:

**“What can I faithfully give to the King through this day?”**

---

## The King Does Not Waste Seasons

There may be seasons in our lives that we would rather forget.

Seasons of failure.

Seasons of waiting.

Seasons of uncertainty.

Seasons when doors seemed closed.

But even difficult seasons can become places where God develops the steward.

Romans 8:28 reminds us that God works all things together for the good of those who love Him and are called according to His purpose.

This does not mean every event is good.

It means the King is capable of working even through difficult seasons.

Therefore, do not despise the season you are in.

Ask:

**“Lord, what are You teaching me here?”**

**“What are You developing in me?”**

**“How can I remain faithful in this season?”**

The steward's responsibility is not to control every season.

It is to remain faithful within it.

---

## A Steward's Daily Question

Before the day begins, the steward can ask:

**“King, what would faithfulness look like today?”**

Perhaps it means finishing an important responsibility.

Perhaps it means spending time with family.

Perhaps it means praying.

Perhaps it means serving someone.

Perhaps it means learning.

Perhaps it means resting.

Perhaps it means finally taking the step God has been prompting you to take.

Faithfulness does not always look dramatic.

Sometimes it looks like ordinary obedience repeated every day.

And those ordinary acts of faithfulness can build an extraordinary legacy.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **Am I intentionally stewarding my time, or am I simply allowing the day to happen?**
2. **What consumes most of my time?**
3. **Are my daily priorities aligned with what I say I value?**
4. **Is there something God has been asking me to do that I keep postponing?**
5. **Am I honoring the current season instead of constantly wishing for another one?**
6. **If today were reviewed by the King, would I see faithfulness in how I used it?**

These questions are not meant to condemn the steward.

They are meant to awaken him.

Because tomorrow is not guaranteed.

But today has been placed in our hands.

---

## The Greatest Example

Jesus understood His assignment.

He knew His time on earth was limited.

Yet He did not live hurriedly.

He did not chase every opportunity.

He did not try to satisfy every demand.

He remained focused on the Father's will.

In John 9:4, Jesus said:

> **“We must quickly carry out the tasks assigned us by the one who sent us.”**

Jesus knew that He had been sent.

His time had purpose.

His life had purpose.

His assignment had purpose.

And the faithful steward learns from his King:

**Do not merely count your days. Make your days count for the Kingdom.**

---

## The Steward's Declaration

I am a steward of the time the King has entrusted to me.

I will not treat my days carelessly.

I will seek wisdom for every season.

I will honor the responsibilities God has placed before me.

I will not waste my life comparing my timeline with someone else's.

I will be faithful in preparation, faithful in waiting, faithful in serving, and faithful in harvest.

I will use my time to love, serve, build, learn, give, and glorify God.

My time belongs to the King.

My days are in His hands.

And while I have today,

**I will be faithful.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of my life,**

Thank You for the gift of time.

Thank You for every morning, every opportunity, every season, and every assignment You have entrusted to me.

Forgive me for the moments I have wasted and the opportunities I have neglected.

Teach me to number my days.

Give me wisdom to recognize the season I am in and courage to be faithful within it.

Help me not to compare my journey with others.

Help me not to live carelessly or become distracted from what truly matters.

Teach me to use my time in a way that honors You.

When I am waiting, teach me patience.

When I am preparing, teach me diligence.

When I am serving, teach me humility.

When I am blessed, teach me gratitude.

And when my season changes, give me wisdom to follow You into the next assignment.

My time is not my possession.

It is an entrusted gift.

So while I have today,

**I give it back to You.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
