# Chapter 16

## The Steward and Money

> **“Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”**
> — Deuteronomy 8:18

Money is one of the areas where stewardship becomes very visible.

We can say that God is the Owner of everything, but the way we handle money reveals whether we truly believe it.

The question of stewardship is not simply:

**“How much money do I have?”**

The deeper question is:

**“How faithful am I with what the King has placed in my hands?”**

Money is not the master of the steward.

It is one of the resources entrusted to him by the Master.

---

## The King Is the Owner

Psalm 24:1 declares:

> **“The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it.”**

Everything belongs to God.

Our income.

Our business.

Our possessions.

Our opportunities.

Our abilities to produce wealth.

Even the strength and wisdom we use to earn are gifts that ultimately come from Him.

This changes the way we see money.

If I believe I am the owner, I can use everything however I want.

But if I understand that I am a steward, I begin to ask:

**“King, how do You want me to manage what You have entrusted to me?”**

That is the difference between ownership thinking and Kingdom thinking.

---

## Money Is a Tool, Not a Master

Money itself is not evil.

The Bible does not teach that possessing resources is automatically wrong.

The danger comes when money takes the place that belongs to God.

Jesus said:

> **“You cannot serve both God and money.”**
> — Matthew 6:24

Money is a useful servant but a terrible master.

It can provide food.

It can support a family.

It can fund a business.

It can help people in need.

It can support ministry.

It can create opportunities.

But when money becomes our master, we begin making decisions based on what benefits our wealth rather than what honors the King.

The steward must therefore keep money in its proper place.

**God is the Master. Money is the resource.**

---

## The Steward Does Not Confuse Provision With Possession

When God provides something, our natural tendency can be to say:

**“This is mine.”**

But the Kingdom mindset says:

**“This has been entrusted to me.”**

There is a difference.

If the King gives a steward a treasury to manage, the steward cannot simply spend the treasury according to his personal desires.

He must remember the purpose of the resources.

Likewise, when God gives us financial provision, we should ask:

* What do I need?
* What responsibilities has God given me?
* What should I save?
* What should I give?
* What should I invest?
* What should I use to serve others?
* What decision would honor the King?

Stewardship brings purpose to provision.

---

## The Principle of Faithfulness

Jesus said:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**
> — Luke 16:10

The Kingdom does not measure faithfulness only by the size of what is entrusted.

A person can have little and still be extremely faithful.

Another person can have much and still manage it poorly.

The amount is not the first test.

**Faithfulness is.**

If I cannot manage ₱1,000 wisely, receiving ₱100,000 will not automatically make me a better steward.

More resources simply create greater responsibility.

That is why the steward must learn faithfulness where he is now.

---

## The Steward Learns Contentment

Paul wrote:

> **“Yet true godliness with contentment is itself great wealth.”**
> — 1 Timothy 6:6

Contentment does not mean refusing to grow.

It means refusing to allow the desire for more to control the heart.

A steward can desire improvement without becoming enslaved to comparison.

He can work toward greater income without making wealth his identity.

He can build a business without allowing business to become his god.

He can pursue success while remembering who gave him the ability to pursue it.

Contentment protects the heart from constantly saying:

**“I will be happy when I have more.”**

The faithful steward learns to say:

**“I am grateful for what the King has entrusted to me today, and I will faithfully manage it while trusting Him for tomorrow.”**

---

## The Danger of Comparison

Money can easily become a measuring stick.

We compare houses.

Cars.

Businesses.

Clothing.

Income.

Achievements.

Lifestyle.

But the Kingdom does not call us to manage someone else's resources.

We are accountable for our own stewardship.

Jesus did not tell Peter to manage John's assignment.

He told Peter to follow Him.

Likewise, the steward should not spend his life looking sideways.

Someone may have more.

Someone may have less.

Someone may have started earlier.

Someone may have received opportunities we never received.

But comparison can distract us from our own assignment.

The question remains:

**“What has the King placed in my hands?”**

---

## Giving Is Part of Stewardship

A steward does not only receive.

He also releases.

Proverbs 11:25 says:

> **“The generous will prosper; those who refresh others will themselves be refreshed.”**

Generosity is one way we demonstrate that money does not own us.

When we give, we acknowledge that our resources are not ultimately ours.

Giving can support ministry.

It can help someone in need.

It can bless a family.

It can become an answer to someone's prayer.

But biblical giving is not about buying God's favor.

We give because we recognize the goodness of the King and because we want what He has entrusted to us to become a blessing.

---

## The Steward Plans

Faithfulness does not mean being careless with money.

A wise steward plans.

Proverbs 21:5 says:

> **“Good planning and hard work lead to prosperity, but hasty shortcuts lead to poverty.”**

Planning is part of stewardship.

A steward should know where his resources are going.

He should distinguish between needs and unnecessary wants.

He should avoid irresponsible debt.

He should prepare for future responsibilities.

He should think before spending.

He should consider whether a financial decision produces long-term value or only temporary satisfaction.

Money requires wisdom because every financial decision is also a stewardship decision.

---

## The Steward Avoids the Trap of Quick Wealth

The desire to become rich quickly can make people vulnerable to foolish decisions.

Proverbs 28:20 says:

> **“A faithful person will be richly blessed, but one eager to get rich will not go unpunished.”**

The Kingdom way is not built on desperation.

The steward does not need to compromise integrity simply to gain more.

He does not need dishonest shortcuts.

He does not need deception.

He does not need to sacrifice character for profit.

Because the steward understands:

**A resource gained through unrighteousness can never become a substitute for faithfulness.**

The King cares not only about what we gain.

He cares about **how we gain it**.

---

## Wealth and Responsibility

More resources mean more opportunities to bless.

But they also mean greater accountability.

Jesus taught that much is required from those who have been given much.

This principle applies beyond money.

Greater income can mean greater responsibility.

Greater business can mean greater responsibility.

Greater influence can mean greater responsibility.

Greater authority can mean greater responsibility.

The Kingdom does not say:

**“The more you have, the more important you are.”**

It teaches us to understand:

**“The more you are entrusted with, the more faithfully you must steward.”**

That is Kingdom responsibility.

---

## The Business Owner Is Still a Steward

A person may own a business legally, but before God, he is still a steward.

The business becomes an opportunity to demonstrate Kingdom values.

How are employees treated?

How are customers treated?

Are promises honored?

Are products represented honestly?

Are finances handled responsibly?

Are suppliers treated fairly?

Are profits used wisely?

Does success produce pride or gratitude?

A Kingdom-minded business owner understands that profit matters, but character matters too.

Because the ultimate question is not:

**“How profitable is my business?”**

It is:

**“Is my business being stewarded in a way that honors the King?”**

---

## The Steward's Financial Heart

Money can expose what is happening inside the heart.

Jesus said:

> **“Wherever your treasure is, there the desires of your heart will also be.”**
> — Matthew 6:21

That means financial stewardship is also heart stewardship.

If money constantly produces fear, perhaps we need to examine our trust.

If money constantly produces pride, perhaps we need to examine our humility.

If money constantly produces greed, perhaps we need to examine our contentment.

If money constantly produces generosity, perhaps we are learning to see resources through Kingdom eyes.

The goal is not merely to have money under control.

The goal is to have **our hearts surrendered to the King.**

---

## When Resources Are Few

Stewardship is not only tested when we have abundance.

Sometimes it is tested when resources are limited.

The faithful steward does not say:

**“I will become faithful when I have more.”**

He becomes faithful with what he has now.

A small amount can still be managed wisely.

A simple business can still operate with integrity.

A modest income can still be stewarded responsibly.

A little can still become a blessing.

Remember the widow who gave two small coins.

Jesus noticed her not because her offering was large, but because of what it represented.

The Kingdom sees faithfulness differently from the world.

---

## When Resources Increase

There is another test that comes when resources increase.

Deuteronomy 8:18 reminds us:

> **“Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”**

Success can make us forget the One who gave us the ability to succeed.

That is why increase requires remembrance.

When the business grows, remember the King.

When income increases, remember the King.

When opportunities multiply, remember the King.

When people recognize your work, remember the King.

When your name becomes known, remember the King.

**Never allow the blessing to make you forget the Blesser.**

---

## The King Looks at Faithfulness

One day, the steward will give an account.

The question will not simply be:

**“How much did you accumulate?”**

The deeper question will be:

**“What did you do with what I entrusted to you?”**

The Parable of the Talents in Matthew 25 reminds us that the Master entrusted different amounts to different servants.

They were not given identical assignments.

But the faithful servants were commended for what they did with what they received.

That is important.

God does not ask you to manage what He gave someone else.

He asks you to be faithful with what **He gave you**.

---

## A Kingdom View of Money

The world says:

**“Get as much as you can.”**

The Kingdom asks:

**“Can you faithfully manage what you have?”**

The world says:

**“Money proves your success.”**

The Kingdom asks:

**“Does your success serve God's purposes?”**

The world says:

**“Spend it all on yourself.”**

The Kingdom asks:

**“How can what you have become a blessing?”**

The world says:

**“More makes you greater.”**

The Kingdom says:

**“More means greater responsibility.”**

This is the heart of stewardship.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **Do I see money as mine or as something entrusted to me by God?**
2. **Do my financial decisions reflect Kingdom values?**
3. **Am I managing what I have faithfully before asking God for more?**
4. **Is money becoming a source of fear, pride, greed, or control in my heart?**
5. **Am I practicing generosity?**
6. **Do I plan wisely for the resources God has entrusted to me?**
7. **If the King examined my finances today, what would my stewardship reveal?**

These questions are not designed to produce condemnation.

They are designed to produce alignment.

Because money is not merely a financial matter.

**It is a stewardship matter.**

---

## The Greatest Treasure

The greatest treasure the steward possesses is not money.

It is the King Himself.

Jesus asked:

> **“And what do you benefit if you gain the whole world but lose your own soul?”**
> — Mark 8:36

We can accumulate possessions and still be empty.

We can build businesses and lose our families.

We can increase our income and decrease our peace.

We can become financially successful and spiritually bankrupt.

Therefore, the steward must never allow temporary treasures to replace eternal treasure.

Money has value.

But it has limits.

God does not.

---

## The Steward's Declaration

I am a steward, not the owner.

Everything I have ultimately belongs to the King.

I will manage money with wisdom, integrity, contentment, generosity, and purpose.

I will not allow money to become my master.

I will not measure my worth by what I possess.

I will not compare my resources with someone else's assignment.

I will be faithful with little and faithful with much.

When resources are few, I will trust God.

When resources increase, I will remember God.

I will use what has been placed in my hands to provide, build, serve, give, and bless.

**The King owns the resources.

I am responsible for the stewardship.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of everything,**

Thank You for every resource You have placed in my hands.

Thank You for the ability to work, create, earn, build, give, and provide.

Forgive me for the times I have treated Your resources as though they belonged entirely to me.

Teach me to manage money with wisdom.

Protect my heart from greed, pride, fear, comparison, and the desire for quick wealth.

Give me discipline to plan.

Give me wisdom to spend.

Give me courage to give.

Give me patience to build.

Give me integrity to earn honestly.

And when You entrust more to me, help me remember that greater provision also means greater responsibility.

May my money never become my master.

May my possessions never become my identity.

May my success never make me forget You.

Everything I have came from You.

Everything I have belongs to You.

And everything You place in my hands,

**I desire to steward faithfully for Your Kingdom.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
