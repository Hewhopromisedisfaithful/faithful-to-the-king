# Chapter 18

## The Steward and Opportunities

> **“Make the most of every opportunity in these evil days.”**
> — Ephesians 5:16

Opportunities are also part of what the King entrusts to a steward.

Sometimes an opportunity looks like a new door.

Sometimes it looks like a responsibility.

Sometimes it comes through a person.

Sometimes through a business.

Sometimes through a conversation.

Sometimes through a problem that needs to be solved.

And sometimes an opportunity does not look like an opportunity at all.

It may look small.

It may look inconvenient.

It may even look like an interruption.

But the wise steward learns to recognize that opportunities can become assignments.

The question is not simply:

**“What opportunity can I get?”**

The better question is:

**“King, which opportunities are You entrusting to me, and what do You want me to do with them?”**

---

## Every Opportunity Carries Responsibility

An open door is not merely a privilege.

It can also be a responsibility.

When a person receives an opportunity to lead, people are affected by his leadership.

When a person receives an opportunity to build a business, customers and employees may be affected.

When someone receives an opportunity to speak, listeners may be influenced.

When someone receives an opportunity to serve, someone else's life may be helped.

Therefore, opportunities should never be viewed only through the question:

**“What will I gain?”**

The steward also asks:

**“Who will be affected by what I do with this opportunity?”**

Kingdom thinking looks beyond personal benefit.

---

## The Difference Between an Open Door and God's Assignment

Not every open door is automatically God's will.

Some opportunities are simply opportunities.

Others may look attractive but lead us away from the King's purpose.

Still others may require us to wait.

This is why discernment matters.

Proverbs 3:5–6 teaches us:

> **“Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take.”**

The steward does not run through every open door.

He seeks the King.

Sometimes the wisest answer to an opportunity is:

**“Yes.”**

Sometimes it is:

**“Not yet.”**

And sometimes it is:

**“No.”**

Faithfulness is not measured by how many opportunities we accept.

It is measured by whether we faithfully follow God's direction.

---

## Preparation Before Opportunity

Many people pray for opportunity.

But fewer people prepare for it.

A steward should understand that preparation is part of stewardship.

If you are asking God for a larger responsibility, prepare yourself for greater responsibility.

If you want to lead, develop your character.

If you want to build a business, learn how to manage one.

If you want to teach, study.

If you want to serve people, develop patience.

If you want to handle greater resources, learn to manage what you already have.

The opportunity may arrive suddenly.

But preparation usually happens quietly.

Joseph's years of preparation in difficult circumstances eventually positioned him for leadership in Egypt.

The opportunity came publicly.

The preparation happened privately.

---

## David Was Ready When the Moment Came

David did not suddenly become courageous when Goliath appeared.

His previous experiences had prepared him.

He had already faced danger while protecting his father's sheep.

When the opportunity came to confront Goliath, David could remember what God had already done.

He said:

> **“The Lord who rescued me from the claws of the lion and the bear will rescue me from this Philistine!”**
> — 1 Samuel 17:37

What looked like an ordinary season of shepherding had prepared David for an extraordinary assignment.

That teaches us something important:

**The opportunity you are waiting for may be connected to the faithfulness you are practicing today.**

Do not despise today's preparation.

It may be preparing you for tomorrow's responsibility.

---

## Opportunity Often Finds the Faithful Working

The Bible contains many moments where people were doing ordinary things when extraordinary opportunities appeared.

David was tending sheep.

Elisha was plowing.

The disciples were working as fishermen.

Ruth was faithfully gathering grain.

They were not sitting around saying:

**“I am waiting for my big moment.”**

They were working.

They were serving.

They were being faithful.

The steward should therefore be careful about becoming so focused on the next opportunity that he neglects the current assignment.

Sometimes the best preparation for tomorrow is simply being faithful today.

---

## Do Not Despise Small Opportunities

We often imagine opportunity as something large.

A major promotion.

A large business.

A significant platform.

A life-changing connection.

But some of the most important opportunities begin very small.

An opportunity to encourage someone.

An opportunity to help.

An opportunity to learn.

An opportunity to apologize.

An opportunity to serve.

An opportunity to improve something.

An opportunity to begin a small project.

An opportunity to demonstrate integrity.

Jesus said:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**
> — Luke 16:10

The steward should never say:

**“This is too small for me.”**

Small opportunities can reveal the heart that will eventually handle larger ones.

---

## Opportunity and Courage

There are moments when opportunity requires courage.

The steward may recognize what needs to be done but feel afraid to take the first step.

Fear says:

**“What if I fail?”**

Faith asks:

**“What if God is leading me?”**

Courage does not mean that fear disappears.

It means obedience becomes greater than fear.

Joshua was told:

> **“Be strong and courageous! Do not be afraid or discouraged. For the Lord your God is with you wherever you go.”**
> — Joshua 1:9

Joshua had a responsibility before him.

God did not promise that the assignment would be effortless.

He promised His presence.

Sometimes that is what the steward needs most.

Not the guarantee of an easy road.

But the assurance that the King is with him.

---

## Opportunity and Wisdom

Courage without wisdom can become recklessness.

The steward therefore needs both.

Proverbs 4:7 says:

> **“Getting wisdom is the most important thing you can do. And whatever else you do, get good judgment.”**

Before accepting an opportunity, ask:

**Does this align with God's Word?**

**Does it align with my current responsibilities?**

**Do I have the capacity to carry it faithfully?**

**What will this require from me?**

**Who will be affected?**

**What are the consequences?**

**Have I prayed about it?**

**Have I sought wise counsel?**

These questions do not demonstrate lack of faith.

They demonstrate responsible stewardship.

---

## Not Every Opportunity Should Be Accepted

A steward can become overwhelmed by saying yes to everything.

Every invitation.

Every project.

Every request.

Every business idea.

Every opportunity.

But every yes requires resources.

Time.

Energy.

Attention.

Money.

Responsibility.

Therefore, sometimes saying **no** is also stewardship.

You cannot faithfully carry every assignment.

Jesus Himself did not respond to every demand by doing everything everyone wanted.

He remained committed to the Father's will.

The steward must learn that:

**A good opportunity can still be the wrong opportunity for this season.**

---

## Opportunity and Timing

There is a difference between the right opportunity and the right time.

Ecclesiastes 3:1 reminds us:

> **“For everything there is a season, a time for every activity under heaven.”**

Something may be good but premature.

A business may be good but the timing may not be right.

A relationship may be valuable but require patience.

A leadership role may be possible but require more preparation.

A project may be worthwhile but need to wait until responsibilities are properly aligned.

The steward should not force what God is asking him to wait for.

**Timing is also part of stewardship.**

---

## Closed Doors Are Not Always Failure

Sometimes we pray for an opportunity and the door closes.

We may become disappointed.

We may ask:

**“Why did God allow this to happen?”**

But a closed door does not automatically mean failure.

Sometimes it is protection.

Sometimes it is redirection.

Sometimes it is preparation for something else.

Paul experienced moments when his plans changed.

Acts 16:6–7 describes how the Holy Spirit prevented Paul and his companions from entering certain regions.

They had plans.

But God redirected them.

The steward must therefore learn to surrender both open doors and closed doors to the King.

An open door should not make us proud.

A closed door should not make us hopeless.

Both can become part of God's direction.

---

## Opportunity Through People

Some opportunities come through relationships.

A conversation can introduce an idea.

A mentor can provide wisdom.

A customer can reveal a need.

A friend can open a door.

A stranger can become part of an important moment.

This is another reason why relationships matter in stewardship.

We should treat people with honor because we do not know how God may use a relationship in our journey.

But we must also be careful not to manipulate relationships for personal advantage.

People are not stepping stones.

They are people created by God.

The Kingdom steward does not ask:

**“What can this person do for me?”**

He asks:

**“How can I honor and serve this person?”**

---

## Opportunity and Integrity

An opportunity that requires us to compromise integrity is not worth accepting.

The world may say:

**“Take the shortcut.”**

**“Nobody will know.”**

**“Everyone does it.”**

But the steward answers:

**“The King knows.”**

Proverbs 10:9 says:

> **“People with integrity walk safely, but those who follow crooked paths will be exposed.”**

Never sacrifice character for an opportunity.

A door opened through dishonesty can become a trap.

A promotion gained through manipulation can damage your character.

A business built through deception can destroy trust.

A relationship created through manipulation is not Kingdom stewardship.

The steward remembers:

**The opportunity is temporary.

Character remains.**

---

## Opportunity and Service

The greatest opportunities are not always the ones that make us more visible.

Sometimes the greatest opportunity is simply to serve.

Jesus demonstrated this when He washed His disciples' feet.

John 13:14–15 shows Jesus using His position to serve rather than to demand service.

That is Kingdom leadership.

The King Himself demonstrated that greatness is connected with service.

Therefore, when an opportunity gives you greater authority, ask:

**“How can I serve more?”**

Not:

**“How can more people serve me?”**

Greater authority should produce greater responsibility.

---

## The Opportunity to Begin Again

One of God's beautiful gifts is the opportunity to begin again.

We may have wasted opportunities.

We may have made poor decisions.

We may have delayed obedience.

We may have allowed fear to stop us.

But failure does not have to become the final chapter.

Peter denied Jesus.

Yet Jesus restored him and entrusted him with responsibility.

John Mark experienced failure in ministry.

Yet later Paul considered him useful for ministry.

The Bible repeatedly shows us that God can restore and redeploy people.

Therefore, if you have missed an opportunity, do not remain trapped in regret.

Learn.

Repent where necessary.

Receive God's grace.

Then ask:

**“King, what should I faithfully do with the opportunity that is in front of me now?”**

---

## The Steward Must Recognize the Moment

Jesus criticized people who could interpret the weather but failed to recognize what God was doing in their time.

There are moments when the steward must recognize:

**“This is a moment that requires action.”**

Not every moment should be analyzed forever.

There comes a time when preparation must become action.

Faith must become obedience.

Knowledge must become practice.

Planning must become execution.

The steward must know when to prepare and when to move.

That requires discernment.

---

## Opportunity Is Not the Same as Success

We often define opportunity by the result.

If something succeeds, we call it a good opportunity.

If it fails, we assume it was a bad opportunity.

But the steward's responsibility is not always to control the outcome.

Sometimes faithfulness means taking the right step even when the result is uncertain.

Noah built the ark before the rain.

Abraham left his homeland without knowing every detail of the journey.

The disciples followed Jesus without knowing everything that would happen.

Faith does not require complete knowledge of the future.

It requires trust in the One who holds the future.

---

## The King Entrusts Opportunities for a Purpose

An opportunity may be given not merely to improve our own lives but to accomplish something larger.

Perhaps a business opportunity creates employment.

Perhaps financial increase enables generosity.

Perhaps leadership creates an environment where others can grow.

Perhaps a platform allows truth to reach people.

Perhaps a difficult assignment develops character.

Perhaps a connection introduces someone to a person who changes their life.

We may not always see the full purpose immediately.

But the steward can still be faithful.

The King sees the whole picture.

We see only our part.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **What opportunities has the King placed before me in this season?**
2. **Am I prepared for the opportunities I am praying for?**
3. **Am I saying yes to too many things?**
4. **Is there an opportunity I know I should act on but fear has stopped me?**
5. **Have I mistaken every open door for God's direction?**
6. **Am I willing to accept a closed door without losing faith?**
7. **Am I protecting my integrity when opportunities arise?**
8. **How can I use my opportunities to serve others rather than simply benefit myself?**

These questions help the steward distinguish between opportunity and assignment.

Not every door needs to be entered.

Not every invitation needs to be accepted.

Not every possibility needs to become a project.

The faithful steward seeks the King first.

---

## The Greatest Opportunity

There is one opportunity greater than every business opportunity, financial opportunity, career opportunity, or earthly success:

**The opportunity to know and serve God.**

Jesus asked:

> **“And what do you benefit if you gain the whole world but lose your own soul?”**
> — Mark 8:36

The greatest opportunity is not becoming famous.

It is becoming faithful.

It is knowing the King.

It is serving His purposes.

It is using our temporary opportunities for eternal purposes.

Everything else eventually passes.

But what is done for God carries eternal significance.

---

## The Steward's Declaration

I am a steward of every opportunity the King places before me.

I will not chase every open door.

I will seek the King's direction.

I will prepare before opportunity arrives.

I will recognize small opportunities and treat them with faithfulness.

I will not allow fear to bury what God has placed before me.

I will not compromise integrity for advancement.

I will learn to accept both open doors and closed doors with trust.

I will understand that greater opportunities bring greater responsibility.

I will use opportunities not only for personal gain but also for service and Kingdom purpose.

I will not live obsessed with my next opportunity.

I will be faithful with the opportunity I have today.

**When the King opens a door, I will walk through it with humility.

When He closes a door, I will trust His wisdom.

And in every season,

I will remain faithful to the King.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of my opportunities,**

Thank You for every door You have opened and every opportunity You have placed before me.

Give me wisdom to recognize what comes from You.

Give me discernment when opportunities look attractive but may not belong to my assignment.

Teach me to prepare faithfully before greater responsibility arrives.

Give me courage when You call me to move.

Give me patience when You call me to wait.

Give me humility when You give me success.

Give me peace when a door closes.

Protect me from chasing opportunities that will pull me away from Your purpose.

Protect my integrity when advancement comes.

Help me remember that people are not stepping stones and opportunities are not merely for personal gain.

Use every opportunity You entrust to me to bless others and advance what is good and pleasing in Your sight.

May I never become so focused on tomorrow's door that I neglect today's assignment.

And when You place an opportunity in my hands,

help me steward it faithfully.

**My opportunities belong to You.

My future belongs to You.

And my response belongs to me.

So I choose faithfulness.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
