# Chapter 22

## The Steward and Influence

> **“In the same way, let your good deeds shine out for all to see, so that everyone will praise your heavenly Father.”**
> — Matthew 5:16

Every steward has influence.

We may not always recognize it, but our words, decisions, attitudes, habits, and example affect the people around us.

A parent influences a child.

A leader influences a team.

A teacher influences students.

A business owner influences employees and customers.

A friend influences another friend.

A believer influences the people who watch his or her life.

Influence is therefore not merely a privilege.

**It is an entrusted responsibility.**

The question is not simply:

**“How many people know me?”**

The better question is:

**“What happens to people because of the influence God has entrusted to me?”**

---

## 1. Influence Is Entrusted, Not Owned

Just as money, time, possessions, opportunities, and authority belong ultimately to God, influence also belongs to Him.

We may have influence because of our position, experience, resources, personality, relationships, achievements, or simply because people trust us.

But none of these things make us the ultimate owner.

They are opportunities placed into our hands.

That means influence should never become a reason for pride.

Paul reminded the Corinthians:

> **“For what gives you the right to make such a judgment? What do you have that God hasn’t given you?”**
> — 1 Corinthians 4:7

Everything we have received came from somewhere.

Even the ability to influence others is ultimately a gift.

The faithful steward therefore says:

**“Lord, whatever influence You have allowed me to have, teach me to use it for Your purposes.”**

---

## 2. Influence Is Not the Same as Popularity

It is easy to confuse influence with being popular.

Popularity asks:

**“How many people like me?”**

Influence asks:

**“How am I affecting people?”**

A person can have thousands of followers and use that influence poorly.

Another person may have only a few people around them but profoundly influence their lives.

A mother raising children may never become famous, yet her influence may shape generations.

A mentor may teach one young person, and that person may later influence hundreds.

A faithful steward does not obsess over the size of the audience.

He focuses on the quality of the influence.

Jesus Himself spent much of His earthly ministry investing deeply in a relatively small group of disciples.

Kingdom influence is not measured merely by numbers.

It is measured by **faithfulness to the people God places within our reach.**

---

## 3. Our Example Speaks Before Our Words

People often learn more from what we do than from what we say.

Paul told Timothy:

> **“Be an example to all believers in what you teach, in the way you live, in your love, your faith, and your purity.”**
> — 1 Timothy 4:12

This is especially important for anyone who wants to influence others spiritually.

We cannot consistently teach one thing while living another and expect our influence to remain healthy.

If we speak about integrity but practice dishonesty, our example contradicts our message.

If we speak about forgiveness but constantly hold grudges, people notice.

If we encourage generosity but live only for ourselves, people notice.

If we speak about serving others but demand to be served, people notice.

Our lives become part of the message.

The steward therefore asks:

**“Does my life support what my mouth teaches?”**

---

## 4. Influence Begins in the Closest Relationships

Sometimes we dream about influencing many people while neglecting the people closest to us.

But stewardship begins with what is already within our reach.

Our family sees us when the public does not.

Our closest friends know our habits.

The people we work with observe our attitude.

Those closest to us often see whether our faith is genuine.

Therefore, influence should begin at home and in our immediate relationships.

If God gives us a larger platform later, we should not assume that a larger audience automatically means greater faithfulness.

**Greater visibility does not replace greater character.**

In fact, greater visibility can expose character that was already there.

---

## 5. Words Are Part of Our Influence

Our words carry weight.

Proverbs 18:21 says:

> **“The tongue can bring death or life; those who love to talk will reap the consequences.”**

A steward must therefore learn to manage speech.

Our words can encourage or discourage.

They can restore or wound.

They can build trust or destroy it.

They can point people toward wisdom or toward foolishness.

A careless sentence can remain in someone's memory for years.

A timely word of encouragement can also remain with someone for years.

This does not mean we must speak perfectly.

It means we should recognize the responsibility attached to our words.

Before speaking, we can ask:

**Is it true?**

**Is it necessary?**

**Is it loving?**

**Will it build up?**

**Does it honor the King?**

---

## 6. Influence Must Never Become Manipulation

Influence becomes dangerous when we use it to control people for personal advantage.

A steward should never use trust, authority, status, money, spiritual position, or relationships to manipulate others.

Peter instructed church leaders:

> **“Don’t lord it over the people assigned to your care, but lead them by your own good example.”**
> — 1 Peter 5:3

This is a powerful Kingdom principle.

Leadership is not ownership.

Influence is not control.

Trust is not permission to exploit.

If people follow us, we must remember that they are not possessions.

They belong to God.

Our responsibility is to serve them faithfully, not to make them dependent on us for our own glory.

---

## 7. The Purpose of Influence Is to Point Beyond Ourselves

Jesus said:

> **“Let your good deeds shine out for all to see, so that everyone will praise your heavenly Father.”**
> — Matthew 5:16

Notice where the glory ultimately goes.

**The Father.**

This is the heart of Kingdom influence.

If our influence causes people to admire us but never points them toward what is good, true, and God-honoring, we have misunderstood stewardship.

The faithful steward does not ask:

**“How can I make everyone notice me?”**

He asks:

**“How can my influence bring glory to the King?”**

Our influence should not become a monument to ourselves.

It should become a window through which people can see something of God's goodness.

---

## 8. Influence Requires Responsibility

The greater the influence, the greater the responsibility.

A careless person may damage only a small circle.

But when a person has a large audience, careless decisions can affect many more people.

That is why increased influence should produce increased humility.

Jesus said:

> **“When someone is given much, much will be required in return.”**
> — Luke 12:48

This principle applies to stewardship.

More influence means more people may be watching.

More people watching means our example carries greater weight.

More responsibility means we must become more intentional about our character.

We should never pray only:

**“Lord, increase my influence.”**

We should also pray:

**“Lord, increase my character so I can faithfully carry whatever influence You entrust to me.”**

---

## 9. Influence Can Be Quiet

Not all influence is public.

Some of the greatest influence happens in places nobody sees.

A parent praying for a child.

A friend listening when someone is struggling.

An employee refusing to participate in dishonesty.

A business owner treating workers fairly.

A believer quietly helping someone in need.

A person choosing forgiveness instead of revenge.

These actions may never become public stories.

But they matter.

The Kingdom does not require an audience for faithfulness to have value.

God sees what people do not see.

Therefore, do not underestimate the influence of a faithful life.

You may not know how many people are learning from your example.

---

## 10. Be Careful What You Pass On

Influence is powerful because it can be transferred.

People often reproduce what they repeatedly observe.

A leader can pass on integrity or corruption.

A parent can pass on faith or fear.

A teacher can pass on wisdom or prejudice.

A friend can pass on discipline or destructive habits.

This means every steward should ask:

**“What am I reproducing in the people around me?”**

If we have influence over younger people, this question becomes even more important.

We are not only living for today.

We are helping shape tomorrow.

The faithful steward seeks to leave behind principles that honor God rather than merely leaving behind a name.

---

## 11. Influence and Humility Must Walk Together

Influence can quietly feed pride.

When people praise us, listen to us, follow us, or seek our advice, it can become tempting to believe that we are more important than others.

But Scripture repeatedly calls us toward humility.

Philippians 2:3 says:

> **“Don’t be selfish; don’t try to impress others. Be humble, thinking of others as better than yourselves.”**

Humility does not mean pretending that our influence does not exist.

It means remembering where it came from and why it was given.

The steward can acknowledge influence without worshiping it.

He can lead without becoming arrogant.

He can receive honor without making honor his identity.

He can be trusted without believing that he owns the trust.

---

## 12. When Influence Is Lost

There may be seasons when our influence decreases.

A position may change.

A business may become smaller.

A ministry role may end.

People may move away.

An audience may disappear.

Someone else may take our place.

When that happens, the steward must remember:

**The King has not changed.**

Our identity was never supposed to depend on the size of our influence.

John the Baptist demonstrated this beautifully when his followers began turning toward Jesus.

John said:

> **“He must become greater and greater, and I must become less and less.”**
> — John 3:30

That is a remarkable picture of surrendered stewardship.

If God increases our influence, we steward it.

If God changes our assignment, we steward the new assignment.

If a season of influence ends, we remain faithful to the King.

**The assignment may change. The Owner does not.**

---

# The Steward's Perspective

The world may ask:

**“How many people can you influence?”**

The steward asks:

**“How faithfully can I serve the people God has placed within my reach?”**

The world may seek followers.

The steward seeks faithfulness.

The world may use influence for personal advancement.

The steward uses influence for service.

The world may celebrate visibility.

The steward values character.

The world may say:

**“Build your own name.”**

The Kingdom reminds us:

**“Whatever you do, do it for the glory of God.”**

Influence is not ultimately about making people remember our name.

It is about faithfully representing the King while we have the opportunity.

---

# Heart Check

Ask yourself honestly:

1. **Who has God placed within my sphere of influence?**
2. **What does my daily example teach those people?**
3. **Do my words build people up or tear them down?**
4. **Have I ever used influence to manipulate or control others?**
5. **Do I seek recognition more than faithfulness?**
6. **Am I using my influence to serve or mainly to benefit myself?**
7. **What values am I passing on to the people who watch my life?**
8. **If God increased my influence, would my character be ready to carry it?**

Influence is not something to possess.

It is something to **steward**.

---

# Steward's Declaration

**I am a steward of the influence the King has entrusted to me.**

I will not use influence to glorify myself.

I will use it to serve, encourage, guide, and build others up.

My words will be handled with wisdom.

My example will support my testimony.

I will not manipulate those who trust me.

I will remain humble when people honor me.

I will remain faithful when nobody notices me.

If the King increases my influence, I will receive it with humility and greater responsibility.

If He changes my influence or closes a season, I will continue serving Him faithfully.

I do not need a large audience to make my life count.

**Wherever the King has placed me, I will faithfully represent Him.**

May my influence point people beyond me and toward the King.

---

# Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of all,**

Thank You for every person You have allowed me to influence.

Forgive me for the times I have sought recognition, approval, or attention instead of seeking to honor You.

Teach me to steward influence with humility.

Guard my words.

Guard my motives.

Guard my example.

Help me never to use trust as a weapon or influence as a means of control.

Give me wisdom to know when to speak, when to listen, when to lead, and when to step aside.

Help me remember that the people around me belong to You.

If You increase my influence, increase my character.

If You entrust more people to me, give me greater humility and greater responsibility.

And if my influence becomes smaller, remind me that my value has never depended on how many people see me.

May my life point people toward You.

May my work, words, relationships, leadership, and example bring glory to the King.

**I am not the owner of influence.

I am its steward.

And while the King allows me to influence others, may I faithfully represent Him.**

In Jesus' name,
**Amen.**
