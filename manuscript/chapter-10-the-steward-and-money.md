# PART II — THE STEWARD

## Chapter 10 — The Steward and Money

Money is one of the clearest places where stewardship becomes practical.

We use money almost every day.

We earn it.

We spend it.

We save it.

We give it.

We make decisions because of it.

And because money touches so many areas of life, it can also reveal what is happening inside our hearts.

The Bible does not teach that money itself is evil.

Instead, Scripture warns us about **loving money** and allowing it to become our master.

Jesus said:

> “You cannot serve God and be enslaved to money.”  
> — Matthew 6:24

The issue is not simply how much money we have.

The deeper issue is:

**Who owns our heart?**

### 1. Money Belongs to the King

We have already established the foundation of this book:

**Everything belongs to the King.**

That includes our material resources.

David declared:

> “Everything we have has come from you, and we give you only what you first gave us!”  
> — 1 Chronicles 29:14

This changes the way we see money.

Our income is not merely something we earned independently of God.

Our ability to work is a gift.

Our opportunities are gifts.

Our skills are gifts.

Our strength is a gift.

Our ability to produce wealth ultimately comes from God.

Deuteronomy says:

> “Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”  
> — Deuteronomy 8:18

Therefore, money should be handled with gratitude and responsibility.

### 2. Money Is a Trust

If God is the Owner, then money becomes a trust.

The question is no longer simply:

**“How much money do I have?”**

It becomes:

**“How faithfully am I managing what the King has entrusted to me?”**

A steward understands that money has purposes.

Some money provides for legitimate needs.

Some may be saved for future responsibilities.

Some may be invested wisely.

Some may be used to help others.

Some may support God's work.

The specific allocation may differ according to each person's circumstances.

But the principle remains:

**Money should serve God's purposes rather than become our master.**

### 3. Money Tests the Heart

Jesus connected money with the condition of the heart.

> “Wherever your treasure is, there the desires of your heart will also be.”  
> — Matthew 6:21

What we consistently do with money can reveal what we value.

If money becomes our greatest source of security, it can replace trust in God.

If money becomes our source of identity, it can replace our identity in Christ.

If money becomes our measure of success, we can begin comparing ourselves with others.

If money becomes our master, we may compromise our values to obtain more.

But when money remains a tool under the King's authority, it can become a blessing rather than a master.

### 4. The Danger of Loving Money

Paul warned Timothy:

> “The love of money is the root of all kinds of evil.”  
> — 1 Timothy 6:10

Notice the problem:

**the love of money.**

Money itself is not identified as the root.

The danger is allowing the desire for wealth to become controlling.

A person can have little money and still be consumed by greed.

A person can have significant resources and still remain generous and content.

Therefore, stewardship cannot be measured simply by someone's bank balance.

The heart must also be examined.

The question is:

**Does money serve me under God's direction, or have I begun serving money?**

### 5. Contentment Protects the Steward

Contentment is an important part of financial stewardship.

Paul wrote:

> “Yet true godliness with contentment is itself great wealth.”  
> — 1 Timothy 6:6

Contentment does not mean refusing to improve our circumstances.

It means refusing to make possessions the foundation of our happiness.

A steward can work hard for greater provision while remaining thankful for what is already available.

A steward can pursue legitimate financial growth without allowing greed to control the heart.

A steward can desire improvement without despising the present assignment.

Contentment says:

**“I can be grateful today while faithfully preparing for tomorrow.”**

### 6. Faithfulness in Financial Matters

Jesus gave a strong connection between financial stewardship and spiritual trustworthiness.

> “And if you are not faithful with other people’s money, why should you be trusted with money of your own?”  
> — Luke 16:12

Financial faithfulness includes honesty.

It includes paying what we genuinely owe.

It includes avoiding dishonest gain.

It includes resisting unnecessary waste.

It includes planning wisely.

It includes generosity.

It includes learning to distinguish needs from wants.

It includes refusing to allow financial pressure to destroy our integrity.

A steward does not need to be wealthy to be faithful.

**Faithfulness can begin with the smallest amount.**

### 7. Generosity Is Part of Stewardship

The King does not entrust resources merely for personal consumption.

Scripture repeatedly teaches generosity.

Paul instructed believers:

> “You should remember the words of the Lord Jesus: ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”  
> — Acts 20:35

Generosity recognizes that we are channels, not final owners.

When God provides, we can become a means through which provision reaches others.

This does not mean giving irresponsibly.

Biblical generosity is not careless stewardship.

A steward must still act wisely.

But generosity reminds us that money exists for more than ourselves.

The question becomes:

**“Who can be blessed through what God has placed in my hands?”**

### 8. Honoring God With What We Have

Proverbs teaches:

> “Honor the Lord with your wealth and with the best part of everything you produce.”  
> — Proverbs 3:9

Honoring God with money means recognizing Him as the source and Owner.

It affects how we earn.

It affects how we spend.

It affects how we save.

It affects how we give.

It affects how we make financial decisions.

Financial stewardship is therefore not merely a mathematical exercise.

It is a spiritual responsibility.

Every peso can become an opportunity to demonstrate faithfulness.

Not because every peso must be spent on religious activity, but because every peso should be managed under the King's authority.

### 9. Money Must Remain a Servant

Money is useful when it remains a tool.

It becomes dangerous when it becomes a master.

A faithful steward can say:

**“Money is useful, but it is not my god.”**

**“Money can provide, but God is my ultimate Provider.”**

**“Money can create opportunities, but it does not define my worth.”**

**“Money can be saved, invested, spent, and given—but it must never own my heart.”**

The goal is not simply to have money.

The goal is to remain faithful while handling money.

## Kingdom Principle

**Money is a trust from the King, not a master over the steward.**

The faithful steward:

- earns honestly,
- manages wisely,
- avoids greed,
- practices contentment,
- gives generously,
- plans responsibly,
- and remembers the true Owner.

The amount may differ from person to person.

The responsibility remains:

**Be faithful with what the King has entrusted.**

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

**What does my relationship with money reveal about my heart?**

Do I see money as God's provision or as my ultimate security?

Am I grateful for what I have?

Am I honest in how I earn?

Am I wise in how I spend?

Am I responsible with what I owe?

Am I willing to give?

Does having more make me more generous or more possessive?

If I lost some of what I have, would I still trust God?

These questions help reveal whether money is in our hands—or whether our hearts have fallen into its hands.

## Faithful Response

King, everything I have ultimately comes from You.

Teach me to handle money as a faithful steward.

Give me wisdom to earn honestly.

Give me discipline to manage wisely.

Give me contentment when I have little.

Give me humility when I have much.

Protect my heart from greed.

Protect me from making money my security, identity, or master.

Teach me to save responsibly, spend wisely, give generously, and honor You with what You provide.

May every financial decision remind me that I am not the owner.

**I am Your steward.**

And may the money You place in my hands never become more important than the King who placed it there.

- Matthew 6:24 — We cannot serve both God and money as masters.
- 1 Timothy 6:6 — Godliness with contentment is great wealth.
- 1 Timothy 6:10 — The love of money can lead people into many kinds of evil.
