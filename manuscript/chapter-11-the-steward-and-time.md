# PART II — THE STEWARD

## Chapter 11 — The Steward and Time

Time is one of the most valuable things the King entrusts to us.

Money can be earned again. Possessions can sometimes be replaced. Opportunities may come again in different forms. But a day that has passed cannot be recovered.

That is why stewardship is not only about how we handle money or possessions. It is also about how we use the days, hours, opportunities, and seasons God has placed in our hands.

The question of a faithful steward is not simply, **“How busy was I?”**

It is:

**“Did I use the time the King entrusted to me according to His purpose?”**

### 1. Number Your Days

Moses prayed:

> “Teach us to realize the brevity of life, so that we may grow in wisdom.”

— Psalm 90:12

The Bible reminds us that our time on earth is limited.

Every morning we receive another day that we did not create and could not guarantee for ourselves. Life itself is a gift from God.

A steward therefore learns to value time.

This does not mean living in fear of tomorrow. It means living with wisdom today.

We should not assume that we will always have another opportunity to do what God has placed before us.

There are people we need to love, responsibilities we need to fulfill, opportunities we need to serve through, and things God may be asking us to do in the season we are currently in.

A faithful steward learns to recognize that **today is also part of God's entrustment.**

### 2. Make the Most of Every Opportunity

Paul wrote:

> “Make the most of every opportunity.”

— Ephesians 5:16

Time stewardship is closely connected with opportunity.

Sometimes we think stewardship means managing what we already possess. But God also entrusts us with moments.

A conversation can become an opportunity to encourage someone.

A difficulty can become an opportunity to demonstrate faith.

A workplace can become an opportunity to show integrity.

A ministry assignment can become an opportunity to serve.

A relationship can become an opportunity to show love.

A season of waiting can become an opportunity to grow.

The faithful steward learns to recognize these moments instead of allowing them to pass unnoticed.

We do not control every opportunity that comes to us, but we can choose how we respond when an opportunity is placed in our hands.

### 3. Being Busy Is Not the Same as Being Faithful

There is an important distinction between **being busy** and **being faithful**.

A person can fill every hour of the day and still neglect what truly matters.

Jesus demonstrated this when Martha became distracted by many tasks while Mary chose to sit at Jesus' feet and listen to Him.

Jesus did not teach that work was unimportant. Instead, He revealed that activity must never cause us to miss what is most important.

Stewardship therefore requires priorities.

There will always be another task.

There will always be another message to answer.

There will always be another responsibility waiting.

But the steward must learn to ask:

**What matters most in this season?**

Sometimes faithfulness means working.

Sometimes faithfulness means serving.

Sometimes faithfulness means resting.

Sometimes faithfulness means spending time with family.

Sometimes faithfulness means praying.

Sometimes faithfulness means stopping long enough to listen to God.

The goal is not to fill every minute.

The goal is to use our minutes wisely.

### 4. Every Season Has Its Purpose

Ecclesiastes 3:1 teaches:

> “For everything there is a season, a time for every activity under heaven.”

God does not give every person the same assignment or the same season.

There is a time to begin.

A time to build.

A time to learn.

A time to serve.

A time to wait.

A time to rest.

A time to finish.

A steward should therefore avoid constantly comparing his season with someone else's.

One person may be building while another is preparing.

One may be leading while another is learning.

One may be receiving an opportunity while another is waiting for one.

The question is not:

**“Why don't I have their season?”**

The better question is:

**“How can I be faithful with the season God has given me?”**

Faithfulness is possible in every season.

### 5. Do Not Waste the Time Entrusted to You

James reminds us that life is like a mist that appears for a little while and then disappears.

This should not make us hopeless.

It should make us intentional.

If God has given us today, then today has value.

If God has given us an opportunity, then that opportunity has value.

If God has given us people to love, then those relationships have value.

If God has given us work to accomplish, then that work has value.

The faithful steward does not need to accomplish everything at once.

He simply needs to be faithful with what is in front of him.

This means learning to say no to things that consume time without purpose.

It means reducing distractions when they begin to control us.

It means making room for prayer, Scripture, relationships, work, rest, and service.

It means remembering that **time belongs to the King just as much as money does.**

### 6. Stewardship Includes Rest

Faithful time management does not mean working without stopping.

God Himself established a rhythm of work and rest.

Rest is not necessarily wasted time.

Sometimes rest restores the strength needed to fulfill the assignment God has given.

Jesus Himself withdrew from the crowds and spent time in prayer.

The steward therefore does not treat exhaustion as proof of faithfulness.

Wisdom recognizes that our bodies, minds, relationships, and spiritual lives also need proper care.

The question is not simply:

**“How much can I accomplish?”**

It is:

**“How can I faithfully steward the strength and time God has given me?”**

There are seasons when we must work diligently.

There are also seasons when we must pause, recover, listen, and prepare.

Both can be acts of stewardship when done according to God's wisdom.

### Kingdom Principle

**Time is not merely something we spend; it is something the King has entrusted to us.**

Every day is a trust.

Every season carries responsibility.

Every opportunity can become an assignment.

The faithful steward does not measure his life merely by how much he accomplished, but by whether he used what the King entrusted to him for His purposes.

We cannot control how many days we will have.

But we can choose how we live the days we have been given.

### Heart Check

Ask yourself:

- Am I intentionally using my time, or am I simply reacting to whatever comes?
- What activities are consuming my time without producing anything valuable?
- Am I giving proper time to God, work, relationships, service, and rest?
- Is there an opportunity God has placed before me that I have been ignoring?
- Am I comparing my season with someone else's?
- If the King examined how I used my time, what would my schedule reveal about what I value?

### Faithful Response

Today, I will remember that my time belongs to the King.

I will not measure faithfulness by how busy I am.

I will seek God's wisdom for my priorities.

I will make the most of the opportunities He places before me.

I will honor the season I am in instead of constantly comparing it with another person's season.

I will work when it is time to work, serve when it is time to serve, rest when it is time to rest, and make room for God in every season.

I may not know how many days remain, but I can choose to make today's day count.

**The King entrusted me with this day. I will be faithful with it.**
