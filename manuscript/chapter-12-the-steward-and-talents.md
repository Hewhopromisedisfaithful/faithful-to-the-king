# PART II — THE STEWARD

## Chapter 12 — The Steward and Talents

God does not give every person the same abilities.

Some can teach.

Some can lead.

Some can create.

Some can organize.

Some can encourage.

Some can serve quietly behind the scenes.

Some have skills developed through years of learning and experience.

Others discover abilities they did not know they had until an opportunity required them to use them.

Whatever form they take, our abilities are not merely possessions.

They are trusts.

The question of the faithful steward is not:

**“Do I have as much talent as someone else?”**

It is:

**“Am I faithfully using what the King has entrusted to me?”**

### 1. Every Ability Is a Trust

Peter wrote:

> “God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts.”

— 1 Peter 4:10

The Bible teaches us to see our abilities differently.

Instead of thinking, **“This is mine,”** the steward learns to think, **“God entrusted this to me.”**

Our abilities may include natural skills, learned skills, spiritual gifts, creativity, knowledge, experience, communication, leadership, craftsmanship, problem-solving, or the ability to serve others.

Some abilities may seem impressive.

Others may appear ordinary.

But their value is not determined by how visible they are.

A gift becomes valuable when it is faithfully used according to the purpose for which God entrusted it.

### 2. The Parable of the Talents

Jesus told the story of a master who went on a journey and entrusted his possessions to three servants.

One received five talents.

Another received two.

Another received one.

They did not receive equal amounts.

But they all received something.

The first two servants put what they received to work.

The servant with one talent buried it.

When the master returned, he asked for an account.

The important lesson is not simply that the servants received different amounts.

It is that **each servant was responsible for what had been entrusted to him.**

The servant with five talents was not expected to produce according to the assignment of the servant with two.

And the servant with two was not expected to produce according to the assignment of the servant with five.

Their assignments were different.

Their responsibility was the same:

**Be faithful with what the master entrusted.**

### 3. Do Not Compare Your Gift With Another Person's Gift

Comparison can easily distort stewardship.

We may look at someone else's ability and think:

**“I wish I had what they have.”**

We may see someone's ministry, business, creativity, leadership, intelligence, or influence and conclude that our own contribution is insignificant.

But the Kingdom does not require every steward to carry the same assignment.

Paul explained this through the picture of the human body.

The body has many parts, and each part has a different function.

In the same way, believers have different gifts and different roles.

One gift does not become unnecessary simply because another gift is more visible.

A hand should not try to become an eye.

An eye should not try to become a foot.

Each has a function.

The same principle applies to stewardship.

**You do not need someone else's gift to be faithful with yours.**

### 4. Do Not Bury What the King Has Given

The third servant in Jesus' parable did not lose the talent.

He did not spend it.

He did not give it away.

He simply buried it.

That is an important warning.

Sometimes stewardship fails not because something was destroyed, but because something was never used.

A person may have the ability to teach but remain silent because of fear.

Someone may have the ability to encourage but never speak encouragement.

Someone may have leadership ability but refuse responsibility.

Someone may have creativity but never develop it.

Someone may have a heart for serving but keep waiting for a more impressive opportunity.

Fear, insecurity, comparison, perfectionism, or the belief that our gift is too small can cause us to bury what God has entrusted.

The King does not ask us to become someone else.

He asks us to faithfully use what He has placed in our hands.

### 5. Develop What Has Been Entrusted

Faithful stewardship also requires development.

A talent may be given, but it can still be developed through learning, practice, discipline, and experience.

Paul encouraged Timothy:

> “Fan into flames the spiritual gift God has given you.”

— 2 Timothy 1:6

This shows that receiving a gift does not mean we have finished growing.

A faithful steward asks:

**“How can I become better at using what God has entrusted to me?”**

That may mean studying.

It may mean practicing.

It may mean receiving correction.

It may mean learning from someone more experienced.

It may mean taking small opportunities before larger ones.

It may mean remaining faithful even when nobody notices.

The goal is not to become impressive.

The goal is to become useful.

### 6. Gifts Are Meant to Serve Others

Peter gives another important principle:

> “Use them well to serve one another.”

— 1 Peter 4:10

God-given abilities are not primarily for self-glorification.

They are opportunities to serve.

A teacher serves by teaching.

A leader serves by leading responsibly.

A skilled worker serves through excellence.

A musician serves through music.

A person with encouragement serves by strengthening others.

A person with resources can serve through generosity.

A person with influence can use that influence to help others.

This changes the question from:

**“What can my gift do for me?”**

to:

**“Whom can my gift serve?”**

That is a Kingdom mindset.

The gift points beyond the steward to the King who gave it.

### 7. Faithfulness Matters More Than Visibility

Not every faithful steward will become famous.

Not every gift will be publicly recognized.

Some of the most important acts of stewardship happen where very few people see them.

A parent caring for a child.

A worker doing honest work.

A church member serving without recognition.

A person encouraging someone privately.

A skilled person helping another person learn.

A leader making a difficult decision with integrity.

The world often measures value through visibility.

The Kingdom measures stewardship through faithfulness.

Jesus' parable reminds us that the master praised the servants who faithfully handled what they had received.

The question is not whether everyone saw your work.

The question is whether the King saw faithfulness.

And He does.

### Kingdom Principle

**Your abilities are trusts from the King, not trophies for the steward.**

You do not need another person's gift to fulfill your assignment.

You do not need to be the most talented person in the room.

You do not need the largest opportunity before you can begin.

You need to be faithful with what has already been placed in your hands.

The King may give different gifts, different capacities, different opportunities, and different assignments.

But the responsibility remains:

**Use what He has entrusted. Develop it faithfully. Use it to serve others. Give the glory back to the King.**

### Heart Check

Ask yourself:

- What abilities has the King entrusted to me?
- Am I using them, developing them, or burying them?
- Have I been comparing my gifts with someone else's?
- Is fear preventing me from using what God has given me?
- Am I using my abilities mainly for myself, or also to serve others?
- What small opportunity can I faithfully act on today?

### Faithful Response

I will not despise what the King has placed in my hands.

I will not compare my assignment with someone else's.

I will develop the abilities God has entrusted to me.

I will use my gifts to serve others rather than simply to gain recognition.

I will not bury what fear, insecurity, or comparison tells me is too small.

Whatever the King has given me, I will seek to use it faithfully.

**My gift belongs to the King. My ability is a trust. My responsibility is faithfulness.**

### Bible Foundation

- 1 Peter 4:10–11 — Using God's gifts to serve others.
- Matthew 25:14–30 — The Parable of the Talents and faithful stewardship.
- Romans 12:3–8 — Different gifts and different functions.
- 1 Corinthians 12:4–27 — Many gifts, one body.
- 2 Timothy 1:6 — Developing and stirring up God's gift.
- Colossians 3:23–24 — Working wholeheartedly for the Lord.
- 1 Corinthians 4:2 — The requirement of a steward: faithfulness.
