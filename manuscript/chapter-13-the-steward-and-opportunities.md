# PART II — THE STEWARD

## Chapter 13 — The Steward and Opportunities

Not every opportunity looks important when it first arrives.

Sometimes it comes as a small responsibility.

Sometimes it appears as a difficult conversation.

Sometimes it is an unexpected chance to help someone.

Sometimes it is an open door that requires courage.

Sometimes it is simply the opportunity to do what is right when nobody is watching.

A steward must learn to recognize that opportunities can also be entrusted by the King.

The question is not simply:

**“What opportunity can I get?”**

It is:

**“What opportunity has the King placed before me, and how should I steward it?”**

### 1. Opportunities Are Also Trusts

Paul instructed the believers:

> “Make the most of every opportunity.”

— Ephesians 5:16

An opportunity gives us a moment in which we can choose how to respond.

We cannot control every opportunity that comes into our lives.

We cannot force every door to open.

We cannot manufacture every relationship.

But when an opportunity comes, we can choose whether to handle it faithfully.

An opportunity may involve work, ministry, relationships, learning, business, service, leadership, or simply doing something good for another person.

The steward recognizes that an opportunity carries responsibility.

What we do with the opportunity matters.

### 2. An Open Door Requires Faithfulness

Sometimes we pray for an open door.

Then the door opens, and we discover that an open door also comes with responsibility.

A new job requires diligence.

A leadership position requires service.

A ministry opportunity requires preparation.

A business opportunity requires integrity.

A new relationship requires wisdom and care.

Greater opportunity does not automatically mean greater importance.

It often means greater responsibility.

This is consistent with Jesus' teaching:

> “When someone has been entrusted with much, even more will be required.”

— Luke 12:48

The steward should therefore not only ask for opportunities.

The steward should also ask for the wisdom and character to handle them faithfully.

### 3. Do Not Despise Small Opportunities

Some opportunities appear too small to matter.

A short conversation.

A simple act of kindness.

Helping someone carry a burden.

Teaching one person.

Serving in an unnoticed place.

Doing a small task with excellence.

Giving someone encouragement at the right moment.

But Scripture repeatedly shows that God can work through what appears small.

Zechariah 4:10 reminds God's people not to despise small beginnings.

The size of an opportunity does not determine its Kingdom value.

A small opportunity faithfully handled may prepare us for a greater responsibility.

More importantly, even when it never leads to anything larger, faithfulness is still valuable because the King sees it.

We should not serve only when the assignment looks impressive.

### 4. Wisdom Is Needed to Recognize Opportunities

Not every opportunity should automatically be accepted.

Stewardship requires wisdom.

Paul instructed the Colossians to:

> “Live wisely among those who are not believers, and make the most of every opportunity.”

— Colossians 4:5

This means opportunity must be evaluated according to God's wisdom.

An opportunity can look attractive and still be unwise.

Another opportunity can look difficult and still be valuable.

The steward therefore learns to pray, seek wisdom, examine motives, consider responsibilities, and compare decisions with God's Word.

The question is not:

**“Can I do this?”**

It is:

**“Should I do this?”**

And sometimes:

**“Is this the right time to do this?”**

Faithfulness requires more than recognizing opportunities.

It requires discerning them.

### 5. Do Not Force What God Has Not Entrusted

There is a difference between faithfully responding to an opportunity and trying to manufacture one.

A steward does not need to manipulate circumstances to prove that he is progressing.

If God has not entrusted a certain responsibility yet, there is no need to pretend that He has.

David is an example of someone who waited for God's timing rather than taking the kingdom by force.

Although David had been anointed as future king, he refused to take Saul's life when he had the opportunity.

David recognized that position and timing ultimately belonged to God.

This teaches an important principle:

**An opportunity to do something is not always permission to do it.**

Wisdom asks whether the opportunity agrees with God's principles, timing, and purpose.

### 6. Opportunities Can Be Tests

Sometimes an opportunity reveals our character.

Joseph was given opportunities to compromise.

Instead, he chose integrity.

Daniel was given opportunities to gain influence within a foreign kingdom.

He continued to honor God.

Jesus' disciples received opportunities to serve crowds, teach others, and eventually carry the Gospel.

Opportunities therefore do not only reveal what we can accomplish.

They reveal what kind of stewards we are becoming.

When responsibility increases, character becomes increasingly important.

A larger opportunity does not remove the need for faithfulness.

It increases it.

### 7. Do Not Miss the Opportunity to Serve

One of the greatest opportunities a steward can receive is the opportunity to serve another person.

Jesus taught His disciples that Kingdom greatness is connected with serving.

> “Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”

— Mark 10:43

This changes how we view opportunities.

We may think an opportunity is valuable because it gives us more recognition.

Jesus teaches us to see value in opportunities to serve.

A leadership position can become an opportunity to serve.

A business can become an opportunity to provide honest work.

A platform can become an opportunity to encourage people.

Resources can become an opportunity to help others.

Influence can become an opportunity to point people toward what is good and true.

The faithful steward asks:

**“How can this opportunity become a blessing to others?”**

### 8. Some Opportunities Are Seasonal

Not every opportunity remains open forever.

Paul's instruction to make the most of every opportunity reminds us that time matters.

There may be a moment to speak.

A moment to act.

A moment to help.

A moment to forgive.

A moment to begin.

A moment to serve.

A moment to learn.

Wisdom recognizes the season.

This does not mean living anxiously or trying to seize everything.

It means remaining attentive.

The steward should not become so distracted by yesterday's opportunities or tomorrow's possibilities that he misses what God has placed before him today.

### Kingdom Principle

**An opportunity is not merely a chance to gain something; it can be a trust from the King that carries responsibility.**

The faithful steward does not chase every open door.

He seeks wisdom to recognize the right opportunities, courage to act when action is required, humility to serve when service is needed, and patience when the right time has not yet come.

Some opportunities will be large.

Some will be small.

Some will be public.

Some will be hidden.

But every opportunity can become an occasion for faithfulness.

### Heart Check

Ask yourself:

- What opportunities has the King placed before me right now?
- Am I recognizing small opportunities to serve?
- Am I trying to force opportunities that God has not entrusted to me?
- Do I seek God's wisdom before accepting major opportunities?
- Have I allowed fear to make me miss an opportunity to do what is right?
- Am I viewing opportunities mainly as ways to advance myself, or as ways to serve God and others?

### Faithful Response

I will not chase every opportunity simply because it looks attractive.

I will seek God's wisdom to recognize what He has placed before me.

I will not despise small opportunities.

I will serve faithfully whether an opportunity is visible or hidden.

I will not force doors that God has not opened.

When the right opportunity comes, I will respond with courage, wisdom, humility, and integrity.

I will remember that every opportunity carries responsibility.

**When the King places an opportunity in my hands, I will treat it as a trust.**

### Bible Foundation

- Ephesians 5:15–16 — Making the most of every opportunity.
- Luke 12:48 — Greater trust brings greater responsibility.
- Colossians 4:5 — Walking wisely and making the most of opportunities.
- Zechariah 4:10 — Not despising small beginnings.
- 1 Samuel 24:1–7 — David refusing to take Saul's life despite the opportunity.
- Genesis 39:7–12 — Joseph choosing integrity when tempted.
- Daniel 1:8 — Daniel choosing faithfulness in a position of influence.
- Mark 10:42–45 — Kingdom leadership expressed through service.
- 1 Corinthians 4:2 — The requirement of a steward: faithfulness.
