# Chapter 14

## The Steward and Relationships

> **“Love each other with genuine affection, and take delight in honoring each other.”**
> — Romans 12:10

A steward does not only manage what can be counted.

He also manages what cannot be measured by money.

There are things the King places into our hands that have no price tag: people.

A friend.
A spouse.
A parent.
A child.
A brother or sister.
A mentor.
A fellow servant.
A business partner.
A leader.
A person who simply crossed our path at a moment when they needed someone.

These relationships are not accidents in the Kingdom.

They are trusts.

We often think stewardship means taking care of money, possessions, opportunities, businesses, talents, or responsibilities. But if God is truly the King, then even the people He allows into our lives must be handled according to His ways.

People are not possessions.

They are not resources for our personal advancement.

They are not tools we use to get what we want.

They are people created by God and loved by Him.

And sometimes, the greatest evidence of our faithfulness as stewards is not what we have accumulated, but how we have treated the people the King placed around us.

---

## The Scripture

Jesus gave us a simple but profound command:

> **“Love your neighbor as yourself.”**
> — Matthew 22:39

This command changes the way a steward sees relationships.

If I see people only according to what they can do for me, I am thinking like an owner.

But if I understand that God has entrusted people into my life, I begin to ask a different question:

**“How does the King want me to treat this person?”**

That question changes everything.

It changes how we speak.

It changes how we listen.

It changes how we correct.

It changes how we forgive.

It changes how we lead.

It changes how we respond when someone disappoints us.

It even changes how we behave when nobody can reward us for doing what is right.

A faithful steward does not ask only, **“What can I get from this relationship?”**

He asks,

**“What has God entrusted me to give through this relationship?”**

Sometimes the answer is encouragement.

Sometimes it is patience.

Sometimes it is correction.

Sometimes it is forgiveness.

Sometimes it is simply being present.

And sometimes stewardship means knowing when to establish wise boundaries while still treating a person with dignity and love.

Faithfulness in relationships does not mean pleasing everyone.

It means representing the King faithfully.

---

## Kingdom Illustration: The People Entrusted to a Steward

Imagine a king appointing a steward over a portion of his kingdom.

The king gives the steward land, resources, authority, and responsibilities.

But there is something else the king expects.

The people under that steward's care must not be abused, neglected, manipulated, or treated as though they exist merely to serve the steward.

The steward has authority, but that authority belongs to the king.

The people belong to the king.

Therefore, the steward's responsibility is not merely to manage the kingdom's resources.

He must care for the people under his responsibility according to the character and commands of the king.

This is the picture of Kingdom stewardship.

God may give us influence over people, but that influence is never ownership.

A parent has authority over a child, but the child ultimately belongs to God.

A leader has authority over a team, but the people ultimately belong to God.

A husband and wife belong to one another in covenant, yet both ultimately belong to God.

A business owner may employ people, but those people are not merely numbers on a payroll.

A pastor may shepherd a congregation, but the flock ultimately belongs to the Chief Shepherd.

**Authority increases responsibility.**

The greater the influence God gives us over people, the greater our responsibility to handle that influence with humility, love, wisdom, and integrity.

---

## People Are Entrusted, Not Owned

One of the most dangerous mistakes a steward can make is confusing **stewardship with ownership**.

When we believe that someone belongs to us, we may begin to control them.

We may demand instead of serve.

We may manipulate instead of communicate.

We may use instead of honor.

We may become possessive instead of protective.

But stewardship says:

**“This person has been entrusted to me, but this person does not belong to me.”**

That truth produces humility.

It reminds us that every relationship is ultimately under God's authority.

Even the people closest to us are gifts we must handle carefully.

We cannot control another person's heart.

We cannot force another person to love us.

We cannot manufacture loyalty.

We cannot make someone become what we want them to become.

Our responsibility is to be faithful with what God has placed in our hands.

That means loving without manipulating.

Serving without demanding recognition.

Correcting without humiliating.

Forgiving without keeping a record of every failure.

Speaking truth without destroying dignity.

And honoring people even when they cannot give anything back to us.

---

## The Steward's Responsibility in Relationships

### 1. Love

Love is not merely an emotion.

Biblical love is a decision to seek what is good according to God's will.

Paul describes love as patient and kind. It does not envy, boast, or dishonor others. It does not insist on its own way.

> **“Love is patient and kind. Love is not jealous or boastful or proud or rude.”**
> — 1 Corinthians 13:4

A steward must learn that love is part of faithful management.

How we love people matters to the King.

---

### 2. Honor

Romans 12:10 tells us to take delight in honoring one another.

Honor means recognizing the value God has placed in another person.

It does not mean agreeing with everything someone does.

It does not mean ignoring wrong behavior.

It means refusing to treat people as worthless.

A steward can disagree while still showing respect.

A steward can correct while preserving dignity.

A steward can establish boundaries without becoming hateful.

Because honor is not based on whether someone is useful to us.

**Honor is based on recognizing that they are God's creation.**

---

### 3. Patience

Relationships test stewardship.

People have weaknesses.

People misunderstand.

People make mistakes.

People grow at different speeds.

And sometimes, we ourselves are the difficult person in someone else's life.

A faithful steward remembers the patience God has shown him.

> **“Always be humble and gentle. Be patient with each other, making allowance for each other’s faults because of your love.”**
> — Ephesians 4:2

Patience does not mean tolerating everything without wisdom.

It means refusing to respond to human weakness with unnecessary harshness.

The King has been patient with us.

Therefore, we learn to extend patience to others.

---

### 4. Forgiveness

Every relationship will eventually encounter offense.

The question is not whether someone will disappoint us.

The question is how we will respond when they do.

Forgiveness does not erase wisdom.

It does not mean pretending that something harmful never happened.

It does not mean refusing to establish boundaries.

But forgiveness releases our right to continually punish someone in our hearts for what they have done.

A steward understands that vengeance does not belong to him.

He entrusts justice to God.

He chooses freedom over bitterness.

---

### 5. Faithfulness

A steward is faithful not only when relationships are easy.

Faithfulness is revealed when there is inconvenience, misunderstanding, sacrifice, or no immediate reward.

Jesus said:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**
> — Luke 16:10

Sometimes we look for great Kingdom assignments while neglecting the people already around us.

But perhaps the person sitting beside us is part of today's assignment.

Perhaps the family member we have been impatient with is part of today's stewardship.

Perhaps the person who needs encouragement is part of the opportunity God placed before us.

We should not overlook small acts of faithfulness simply because they appear ordinary.

Kingdom stewardship often looks ordinary from the outside.

---

## When Relationships Become Difficult

A mature steward must understand something important:

**Not every relationship will remain close, and not every relationship can be restored in the same way.**

Faithfulness does not mean forcing every relationship to continue exactly as it was.

There are moments when wisdom requires distance.

There are situations where boundaries are necessary.

There are circumstances where reconciliation may require genuine repentance and change.

The steward's responsibility is not to control the outcome.

The steward's responsibility is to remain faithful to God's character.

We can speak truth.

We can forgive.

We can apologize.

We can pursue peace.

We can establish boundaries.

We can pray.

And after doing what is right, we can entrust the outcome to the King.

> **“Do all that you can to live in peace with everyone.”**
> — Romans 12:18

Notice the wisdom in those words:

**“Do all that you can.”**

There are things within our responsibility, and there are things that are beyond our control.

A steward must know the difference.

---

## Relationships and the Kingdom

The Kingdom of God is not merely demonstrated through what we build.

It is demonstrated through how we treat people.

We can build businesses and still fail at stewardship.

We can gain influence and still fail at stewardship.

We can possess wealth and still fail at stewardship.

We can receive opportunities and still fail at stewardship.

Because the King is not only interested in what passes through our hands.

He is interested in what happens inside our hearts while we handle it.

A person who understands Kingdom stewardship eventually realizes:

**People are part of the assignment.**

The conversation matters.

The apology matters.

The encouragement matters.

The act of listening matters.

The decision to forgive matters.

The decision not to manipulate matters.

The decision to honor someone when nobody is watching matters.

These things may never appear on a financial statement.

But they matter in the Kingdom.

---

## Heart Check

Before asking God for more influence, perhaps we should ask whether we are faithful with the influence we already have.

Before asking for more relationships, ask whether we are caring for the relationships already entrusted to us.

Before asking for greater authority, ask whether we are using the authority we already possess with humility.

Before asking for greater opportunities, ask whether we are honoring the people connected to the opportunities we already have.

And before asking, **“Who will God send into my life?”**

perhaps we should ask:

**“How am I stewarding the people He has already placed in my life?”**

### Ask yourself:

* Do I treat people as people, or as what they can do for me?
* Do I honor people even when they cannot benefit me?
* Do I listen, or do I always need to be heard?
* Do I correct with love or with pride?
* Do I forgive, or do I keep collecting offenses?
* Do I respect healthy boundaries?
* Am I faithful to the people God has already entrusted to me?
* Does my treatment of people reflect the character of my King?

The answers may reveal areas where the King is inviting us to grow.

---

## The Steward's Greatest Example

Jesus showed us what faithful stewardship of people looks like.

He served.

He listened.

He taught.

He corrected.

He forgave.

He showed compassion.

He welcomed the overlooked.

He touched the broken.

He washed His disciples' feet.

And ultimately, He gave His life.

Jesus did not use people to advance Himself.

He gave Himself for people.

That is the heart of Kingdom leadership.

That is the heart of Kingdom stewardship.

The closer we come to the King, the more His character should become visible in the way we treat others.

---

## Steward's Declaration

**I am a steward, not an owner.**

The people God has placed in my life are not possessions for me to control.

They are people I am called to love, honor, serve, and treat with dignity.

I will not measure relationships only by what they can give me.

I will learn to give without demanding.

I will forgive without keeping records of offenses.

I will speak truth with love.

I will establish wise boundaries when necessary.

I will serve with humility.

I will honor people because they matter to God.

And when I cannot control the outcome of a relationship, I will entrust it to the King.

**Everything belongs to Him.

Everyone is under His authority.

And my responsibility is to be faithful with whatever He places in my hands.**

---

## Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of all,**

Thank You for every person You have allowed to become part of my life.

Forgive me for the times I have treated people as possessions, opportunities, or instruments for my own purposes.

Teach me to see people through Your eyes.

Give me wisdom to know when to speak and when to listen, when to correct and when to encourage, when to hold close and when to establish a boundary.

Give me patience with the weaknesses of others, because You have been patient with mine.

Teach me to forgive.

Teach me to honor.

Teach me to love without manipulation and serve without expecting recognition.

May my relationships reflect Your Kingdom.

And when I am entrusted with greater influence, help me remember that greater influence means greater responsibility.

Everything I have comes from You.

Every opportunity comes from You.

And every person You place in my life is a trust I must handle faithfully.

**Make me a faithful steward—not only of what I possess, but of the people You have entrusted to me.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
