# Chapter 17

## The Steward and Gifts & Talents

> **“A spiritual gift is given to each of us so we can help each other.”**
> — 1 Corinthians 12:7

The King does not entrust only money and possessions to His stewards.

He also places gifts, abilities, talents, creativity, wisdom, knowledge, skills, and opportunities into their hands.

Some people can sing.

Some can teach.

Some can build.

Some can lead.

Some can organize.

Some can write.

Some can create.

Some can encourage.

Some can solve problems.

Some can work with their hands.

Some can work with numbers.

Some can communicate ideas.

Others may possess gifts that are difficult to see but deeply valuable—wisdom, discernment, compassion, patience, faith, or the ability to encourage someone who is struggling.

The question is not:

**“Why don't I have the gift someone else has?”**

The question is:

**“What has the King placed in my hands, and what am I doing with it?”**

---

## The King Gives Differently

God does not give every steward the same gifts.

Paul explained this through the picture of the body:

> **“Just as our bodies have many parts and each part has a special function, so it is with Christ’s body.”**
> — Romans 12:4–5

A hand is not a foot.

An eye is not an ear.

Each part has a different function.

In the same way, the Kingdom does not require every person to have the same assignment.

One person may stand on a platform.

Another may work quietly behind the scenes.

One may preach.

Another may prepare.

One may lead.

Another may faithfully support.

One may be visible.

Another may never receive public recognition.

But visibility does not determine value.

**Faithfulness does.**

---

## Your Gift Is an Entrustment

A gift is called a gift because it was received.

You did not create yourself.

You did not choose every ability you possess.

You did not determine every opportunity that came into your life.

Even the abilities you developed through discipline exist within a life God allowed you to have.

James 1:17 says:

> **“Whatever is good and perfect is a gift coming down to us from God our Father.”**

Therefore, gifts should produce gratitude rather than pride.

When the steward discovers that he can do something well, his response should not simply be:

**“Look how great I am.”**

It should be:

**“Thank You, King, for entrusting this ability to me.”**

The gift points back to the Giver.

---

## The Parable of the Talents

Jesus gave a powerful picture of stewardship in Matthew 25.

A master went on a journey and entrusted his servants with different amounts of money.

One received five talents.

Another received two.

Another received one.

They did not receive equal amounts.

But each servant was responsible for what had been placed in his hands.

The servant who received five produced more.

The servant who received two also produced more.

The servant who received one buried it.

When the master returned, the first two servants were commended.

Their reward was not based on having received the same amount.

Their reward was connected to their **faithfulness**.

This is one of the clearest principles of stewardship:

**The King does not require you to have someone else's gift. He requires you to be faithful with yours.**

---

## Do Not Bury What God Gave You

The servant with one talent buried what he had received.

Why?

Fear.

He was afraid of failing.

Perhaps he compared himself with the servants who received more.

Perhaps he thought:

**“What difference can my one talent make?”**

But the problem was not that he had only one.

The problem was that he did nothing with it.

This can happen to us.

We may say:

**“I am not as talented as them.”**

**“I am not educated enough.”**

**“I don't have enough experience.”**

**“Someone else can do it better.”**

**“Maybe my contribution is too small.”**

And slowly, the gift that God intended to be used becomes buried beneath fear, insecurity, comparison, or procrastination.

The King does not ask you to multiply someone else's talent.

He asks you to faithfully use what He placed in your hands.

---

## Small Gifts Can Serve Great Purposes

Not every gift looks impressive.

A person who encourages someone may never appear on a stage.

A person who faithfully prepares things behind the scenes may never receive applause.

A parent raising children with wisdom may not receive public recognition.

A worker who performs his job with excellence may never become famous.

A friend who consistently listens may never be celebrated.

Yet these things matter.

Zechariah 4:10 reminds us:

> **“Do not despise these small beginnings.”**

The Kingdom is not built only through spectacular moments.

It is also built through countless acts of ordinary faithfulness.

Never despise what appears small when it is placed in the hands of God.

---

## Excellence Is Part of Stewardship

Being a steward does not mean simply using our gifts.

It also means developing them.

Proverbs 22:29 says:

> **“Do you see any truly competent workers? They will serve kings rather than working for ordinary people.”**

Skill matters.

Discipline matters.

Practice matters.

Learning matters.

Preparation matters.

If God has entrusted an ability to you, developing that ability can be an act of worship.

A musician should practice.

A teacher should study.

A businessperson should learn.

A writer should write.

A leader should grow in wisdom.

A craftsman should improve his craft.

A servant should become more effective in serving.

We should not use “God gave me this gift” as an excuse for remaining undeveloped.

**Stewardship includes cultivation.**

---

## Gift and Character Must Grow Together

A gifted person without character can become dangerous.

Talent can open doors.

Character determines what happens after the door opens.

A person may be highly skilled but dishonest.

A person may be talented but arrogant.

A person may be gifted but unreliable.

A person may be influential but lacking integrity.

The Kingdom requires more than ability.

It requires character.

This is why the steward should pray:

**“King, develop not only my gift. Develop me.”**

We should not merely ask God:

**“Increase my ability.”**

We should also ask:

**“Increase my wisdom, humility, integrity, patience, and faithfulness.”**

A great gift in an immature character can become a great responsibility poorly managed.

---

## Do Not Compare Your Gift

Comparison is one of the fastest ways to become dissatisfied with what God has given you.

We see someone else's ability and think:

**“I wish I had that.”**

We see someone's success and think:

**“Why am I not there?”**

We see someone's platform and think:

**“Maybe my gift doesn't matter.”**

But comparison changes our attention from stewardship to competition.

The question is not:

**“Why did God give them more?”**

The question is:

**“What am I doing with what God gave me?”**

Peter once asked Jesus about another disciple.

Jesus answered him:

> **“What is that to you? As for you, follow me.”**
> — John 21:22

That is a powerful reminder.

Someone else's assignment does not cancel yours.

Someone else's success does not make your obedience less valuable.

Someone else's gift does not make your gift unnecessary.

---

## Gifts Are Meant to Serve

Paul gives us an important principle:

> **“A spiritual gift is given to each of us so we can help each other.”**
> — 1 Corinthians 12:7

The purpose of a gift is not merely personal achievement.

It is service.

God may give someone the ability to teach so others can understand.

He may give someone encouragement so others can continue.

He may give someone leadership so people can be guided.

He may give someone generosity so others can receive.

He may give someone wisdom so difficult situations can be handled well.

A gift becomes more beautiful when it becomes a blessing.

The question should therefore be:

**“Who can be helped because the King placed this gift in me?”**

---

## Your Gift May Create Your Assignment

Sometimes the gift God placed within you points toward the responsibility He wants you to carry.

A person who loves teaching may eventually be entrusted with people to teach.

A person who is skilled in business may be entrusted with a company.

A person who can create may be entrusted with projects.

A person who communicates well may be entrusted with a message.

A person who knows how to organize may be entrusted with greater responsibility.

This does not mean every talent automatically becomes a calling.

But gifts can become clues to assignment.

The steward pays attention.

He asks:

**“King, how can this ability serve Your purpose?”**

---

## Gifts Require Responsibility

The greater the gift, the greater the responsibility to steward it well.

A person with influence can affect many people.

A person with leadership can affect an entire organization.

A person with financial ability can affect families and communities.

A person with communication skills can influence how people think.

A person with technical ability can create things that impact thousands.

This is why:

**Bigger gift = bigger responsibility.**

The gift itself is not the destination.

It is an entrusted tool.

The King gives it.

The steward develops it.

The Kingdom benefits from it.

---

## When the Gift Brings Recognition

Recognition can be another test of stewardship.

People may praise you.

They may applaud your work.

They may call you talented.

They may recommend you.

They may give you opportunities.

Receive encouragement with gratitude.

But do not allow applause to become your identity.

The moment the steward begins thinking:

**“I am successful because I am great,”**

he has forgotten the Giver.

A humble steward can say:

**“Thank you for the encouragement, but I know where this ability came from.”**

Psalm 115:1 gives us the right posture:

> **“Not to us, Lord, not to us, but to your name goes all the glory.”**

The gift may be visible.

The Giver should receive the glory.

---

## When the Gift Seems Hidden

Not every season will bring recognition.

Sometimes the King develops a gift in hidden places.

David was a shepherd before he became king.

Moses spent years in the wilderness.

Jesus Himself spent many years in relative obscurity before His public ministry.

Hidden seasons are not necessarily wasted seasons.

A gift can be developed without an audience.

In fact, hidden faithfulness can prepare us for visible responsibility.

So if nobody is applauding right now, keep developing.

If nobody sees the practice, keep practicing.

If nobody recognizes the work, keep serving.

The King sees.

---

## Do Not Wait Until You Feel Ready

One of the easiest ways to bury a gift is to keep waiting until everything is perfect.

**“When I know more…”**

**“When I have more money…”**

**“When I become more confident…”**

**“When I have better equipment…”**

**“When people notice me…”**

There will always be another reason to wait.

But stewardship often begins with what is already in our hands.

Moses had a staff.

David had a sling.

The widow had a small amount of oil.

The disciples had limited resources.

God repeatedly demonstrated that He can use what appears small when it is surrendered to Him.

Do not wait for everything to become perfect before becoming faithful.

Start with what the King has already entrusted to you.

---

## Gifts and Humility

The steward must remember:

**A gift is not a reason to feel superior.**

If God gave you an ability that someone else does not have, that is not permission to look down on them.

Instead, it creates an opportunity to serve them.

The hand does not boast against the foot.

The eye does not insult the ear.

Different functions create a complete body.

Likewise, the Kingdom needs different people with different gifts.

Your gift does not make you better.

It makes you **responsible**.

---

## The Gift Must Remain Under the King's Authority

Even a good gift can become dangerous when it is separated from God's authority.

A person can use communication to manipulate.

A person can use intelligence to deceive.

A person can use leadership to control.

A person can use business skills to exploit.

A person can use creativity for destructive purposes.

Therefore, the steward must continually ask:

**“Is the way I am using this gift pleasing to the King?”**

The issue is not only what we can do.

It is also **how and why we do it.**

Kingdom stewardship requires both ability and obedience.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **What gifts and abilities has the King entrusted to me?**
2. **Am I developing those gifts or allowing them to remain unused?**
3. **Am I comparing my gifts with someone else's?**
4. **Am I using my abilities to serve others or primarily to glorify myself?**
5. **How am I handling recognition and praise?**
6. **Is my character growing alongside my abilities?**
7. **What gift have I been afraid to use?**
8. **What could I begin doing today with what is already in my hands?**

These questions bring us back to the heart of stewardship.

The issue is not how impressive the gift appears.

The issue is whether the steward is faithful.

---

## The Greatest Gift

Every ability we possess is temporary.

Our strength changes.

Our opportunities change.

Our roles change.

Our seasons change.

But the greatest gift is not a talent.

It is the relationship we have with the King.

Everything else flows from there.

If we become successful but lose our relationship with God, we have missed the greater treasure.

If our gifts become famous but our hearts become distant from God, we have not truly stewarded them well.

The greatest achievement of a steward is not becoming known.

It is remaining faithful.

---

## The Steward's Declaration

I am a steward of the gifts and abilities the King has entrusted to me.

I will not compare my assignment with another person's assignment.

I will not bury my gifts because of fear, insecurity, or comparison.

I will develop what God has placed in my hands.

I will pursue excellence without pursuing pride.

I will allow my character to grow alongside my abilities.

I will use my gifts to serve others.

When people praise me, I will remember the Giver.

When nobody sees me, I will remain faithful.

When opportunities increase, I will remember that greater gifts bring greater responsibility.

My gift is not my identity.

My gift is an entrusted responsibility.

**The King gave it.

I will steward it.

And I will use it for His glory.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Giver of every good gift,**

Thank You for the abilities, talents, opportunities, and skills You have placed in my hands.

Forgive me for the times I have compared myself with others or allowed fear to bury what You entrusted to me.

Teach me to recognize the gifts You have given me.

Give me discipline to develop them.

Give me humility to use them.

Give me wisdom to know when and where to use them.

Protect me from pride when people recognize my work.

Protect me from discouragement when nobody notices.

Help me remember that my gifts came from You and ultimately belong to You.

Let my abilities become instruments of service.

Let my skills become blessings.

Let my opportunities become doors through which Your goodness can reach others.

And above everything else, develop my character so that whatever You place in my hands will be handled with faithfulness.

I do not want merely to be gifted.

I want to be **faithful**.

Because one day I want to hear the words:

**“Well done, good and faithful servant.”**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
