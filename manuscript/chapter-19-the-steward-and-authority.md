# Chapter 19

## The Steward and Authority

> **“Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”**
> — Matthew 20:26

Authority is one of the most misunderstood things in the world.

Many people desire authority because they associate it with power, recognition, influence, and control.

But in the Kingdom, authority is different.

Authority is not primarily about being above people.

It is about being **responsible for people**.

When the King entrusts authority to a steward, He is not simply giving that steward a higher position.

He is giving him a greater responsibility.

The question is therefore not:

**“How much authority do I have?”**

The better question is:

**“How faithfully am I using the authority the King has entrusted to me?”**

---

## Authority Comes From the King

Romans 13:1 teaches that authority ultimately exists under God's sovereignty.

This does not mean every action of every human authority is automatically approved by God.

It means that the steward must recognize that authority itself carries accountability before the King.

Whether the authority is in a family, workplace, business, ministry, community, or organization, the person entrusted with leadership is still under God's authority.

The steward should therefore never think:

**“I am the highest authority here.”**

Instead:

**“I am under the authority of the King, and I am accountable to Him for how I use the authority He has allowed me to carry.”**

That perspective changes everything.

---

## Position Is an Entrustment

A position can look like a promotion.

But Kingdom thinking sees it as an assignment.

A manager has an assignment.

A parent has an assignment.

A business owner has an assignment.

A ministry leader has an assignment.

A supervisor has an assignment.

A government official has an assignment.

A team leader has an assignment.

Different positions carry different responsibilities, but the principle remains:

**Position is not ownership.**

The King may place someone in a position today and move that person somewhere else tomorrow.

Therefore, the wise steward does not build his identity around his title.

He builds his identity around his relationship with the King.

---

## The Test of Leadership

Leadership reveals character.

When a person has no authority, it may be easy to appear humble.

But what happens when people begin listening to him?

What happens when he can make decisions?

What happens when he can approve or reject something?

What happens when people depend on him?

What happens when nobody can easily challenge him?

That is where the heart can be revealed.

Power does not necessarily create character.

Often, power **reveals** character.

The faithful steward therefore understands that authority requires self-control.

---

## Jesus Redefined Greatness

Jesus completely challenged the world's understanding of leadership.

He said:

> **“Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”**
> — Matthew 20:26

Then He continued:

> **“Whoever wants to be first among you must become your slave.”**
> — Matthew 20:27

Jesus did not define greatness by how many people serve you.

He defined greatness through service.

This is Kingdom leadership.

The greater the authority, the greater the opportunity to serve.

That means:

**Bigger position = bigger responsibility.**

**Greater influence = greater accountability.**

**More authority = greater responsibility to serve.**

---

## The King Himself Served

Jesus did not merely teach servant leadership.

He demonstrated it.

In John 13, Jesus washed the feet of His disciples.

Think about the significance of that moment.

The One who had authority chose humility.

The Master served.

The Teacher knelt.

The Lord washed feet.

He showed His disciples that authority in the Kingdom is not about demanding privilege.

It is about using one's position to bless others.

The steward should therefore ask:

**“Because I have authority, who can I serve?”**

Not:

**“Because I have authority, who should serve me?”**

---

## Authority Without Character

Authority without character can become destructive.

A leader who lacks integrity can misuse trust.

A manager who lacks compassion can damage people.

A parent who lacks self-control can create fear.

A business owner who lacks honesty can exploit others.

A ministry leader who lacks humility can become controlling.

This is why character must grow alongside authority.

The steward should never pray only:

**“King, give me greater influence.”**

He should also pray:

**“King, give me greater character to carry that influence.”**

Because the weight of authority should never exceed the strength of character required to carry it faithfully.

---

## Authority Is Not Permission to Control

There is a difference between leadership and control.

Leadership provides direction.

Control seeks domination.

Leadership develops people.

Control uses people.

Leadership accepts responsibility.

Control avoids accountability.

Leadership listens.

Control demands.

Leadership serves.

Control consumes.

The Kingdom steward must be careful not to use authority to manipulate people into doing what he wants.

Authority should create order, protection, development, and service.

---

## The Steward Must Know His Limits

Every steward has boundaries.

No matter how powerful a person's position may appear, he is still a steward.

He does not own the people.

He does not own the organization.

He does not own the resources.

He does not own the authority.

Everything remains under the King.

This is why humility is essential.

The steward must know:

**“This position was entrusted to me. It was not permanently given to me as personal property.”**

A position can be temporary.

A title can disappear.

A business can change.

A ministry assignment can change.

A season of leadership can end.

But faithfulness to God remains.

---

## Authority and Accountability

Jesus taught a principle that every steward must remember:

> **“When someone has been given much, much will be required in return.”**
> — Luke 12:48

Greater responsibility produces greater accountability.

If a steward has influence over ten people, he has responsibility for how he influences them.

If he has influence over one hundred, the responsibility becomes greater.

If he has influence over thousands, the consequences of his decisions can become much larger.

Therefore, influence should never produce arrogance.

It should produce reverence.

The steward should think:

**“King, help me handle this carefully because people are affected by my decisions.”**

---

## The Steward Leads by Example

People often follow what they see more than what they hear.

A leader may speak about integrity, but people watch how he handles money.

A leader may speak about humility, but people watch how he treats those beneath his position.

A leader may speak about service, but people watch whether he serves when nobody is watching.

A leader may speak about faithfulness, but people watch whether he keeps his promises.

Paul told Timothy:

> **“Be an example to all believers in what you teach, in the way you live, in your love, your faith, and your purity.”**
> — 1 Timothy 4:12

Leadership is therefore not simply about instruction.

It is about example.

---

## Authority and Listening

A steward with authority must still know how to listen.

The person at the top does not automatically know everything.

Proverbs 15:22 says:

> **“Plans go wrong for lack of advice; many advisers bring success.”**

Wise leaders listen.

They seek counsel.

They ask questions.

They receive correction.

They admit when they are wrong.

They allow others to contribute.

The steward who refuses correction becomes vulnerable to his own blind spots.

Humility says:

**“I may have authority, but I still need wisdom.”**

---

## The Danger of Pride

Pride is especially dangerous when authority increases.

A person may begin with a servant's heart.

Then recognition comes.

People begin praising him.

His decisions carry more weight.

His influence grows.

And slowly, he can begin believing:

**“I am important because people need me.”**

But the Kingdom steward must remember:

People may need the assignment God gave him.

They do not need him to become their master.

Only God occupies that place.

Proverbs 16:18 warns:

> **“Pride goes before destruction, and haughtiness before a fall.”**

The higher the position, the more important humility becomes.

---

## Authority and Protection

Authority is not only about decision-making.

It is also about protection.

A good leader protects the people entrusted to him.

He creates healthy boundaries.

He addresses problems.

He takes responsibility.

He does not allow weaker people to be continually exploited.

He does not ignore wrongdoing simply because confronting it is uncomfortable.

The shepherd protects the flock.

In the same way, a steward with authority should use his position to create an environment where people can work, grow, and serve safely and responsibly.

---

## Authority and Correction

Sometimes leadership requires correction.

Correction should not come from anger, pride, or humiliation.

It should come from a desire to restore what is right.

Galatians 6:1 teaches believers to restore someone gently when they have fallen into wrongdoing.

The steward should therefore learn the difference between:

**correction and condemnation,**

**discipline and humiliation,**

**accountability and abuse.**

A leader does not become faithful by ignoring every mistake.

But neither does he become faithful by crushing people for their mistakes.

Kingdom leadership seeks restoration.

---

## Authority and Delegation

A wise steward understands that he cannot do everything alone.

Moses learned this lesson when Jethro advised him to delegate responsibilities.

Exodus 18 describes how Moses was becoming overwhelmed by carrying every matter himself.

Jethro told him to appoint capable people to help carry the responsibility.

This teaches an important principle:

**Delegation is not weakness.**

Sometimes trying to control everything is actually poor stewardship.

If the King has entrusted people around you with abilities, allow them to use those abilities.

Develop people.

Trust people appropriately.

Give responsibility.

Teach.

Equip.

Release.

A leader's success should not depend on everyone remaining dependent on him.

---

## The Goal Is to Develop Other Stewards

The greatest leader is not the one who makes everyone dependent on him.

It is the one who helps others become faithful stewards themselves.

Jesus invested in twelve disciples.

He taught them.

Corrected them.

Prepared them.

Sent them.

Eventually, they carried the message beyond His earthly ministry.

That is multiplication.

A Kingdom steward should therefore ask:

**“Who am I preparing to carry responsibility after me?”**

If everything collapses when you leave, perhaps you built dependence instead of stewardship.

But if others become stronger, wiser, and more faithful because you invested in them, your leadership has multiplied its impact.

---

## When Authority Is Removed

One of the hardest tests of stewardship is losing a position.

A title can be removed.

A promotion can disappear.

A business role can change.

A ministry position can end.

Someone else can take your place.

When that happens, the steward must remember:

**The assignment changed.

The King did not.**

Our identity cannot be built entirely around a position.

David was anointed before he became king.

Joseph went from prison to leadership.

Daniel served under different kings.

Paul experienced different seasons of ministry.

Their circumstances changed.

Their calling to remain faithful to God remained.

If the position changes, remain faithful.

If the title disappears, remain faithful.

If recognition disappears, remain faithful.

Because the ultimate audience has always been the King.

---

## Authority Is Temporary

Every earthly position eventually changes hands.

Every organization eventually has another leader.

Every business eventually passes to someone else.

Every generation eventually makes room for another.

This should produce humility.

We are temporary stewards of eternal principles.

Therefore, we should not cling to position as though it belongs to us forever.

Instead, we should ask:

**“While this responsibility is in my hands, how can I steward it faithfully?”**

That is enough.

---

## The Steward and Influence

Not everyone has an official title.

But everyone has some level of influence.

A parent influences children.

A friend influences friends.

A teacher influences students.

A worker influences coworkers.

A business owner influences employees and customers.

A content creator influences an audience.

A Christian influences people through daily life.

Therefore, influence itself is a stewardship responsibility.

You may not consider yourself a leader.

But someone may be watching your example.

Someone may be learning from your choices.

Someone may be encouraged by your faith.

Someone may be discouraged by your behavior.

Influence is not limited to platforms.

**Influence begins with everyday life.**

---

## The Question the King Will Ask

The steward may one day realize that the most important question was never:

**“How high did I rise?”**

It was:

**“How faithfully did I lead?”**

Not:

**“How many people knew my name?”**

But:

**“How many people were blessed by my stewardship?”**

Not:

**“How much power did I possess?”**

But:

**“How responsibly did I use the authority I was given?”**

This is the Kingdom perspective.

The King is not impressed by titles.

He looks at faithfulness.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **What authority or influence has the King entrusted to me?**
2. **How do I treat people who are under my responsibility?**
3. **Do I use authority to serve or to control?**
4. **How do I respond when someone corrects me?**
5. **Is my character strong enough to carry my current level of influence?**
6. **Am I developing other people or making them dependent on me?**
7. **Would people experience God's character through the way I lead?**
8. **If my position disappeared tomorrow, would I still know who I am in God?**

These questions remind the steward that authority is not a trophy.

It is a trust.

---

## Jesus: The Greatest Model of Authority

Jesus had all authority.

Yet He humbled Himself.

He served.

He listened.

He taught.

He corrected.

He protected.

He restored.

He sacrificed.

And ultimately, He gave His life.

Philippians 2:6–7 describes how Christ, though possessing divine nature, humbled Himself and took the form of a servant.

This is the ultimate picture of Kingdom authority.

The greatest King became the greatest servant.

Therefore, the steward does not have to wonder what Kingdom leadership looks like.

**Look at Jesus.**

---

## The Steward's Declaration

I am a steward of every position, responsibility, and influence the King has entrusted to me.

I will never confuse authority with ownership.

I will use my position to serve, protect, develop, and bless others.

I will lead with humility.

I will listen to wise counsel.

I will receive correction.

I will not use authority to manipulate or control.

I will develop people rather than make them dependent on me.

I will remember that every position is temporary and every responsibility is accountable to the King.

When influence increases, I will remain humble.

When recognition disappears, I will remain faithful.

When my position changes, I will remember that my identity is not in my title.

**I do not lead because I am the owner.

I lead because the King has entrusted me with responsibility.

And I will steward that responsibility faithfully.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of all authority,**

Thank You for every position, responsibility, and influence You have entrusted to me.

Forgive me for the times I have used authority for myself instead of using it to serve others.

Give me humility when people recognize me.

Give me wisdom when difficult decisions must be made.

Give me courage to protect those entrusted to me.

Give me patience to correct with love.

Give me wisdom to listen.

Give me humility to receive correction.

Help me develop people instead of controlling them.

Teach me to delegate when necessary and to trust others appropriately.

Protect me from pride.

Protect me from the desire for recognition.

Remind me that every earthly position is temporary.

If You increase my influence, increase my character.

If You increase my authority, increase my responsibility.

And if You ever remove a position from my hands, help me remember that You remain my King.

May my leadership never point people toward myself.

May it always point people toward You.

**I am not the owner.

I am the steward.

And whatever authority You place in my hands,

I will use it faithfully for Your Kingdom.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
