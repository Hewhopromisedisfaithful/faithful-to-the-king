# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 2 — The Steward Is Entrusted, Not Entitled

In the Kingdom of God, there is an important distinction we must learn:

**We are entrusted, but we are not entitled.**

The King gives.

The King entrusts.

The King provides.

But everything remains His.

This truth protects the heart of a steward from pride, entitlement, and selfishness.

When we forget that God is the Owner, we can begin to believe that everything we have is the result of our own ability.

We may say:

**“I worked for this.”**

**“I earned this.”**

**“I built this.”**

**“I deserve this.”**

There may be truth in the fact that we worked hard.

But the Kingdom perspective asks a deeper question:

**Who gave you the strength, ability, opportunity, time, and wisdom to do it?**

### Everything We Have Is Received

Paul asked the church in Corinth a question that reaches directly into the heart of stewardship:

> “What do you have that God hasn’t given you?”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 4:7

That question changes our perspective.

Our abilities were received.

Our opportunities were received.

Our resources were received.

Even our capacity to work and produce came through what God has allowed us to have.

This does not make our effort meaningless.

It makes our effort an act of stewardship.

We work faithfully because something has been entrusted to us.

We develop our abilities because they have been entrusted to us.

We manage our resources because they have been entrusted to us.

We serve others because influence and relationships have been entrusted to us.

### A Steward Has Responsibility, Not Ownership

Imagine a king appointing a trusted servant to manage one of his estates.

The king gives the servant access to the land.

He gives him authority to oversee the workers.

He gives him resources to maintain the estate.

The servant may live in the estate.

He may make important decisions.

He may have authority over many things.

But none of those privileges make him the owner.

He remains a steward.

His authority comes from the king.

His resources come from the king.

His assignment comes from the king.

And ultimately, he answers to the king.

This is the picture we need to carry into our own lives.

God may give us authority, but authority is not ownership.

God may give us wealth, but wealth is not ownership.

God may give us influence, but influence is not ownership.

God may give us leadership, but leadership is not ownership.

**Everything remains under the authority of the King.**

### Entitlement Changes the Heart

Entitlement says:

**“I deserve this.”**

Stewardship says:

**“I have been entrusted with this.”**

Entitlement asks:

**“What do I get?”**

Stewardship asks:

**“What am I responsible for?”**

Entitlement focuses on privilege.

Stewardship focuses on responsibility.

Entitlement wants recognition.

Stewardship seeks faithfulness.

This is why understanding our position before God matters.

When we see ourselves as owners, we can become protective of what we possess.

But when we see ourselves as stewards, we become more willing to ask how the King wants His resources used.

### The Danger of Forgetting the Giver

Deuteronomy gives an important warning to God's people.

After they entered the Promised Land, God warned them not to become proud when they experienced prosperity.

He reminded them:

> “Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”
>
> — Deuteronomy 8:18

The warning was not against success itself.

The warning was against forgetting the One who gave them the ability to succeed.

This is an important Kingdom principle.

**Blessing should produce gratitude, not entitlement.**

When God increases our resources, we should become more aware of our responsibility, not less.

When God gives us greater influence, we should become more humble, not more proud.

When God gives us greater authority, we should become more accountable, not more independent.

### Greater Trust, Greater Responsibility

A king would not entrust an entire province to someone without expecting greater responsibility from that person.

The greater the assignment, the greater the accountability.

The same principle appears throughout Scripture.

Jesus said:

> “When someone has been given much, much will be required in return.”
>
> — Luke 12:48

This principle should change the way we view increase.

We often pray for more.

More money.

More opportunities.

More influence.

More success.

More responsibility.

There is nothing wrong with asking God for greater opportunities.

But we should also ask:

**“Am I ready to be faithful with more?”**

Because Kingdom increase is not merely about receiving more.

It is about becoming responsible for more.

### The King Does Not Need Our Possessions

There is another important truth.

God does not need our resources because He lacks something.

He owns everything already.

When we give, serve, build, lead, or sacrifice for God's purposes, we are not making God richer.

We are participating in what already belongs to Him.

David understood this when he said:

> “Everything we have has come from you, and we give you only what you first gave us.”
>
> — 1 Chronicles 29:14

This is the heart of generosity.

We are not giving God something that was completely ours.

We are returning something that first came from Him.

### The Steward's Greatest Privilege

Being a steward may sound like a position of limitation.

But it is actually a position of trust.

The King could have chosen not to entrust anything to us.

Instead, He allows us to participate in His purposes.

He allows us to use our hands to serve.

Our resources can help someone.

Our time can encourage someone.

Our abilities can build something useful.

Our influence can point someone toward God.

Our leadership can protect and guide others.

Our opportunities can become doors through which God's purposes are fulfilled.

The steward does not own the kingdom.

But the steward has the privilege of serving the King.

### Do Not Confuse Blessing With Ownership

Sometimes God allows a person to experience increase.

A business grows.

A career progresses.

A ministry expands.

Resources increase.

Influence becomes wider.

A position becomes greater.

These things can be blessings.

But blessing does not change ownership.

**The King remains the Owner.**

And therefore, increase should lead to deeper stewardship.

If God gives more money, manage more faithfully.

If God gives more influence, serve more responsibly.

If God gives more authority, lead with greater humility.

If God gives more opportunity, use it for His purposes.

If God gives more, ask not only:

**“What can I enjoy?”**

but also:

**“What responsibility has come with this increase?”**

### The Steward's Attitude

A faithful steward can say:

**“Lord, what I have came from You.”**

**“What I have belongs ultimately to You.”**

**“What You have entrusted to me has a purpose.”**

**“Teach me to manage it faithfully.”**

This attitude produces gratitude.

It produces humility.

It produces responsibility.

And it protects us from the dangerous belief that God's blessings make us independent from God.

The more we understand the King, the more clearly we understand our position.

We are not forgotten servants.

We are trusted stewards.

We are not owners of the Kingdom.

We are servants within it.

And the privilege of stewardship comes with responsibility.

## Bible Foundation

The central teaching of this chapter is supported by these passages:

- **1 Corinthians 4:7** — Everything we have is ultimately received from God.
- **Deuteronomy 8:18** — God gives the ability to produce wealth and success.
- **Luke 12:48** — Greater trust brings greater responsibility.
- **1 Chronicles 29:14** — What we give to God first came from Him.
- **Psalm 24:1** — Everything in the earth belongs to the Lord.

## Kingdom Principle

**The steward does not own what the King entrusts.**

The greater the trust, the greater the responsibility.

The greater the blessing, the greater the opportunity for faithfulness.

Protect my heart from entitlement and pride.

Teach me to receive Your blessings with gratitude and to carry Your responsibilities with humility.

If You entrust me with little, make me faithful.

If You entrust me with much, make me even more faithful.
