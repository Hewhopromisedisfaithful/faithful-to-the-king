# Chapter 20

## The Steward and Possessions

> **“The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it. The world and all its people belong to him.”**
> — Psalm 24:1

Everything we possess tells a story about stewardship.

A house.

A vehicle.

A phone.

A computer.

Clothing.

Tools.

Equipment.

Property.

Books.

Furniture.

Technology.

Business assets.

Even the things we consider small and ordinary.

We often say:

**“These are my things.”**

But Kingdom stewardship teaches us to think differently.

The question is not simply:

**“What do I own?”**

The question is:

**“What has the King entrusted to me, and how am I managing it?”**

---

## The King Owns Everything

Psalm 24:1 gives us the foundation:

> **“The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it.”**

God is the ultimate Owner.

We may legally own something according to human systems, but from a Kingdom perspective, everything ultimately belongs to God.

That means our possessions are not the final destination.

They are entrusted resources.

The house can be used to provide shelter.

The vehicle can be used to serve.

The computer can be used to create.

The tools can be used to work.

The table can be used to gather family and friends.

The business equipment can be used to produce value.

Possessions become meaningful when they serve a purpose.

---

## Possession Is Not the Same as Ownership

The steward can possess something without treating it as absolute personal property.

This sounds like a small distinction, but it changes everything.

If I think:

**“This is completely mine,”**

I may use it only for myself.

But if I think:

**“The King has entrusted this to me,”**

I begin to ask:

**“How should I use it?”**

That question introduces responsibility.

A steward does not merely consume.

He manages.

He maintains.

He protects.

He uses.

He shares when appropriate.

He preserves value.

He prepares for the future.

---

## The Purpose of Possessions

Possessions are not automatically wrong.

The Bible contains examples of people who had significant resources.

Abraham had great wealth.

Job had substantial possessions.

Joseph of Arimathea was wealthy.

The issue is not simply whether we possess things.

The issue is what possesses **our hearts**.

Jesus warned against storing up treasures only for ourselves.

In Luke 12:15 He said:

> **“Beware! Guard against every kind of greed. Life is not measured by how much you own.”**

Possessions have practical value.

But they cannot determine our worth.

They cannot give us identity.

They cannot replace God.

They cannot guarantee tomorrow.

---

## The Danger of Possessions Owning the Steward

A person may begin by owning possessions.

Eventually, the possessions can begin owning the person.

The house requires constant maintenance.

The vehicle demands expenses.

The business equipment requires attention.

The latest technology creates pressure to upgrade.

The desire for a better lifestyle creates endless comparison.

Soon, instead of possessions serving us, we become servants of possessions.

Jesus warned about this because the heart can become attached to material things.

The steward must therefore ask:

**“Do I own this possession, or has this possession begun to own me?”**

---

## Stewardship Includes Maintenance

One overlooked part of stewardship is maintenance.

We often want something new.

But faithfulness includes caring for what we already have.

A vehicle that is maintained can serve longer.

A tool that is cared for can remain useful.

A house that is maintained can remain a blessing.

Equipment that is properly handled can continue producing value.

Technology that is protected can remain useful.

A steward does not automatically replace something simply because it is no longer new.

He asks:

**“Can I responsibly maintain and maximize what I already have?”**

Stewardship values usefulness over unnecessary consumption.

---

## Excellence in Small Things

Jesus taught:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**
> — Luke 16:10

This applies to possessions too.

How do we treat inexpensive things?

How do we treat borrowed things?

How do we treat shared things?

How do we treat things nobody else notices?

Faithfulness is revealed in small details.

A person who returns borrowed property in good condition demonstrates stewardship.

A person who keeps shared spaces clean demonstrates stewardship.

A person who properly stores tools demonstrates stewardship.

A person who protects equipment demonstrates stewardship.

These things may seem ordinary.

But the Kingdom is built through ordinary faithfulness.

---

## Borrowed Things Are Still a Trust

Sometimes something in our hands does not belong to us at all.

It belongs to another person.

That makes stewardship even more important.

If someone lends you a tool, vehicle, device, book, or other possession, you have temporarily become a steward of something that belongs to someone else.

The proper response is care.

Return it.

Protect it.

Use it responsibly.

Do not assume that because it is temporarily in your hands, you can treat it however you want.

This principle extends beyond physical possessions.

Trust itself can be borrowed.

And trust should be handled carefully.

---

## Possessions and Contentment

The world constantly tells us that we need more.

A newer phone.

A bigger house.

A better vehicle.

More clothes.

More gadgets.

More equipment.

More luxury.

There is nothing inherently wrong with improvement.

But the steward must distinguish between **need, usefulness, and endless desire**.

Hebrews 13:5 says:

> **“Don’t love money; be satisfied with what you have.”**

Contentment protects the steward from becoming trapped in endless consumption.

A person can appreciate beautiful things without worshiping them.

A person can improve his possessions without allowing possessions to define him.

A person can enjoy blessings while remembering the Blesser.

---

## The Steward Avoids Waste

Stewardship also means reducing unnecessary waste.

Food should not be carelessly thrown away.

Resources should not be recklessly consumed.

Materials should not be wasted without reason.

Money should not be spent thoughtlessly.

Equipment should not be neglected until it becomes unusable.

God's provision should be treated with respect.

The principle is simple:

**If the King placed it in your hands, treat it as valuable.**

That does not mean becoming fearful or excessively restrictive.

It means becoming responsible.

---

## Possessions Can Become Instruments of Service

One of the most beautiful aspects of stewardship is that possessions can become tools for blessing.

A home can become a place of hospitality.

A vehicle can help someone who needs transportation.

A computer can help someone learn.

A camera can document something meaningful.

A kitchen can prepare food for people.

A business can create employment.

Tools can help someone work.

Money can help someone recover.

A possession becomes more valuable when it is used for a purpose greater than personal comfort.

The question becomes:

**“How can what I have become useful to someone else?”**

---

## Hospitality Is Stewardship

Romans 12:13 tells believers to:

> **“Always be eager to practice hospitality.”**

Hospitality does not require a perfect home.

It does not require expensive furniture.

It does not require a luxurious lifestyle.

It requires a willing heart.

The steward can use whatever he has to welcome, encourage, feed, comfort, and serve others.

A simple table can become a place where someone feels loved.

A small home can become a place where someone finds peace.

A simple meal can become an expression of generosity.

The value is not in the size of the possession.

It is in how faithfully it is used.

---

## Possessions and Generosity

The steward understands that not everything must remain in his hands.

Sometimes faithfulness means releasing something.

Acts 2:44–45 describes believers sharing their possessions to help those who had needs.

This does not mean that every Christian must give away everything.

It demonstrates something deeper:

**Possessions should never become more important than people.**

If holding onto something prevents us from helping someone whom God has placed before us, we should examine our hearts.

The steward must be willing to hold possessions with an open hand.

---

## The Open Hand

Imagine holding a possession tightly in your fist.

It is difficult to give.

It is difficult to share.

It is difficult to release.

Now imagine holding it with an open hand.

You can receive.

You can use.

You can share.

You can release.

That is the posture of a steward.

An open hand does not mean irresponsibility.

It means recognizing that the King can ask us to use or release what He entrusted.

We do not worship the resource.

We worship the One who provided it.

---

## Possessions and Identity

One of the greatest dangers of material possessions is allowing them to become our identity.

A person may think:

**“I am successful because of what I own.”**

**“I am important because of what I drive.”**

**“I am valuable because of the house I live in.”**

**“I am superior because I can afford more.”**

But Jesus teaches a different foundation.

Our identity comes from God.

Possessions can change overnight.

A house can be lost.

A business can fail.

A vehicle can break.

Technology can become outdated.

Money can disappear.

But our identity in Christ does not depend on these things.

The steward must therefore enjoy possessions without becoming possessed by them.

---

## The Steward Does Not Compare

Social media can make comparison especially powerful.

We see someone's home.

Someone's car.

Someone's vacation.

Someone's business.

Someone's lifestyle.

And suddenly our own blessings appear small.

But we rarely see the full story behind someone else's possessions.

Comparison can create dissatisfaction with what God has entrusted to us.

The steward should instead practice gratitude.

Look at what is already in your hands.

Ask how you can use it well.

Improve what needs improvement.

Release what needs to be released.

And thank God for what He has provided.

---

## Possessions and Responsibility to Others

Some possessions affect more than the owner.

A vehicle affects passengers.

A business affects employees and customers.

A house affects family members.

Technology can affect children.

Property can affect neighbors.

Therefore, stewardship is never completely isolated.

The steward asks:

**“How does the way I use this resource affect the people around me?”**

Kingdom stewardship considers consequences.

---

## The Difference Between Luxury and Idolatry

There is nothing inherently sinful about enjoying quality.

A beautiful house can be appreciated.

A good vehicle can be useful.

Quality tools can improve work.

Beautiful clothing can be enjoyed.

Excellent products can be valuable.

The danger comes when these things become objects of worship.

When possessions determine our worth.

When acquiring more becomes our life's purpose.

When we compromise integrity to obtain them.

When we refuse to help others because we are obsessed with keeping everything for ourselves.

The issue is not always **what we have**.

It is **what has our heart**.

---

## The Steward Leaves a Legacy

Possessions eventually pass from one generation to another.

This reminds us that material things are temporary.

We cannot take our houses with us.

We cannot take our vehicles.

We cannot take our equipment.

We cannot take our bank accounts.

But we can leave behind something more valuable:

Character.

Wisdom.

Faith.

Generosity.

Integrity.

A Kingdom legacy is not measured merely by what we leave people materially.

It is measured by what we teach them about faithful stewardship.

---

## Teach the Next Generation

Children should learn that possessions are blessings and responsibilities.

Teach them to care for things.

Teach them to return what they borrow.

Teach them not to waste.

Teach them to share.

Teach them to be grateful.

Teach them that having more does not make someone more valuable.

Teach them that money and possessions are tools, not masters.

The next generation needs more than resources.

They need wisdom to steward resources.

---

## When Possessions Are Lost

Sometimes stewardship is tested through loss.

A possession can break.

A business asset can disappear.

A home can be damaged.

Something valuable can be stolen.

A financial investment can fail.

Loss can hurt deeply.

But the steward must remember:

**The resource was never the King.**

God remains God even when possessions change.

Job experienced tremendous loss.

Yet his relationship with God remained the foundation of his life.

This does not mean loss is painless.

It means our hope does not ultimately depend on material things.

We can grieve loss while still trusting the King.

---

## The King Can Entrust More

The purpose of stewardship is not to prove that we deserve more.

It is to demonstrate faithfulness with what we already have.

If God increases our resources, we receive them with gratitude.

If He keeps our resources small, we remain faithful.

The steward does not say:

**“I will be responsible when I have more.”**

He says:

**“I will be responsible with what I have now.”**

Luke 16:10 remains the principle:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**

Faithfulness comes before increase.

---

## The Steward's Heart Toward Possessions

A mature steward can say:

**“I am thankful for what I have, but I am not defined by what I have.”**

**“I will care for my possessions, but I will not worship them.”**

**“I will enjoy God's blessings, but I will remember the Blesser.”**

**“I will protect what has been entrusted to me, but I will keep an open hand.”**

**“I will use my resources wisely, and I will remain ready to serve others with them.”**

That is freedom.

The steward possesses things without allowing things to possess him.

---

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

1. **Do I see my possessions as mine or as resources entrusted by the King?**
2. **Do I properly care for what God has placed in my hands?**
3. **Am I wasting resources that could be used wisely?**
4. **Have my possessions begun to define my identity?**
5. **Am I comparing what I have with what others have?**
6. **Am I willing to use my possessions to serve others?**
7. **Can I release something when the King asks me to?**
8. **What kind of stewardship am I teaching the next generation?**

These questions remind us that stewardship is not about having fewer things.

It is about having the **right relationship with the things we have**.

---

## The Greatest Possession

The greatest treasure is not what sits inside our house.

It is the presence of God in our lives.

Jesus said:

> **“Store your treasures in heaven.”**
> — Matthew 6:20

Earthly possessions have an expiration date.

Kingdom treasures do not.

Therefore, the steward should use temporary possessions for eternal purposes.

Use the house to love.

Use the resources to serve.

Use the tools to create.

Use the business to bless.

Use the opportunities to build.

Use what is temporary to accomplish something that lasts.

---

## The Steward's Declaration

Everything I have ultimately belongs to the King.

My possessions are entrusted resources, not objects of worship.

I will care for what God has placed in my hands.

I will avoid waste.

I will practice contentment.

I will not measure my worth by what I possess.

I will not allow comparison to steal my gratitude.

I will use my possessions to serve, provide, build, welcome, and bless others.

I will keep an open hand.

When the King gives, I will receive with gratitude.

When He asks me to use, I will use faithfully.

When He asks me to release, I will release with trust.

My possessions are temporary.

My stewardship matters.

**I will possess things without allowing things to possess me.

The King is my treasure.

And everything else is entrusted to me.**

---

## The Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of everything,**

Thank You for every possession and resource You have placed in my hands.

Forgive me for the times I have treated Your resources as though they were completely mine.

Teach me to care for what You have entrusted to me.

Give me wisdom to use resources properly.

Give me contentment when I have enough.

Give me discipline to avoid waste.

Give me generosity when someone needs help.

Protect my heart from greed, comparison, pride, and materialism.

Help me enjoy Your blessings without worshiping them.

Teach me to keep an open hand.

If You give more, help me remain humble.

If You ask me to release something, help me trust You.

May my home become a place of love.

May my possessions become tools of service.

May my resources become blessings.

May everything in my hands ultimately point back to You.

Because I know that nothing I possess is truly mine forever.

You are the Owner.

I am the steward.

And while these things are in my hands,

**I will manage them faithfully for Your Kingdom.**

In Jesus' name,

**Amen.**
