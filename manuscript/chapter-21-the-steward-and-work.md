# Chapter 21

## The Steward and Work

> **“Work willingly at whatever you do, as though you were working for the Lord rather than for people.”**
> — Colossians 3:23

Work is more than a way to earn money.

For the steward of the King, work is an opportunity to demonstrate **faithfulness, excellence, integrity, discipline, and service**.

Whether we work in a company, run a business, serve in ministry, manage a household, study, create something, or serve people in ways that may never receive recognition, our work matters to God.

The world may measure work by position, salary, title, recognition, or success.

The Kingdom measures it differently.

The question is not simply:

**“How successful am I?”**

The deeper question is:

**“How faithful am I with the work the King has placed in my hands?”**

---

## 1. The King Is the Ultimate Master

Paul gives us a powerful perspective:

> **“Work willingly at whatever you do, as though you were working for the Lord rather than for people.”**
> — Colossians 3:23

This changes the way we work.

When our only motivation is people, we may work hard when someone is watching and become careless when nobody is watching.

But when we understand that ultimately we are working for the Lord, our standard changes.

The King sees what people do not see.

He sees the preparation nobody notices.

He sees the effort nobody applauds.

He sees the integrity nobody rewards.

He sees the work completed when nobody is checking.

Therefore, the faithful steward does not ask only:

**“Will someone notice?”**

He asks:

**“Is the King pleased with how I am handling this responsibility?”**

---

## 2. Excellence Is Part of Stewardship

God deserves more than careless service.

Excellence does not mean perfection.

It means giving our best with the ability, resources, knowledge, and opportunity God has entrusted to us.

Proverbs 22:29 says:

> **“Do you see any truly competent workers? They will serve kings rather than working for ordinary people.”**

Competence matters.

Learning matters.

Improving matters.

Developing skills matters.

A steward should not use “God knows my heart” as an excuse for poor preparation or careless work.

If the King has entrusted something to us, we should seek to become better at managing it.

Excellence says:

**“Because this responsibility came into my hands, I will treat it with honor.”**

---

## 3. Faithfulness Matters Even in Small Work

Not every assignment will feel important.

Some responsibilities may seem ordinary.

Some work may be repetitive.

Some tasks may never bring recognition.

But Jesus said:

> **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”**
> — Luke 16:10

The Kingdom does not ignore small acts of faithfulness.

A person who consistently arrives on time, keeps promises, finishes assignments, takes care of entrusted resources, treats people respectfully, and does honest work is developing something deeper than a good reputation.

They are developing **character**.

Before God entrusts greater responsibility, He often develops faithfulness through ordinary responsibilities.

The small assignment may be preparing us for the larger assignment.

---

## 4. Integrity Must Remain When Nobody Is Watching

A steward's true character is revealed when there is no audience.

It is easy to appear honest when people are watching.

The test comes when dishonesty could benefit us and nobody seems likely to discover it.

The faithful steward remembers that the King is always watching.

Proverbs 10:9 says:

> **“People with integrity walk safely, but those who follow crooked paths will be exposed.”**

Integrity means our private behavior agrees with our public confession.

If we say we belong to the King, our work should reflect the values of the King.

We should not steal time.

We should not deceive customers.

We should not manipulate people.

We should not take credit for another person's work.

We should not intentionally produce poor work while pretending that it is excellent.

We should not compromise truth simply because compromise appears profitable.

The steward understands:

**I may work for an organization, a company, a client, or a customer—but my ultimate accountability is before the King.**

---

## 5. Work Is Also Service

Work is not only about what we receive.

It is also about what we contribute.

Jesus taught:

> **“For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others.”**
> — Mark 10:45

Kingdom stewardship changes our question.

Instead of asking only:

**“What can I get from this work?”**

we begin asking:

**“Whom can I serve through this work?”**

A teacher serves students.

A business owner serves customers and employees.

A driver serves passengers.

A builder serves families and communities.

A cleaner serves those who use the space.

A parent serves a household.

A student prepares himself or herself for future responsibility.

Every honest form of work can become an avenue of service.

Work becomes meaningful when we recognize the people affected by what we do.

---

## 6. Do Not Let Position Become Your Identity

A steward may have an important position today and a different position tomorrow.

A person may become a manager, then later become an employee again.

A business may grow and later face difficulty.

A title may be given and eventually removed.

But our identity must not depend entirely on our position.

Our position is something we steward.

It is not who we ultimately are.

This protects us from pride when we rise and despair when our position changes.

The King remains the King regardless of our position.

Therefore, whether we are leading ten people or serving one person, we can remain faithful.

**The value of stewardship is not determined by the size of the position.**

---

## 7. Work Without Becoming a Slave to Work

Stewardship does not mean working endlessly.

Work is important, but work itself must not become our master.

God established rhythms of work and rest.

Exodus 20:9–10 teaches the principle of working and also setting apart a Sabbath rest.

There is wisdom in knowing when to work and when to stop.

A person can become so focused on achievement that family, relationships, health, spiritual life, and rest are neglected.

That is not balanced stewardship.

The faithful steward understands that **time is also entrusted by the King**.

Therefore, our work must be managed within the larger responsibility God has given us.

We should work diligently without allowing work to become an idol.

---

## 8. When Work Is Difficult

Not every season of work will be enjoyable.

There will be difficult supervisors.

Difficult customers.

Unexpected problems.

Unfair situations.

Long days.

Failed projects.

Financial pressure.

Disappointments.

Sometimes the steward may even wonder whether the work is worth continuing.

During those seasons, faithfulness becomes especially important.

Galatians 6:9 says:

> **“So let’s not get tired of doing what is good. At just the right time we will reap a harvest of blessing if we don’t give up.”**

Faithfulness does not mean ignoring legitimate problems.

It means refusing to allow difficulty to destroy our character.

We can seek wisdom.

We can correct what needs correcting.

We can establish healthy boundaries.

We can pursue better opportunities.

But wherever God has presently placed us, we should handle our responsibility with integrity.

---

## 9. The Steward Must Keep Learning

A faithful worker should remain teachable.

Proverbs 1:5 says:

> **“Let the wise listen to these proverbs and become even wiser. Let those with understanding receive guidance.”**

There is danger in thinking:

**“I already know enough.”**

The world changes.

Industries change.

Technology changes.

Methods change.

People change.

New responsibilities require new knowledge.

A steward who keeps learning increases his capacity to serve.

Learning is not a sign that we are inadequate.

It is often evidence that we recognize the responsibility God has given us.

The question becomes:

**“What do I need to learn so I can steward this responsibility better?”**

---

## 10. Work Can Become Worship

When we understand stewardship correctly, even ordinary work can become an offering to God.

Not because every task is easy.

Not because every workplace is perfect.

But because our attitude can honor the One who entrusted the opportunity to us.

Romans 12:1 speaks of presenting our lives to God as a living sacrifice.

Our worship is not limited to singing inside a church building.

The way we work can also testify about the King we serve.

When we work honestly, serve faithfully, treat people with dignity, pursue excellence, and refuse corruption, our work becomes part of our witness.

People may never read our Bible.

But they may observe our character.

They may see our honesty.

They may experience our service.

They may notice our consistency.

And through those things, they may see something of the King we serve.

---

## 11. What Happens When the Work Is Done?

Every assignment eventually reaches an end.

Projects finish.

Contracts expire.

Businesses change.

Careers transition.

Seasons close.

One day, our earthly work will also be finished.

Jesus told the parable of the talents in Matthew 25:14–30. The servants were not judged merely by what they had received, but by how they handled what had been entrusted to them.

That is the heart of stewardship.

The King does not ask us to possess everything.

He asks us to be faithful with what He has placed in our hands.

The final question is not:

**“Did you have the biggest position?”**

Not:

**“Did everyone recognize you?”**

Not:

**“Did you become famous?”**

But:

**“Were you faithful?”**

---

# The Steward's Perspective

The world often says:

**“Work so you can become somebody.”**

The Kingdom says:

**“Work faithfully because you already belong to the King.”**

The world may say:

**“Get the highest position.”**

The Kingdom teaches:

**“Be faithful wherever you are placed.”**

The world may say:

**“Work only when someone is watching.”**

The Kingdom says:

**“Remember that the King sees.”**

The world may measure success by what we gain.

The steward measures responsibility by how faithfully he manages what has been entrusted.

This is the difference between working merely for a paycheck and working with a **Kingdom mindset**.

---

# Heart Check

Ask yourself honestly:

1. **Do I treat my work as something entrusted to me by God?**
2. **Do I give my best even when nobody is watching?**
3. **Am I growing in competence and skill?**
4. **Is my work characterized by honesty and integrity?**
5. **Do I treat the people I serve with dignity?**
6. **Have I allowed my position or career to become my identity?**
7. **Am I balancing work with the other responsibilities God has entrusted to me?**
8. **What area of my work needs greater faithfulness today?**

The goal is not to become the most impressive worker.

The goal is to become a **faithful steward**.

---

# Steward's Declaration

**I am a steward of the King.**

The work placed in my hands is not meaningless.

I will work with diligence, integrity, excellence, and humility.

I will not work only when people are watching, because I remember that the King sees.

I will continue learning, growing, and improving.

I will serve people through the work entrusted to me.

I will not allow position, recognition, or success to become my identity.

I will work faithfully without making work my master.

Whatever assignment is placed before me, I will seek to honor the King through it.

**I do not need to be recognized by everyone to be faithful before God.

I only need to faithfully steward what He has placed in my hands.**

---

# Steward's Prayer

**King and Owner of all,**

Thank You for every opportunity to work, serve, create, build, learn, and contribute.

Forgive me for the times I have treated my work carelessly or allowed recognition, money, position, or success to become more important than honoring You.

Teach me to work with excellence without becoming proud.

Teach me to work with humility without thinking that my work has no value.

Give me integrity when nobody is watching.

Give me strength when the work becomes difficult.

Give me wisdom to keep learning and growing.

Help me to remember that the people I serve are not merely customers, coworkers, employees, or clients. They are people created by You.

May my work reflect the character of the King I serve.

And when the assignment You have given me is complete, may I be found faithful.

**I am not the owner.

I am the steward.

And whatever You place in my hands, may I handle it faithfully for Your glory.**

In Jesus' name,
**Amen.**
