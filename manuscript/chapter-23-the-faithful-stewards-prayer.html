# Chapter 23 ## The Faithful Steward’s Prayer > **“Moreover it is required in stewards that one be found faithful.”** > — 1 Corinthians 4:2 (NKJV) A steward eventually comes to a place where words are no longer enough. It is one thing to say that God is the Owner. It is another thing to live as though He truly is. It is one thing to say, “Everything I have belongs to God.” It is another thing to surrender our time, resources, abilities, relationships, opportunities, and influence into His hands. This is where stewardship becomes more than a responsibility. It becomes worship. The faithful steward does not simply ask, “What can I receive from the King?” He also asks: **“What does the King desire me to do with what He has entrusted to me?”** That question changes everything. --- ## The King Owns It All Scripture reminds us: > **“The earth is the Lord’s, and all its fullness, The world and those who dwell therein.”** > — Psalm 24:1 (NKJV) Before we can become faithful stewards, we must settle the question of ownership. God is the Owner. We are stewards. Our life is entrusted to us. Our possessions are entrusted to us. Our abilities are entrusted to us. Our opportunities are entrusted to us. Even the relationships and positions placed in our hands are responsibilities that ultimately belong to Him. This truth protects our hearts from pride. When we understand that we are stewards, we no longer have to live as though everything depends on us. We are responsible for our faithfulness, but we are not the King. The King carries the authority. The King establishes the purpose. The King determines what ultimately matters. Our role is to faithfully manage what He has placed in our hands. --- ## A Steward Must Have a Willing Heart Faithfulness is not merely about performing duties. God looks at the heart behind the stewardship. A person may give, serve, work, lead, build, and accomplish many things while still carrying an unwilling heart. But the faithful steward learns to say: **“Lord, I do not want merely to use what You gave me for my own purposes. Teach me to use it according to Your purposes.”** That is the heart of surrender. Jesus taught that where our treasure is, our heart will also be. > **“For where your treasure is, there your heart will be also.”** > — Matthew 6:21 (NKJV) Our stewardship reveals something about our hearts. How we use money reveals something. How we use time reveals something. How we treat people reveals something. How we handle opportunities reveals something. How we respond when God gives us more reveals something. And how we respond when He asks us to release something reveals something as well. The faithful steward therefore does not only ask God to bless what is in his hands. He asks God to govern his heart. --- ## “Teach Me to Be Faithful” Sometimes we pray for greater things while God is teaching us faithfulness with the things already in front of us. We may desire a larger assignment while overlooking today's responsibility. We may pray for greater influence while failing to steward the people already within our reach. We may ask God for abundance while neglecting the little that He has already entrusted to us. But Jesus taught an important kingdom principle: > **“He who is faithful in what is least is faithful also in much.”** > — Luke 16:10 (NKJV) The little matters. The ordinary matters. The hidden work matters. The small acts of obedience matter. The faithful steward does not despise small beginnings because he understands that the King watches faithfulness, not merely visibility. A task may look insignificant to people and yet be significant before God. A responsibility may never receive applause and yet be precious to the King. Therefore our prayer should not always be: **“Lord, give me more.”** Sometimes the better prayer is: **“Lord, make me faithful with what I already have.”** --- ## When the King Gives More There is nothing wrong with receiving increase. There is nothing wrong with growth, opportunity, success, or greater responsibility when they come from God. But increase brings responsibility. If the King places more in our hands, He is not merely giving us more to enjoy. He may also be giving us more to steward. More resources can mean more responsibility. More influence can mean more people to serve. More authority can mean greater accountability. More opportunities can mean greater expectations. More possessions can mean more opportunities to bless others. This is why the faithful steward must continually return to the King. **“Lord, what do You want me to do with this?”** That question keeps success from becoming self-worship. It keeps blessing from becoming entitlement. It keeps influence from becoming pride. It reminds us that the crown still belongs to the King. --- ## When the King Asks Us to Release Stewardship is also tested when God asks us to let go. Sometimes we become attached to what was only entrusted to us. We begin to say: **“This is mine.”** But the King may remind us: **“It was always Mine.”** Perhaps He asks us to release money. Perhaps an opportunity. Perhaps a position. Perhaps a relationship. Perhaps an expectation. Perhaps something we worked hard to build. The faithful steward learns that surrender is not losing ownership of something that belongs to us. It is returning something to the One who owned it all along. Abraham's willingness to offer Isaac is a powerful picture of this kind of trust. What God had promised Abraham had become deeply precious to him, yet Abraham still had to trust the Giver above the gift. The lesson is not that everything precious must be taken away. The lesson is that **nothing entrusted by God should become more important to us than God Himself.** The King must remain King. --- ## The Steward's Greatest Treasure After everything has been said about money, possessions, time, talents, opportunities, leadership, relationships, and influence, we must remember something greater: **The greatest treasure of the steward is not what is in his hands. It is the King whom he serves.** If God gives much, we serve Him. If God gives little, we serve Him. If the season is full, we serve Him. If the season is difficult, we serve Him. If people notice our work, we serve Him. If nobody notices, we still serve Him. Our faithfulness cannot depend entirely on what we receive in return. Why? Because our reward is ultimately connected to the King Himself. Paul reminded believers that their labor in the Lord is not meaningless: > **“Therefore, my beloved brethren, be steadfast, immovable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, knowing that your labor is not in vain in the Lord.”** > — 1 Corinthians 15:58 (NKJV) The faithful steward serves because the King is worthy. --- ## A Prayer of Surrender At the end of every lesson about stewardship, there comes a moment when knowledge must become surrender. We can understand stewardship and still fail to practice it. We can know that God owns everything and still hold tightly to what He has entrusted. We can talk about faithfulness and still live for ourselves. So the faithful steward must continually bring everything back before the King. Not only our possessions. Our hearts. Not only our money. Our motives. Not only our abilities. Our ambitions. Not only our opportunities. Our plans. Everything. The prayer of the faithful steward is therefore not complicated. It is surrendered. --- ## The Faithful Steward’s Prayer **King of Kings,** Everything I have comes from You. My life is Yours. My time is Yours. My strength is Yours. My abilities are Yours. My resources are Yours. My opportunities are Yours. My relationships are Yours. My influence is Yours. Everything placed in my hands has first passed through Yours. Forgive me for the times I have treated Your blessings as though they belonged entirely to me. Forgive me when I have pursued the gift more than the Giver. Forgive me when I have used what You entrusted for my own glory instead of Your purpose. Teach me to remember that I am a steward and You are the Owner. Give me wisdom to manage what You have placed in my hands. Give me discipline when I am tempted to waste it. Give me humility when You give me increase. Give me patience when I am entrusted with little. Give me courage when faithfulness becomes difficult. Give me a generous heart when someone needs what You have placed in my hands. Give me clean motives when You give me influence. Give me wisdom when You give me authority. And when You ask me to release something, give me the faith to trust You. Lord, do not allow Your blessings to become idols in my heart. Do not allow success to make me proud. Do not allow lack to make me unfaithful. Do not allow recognition to become my reward. Teach me to serve You whether people see me or not. Teach me to honor You in the small things and in the great things. Teach me to be faithful with what I have today. And if You entrust more to me tomorrow, prepare my heart to carry it faithfully. I do not want merely to have more. I want to be more faithful. I do not want merely to build something great. I want to build what honors You. I do not want my name to be remembered above Yours. I want everything You place in my hands to ultimately point back to You. You are the King. I am Your steward. You are the Owner. I am entrusted. You are the Master. I am called to be faithful. May everything I have, everything I am, and everything I become be used for Your glory. And when my stewardship is finally finished, may I hear the words I long to hear: **“Well done, good and faithful servant.”** In Jesus' name, **Amen.** --- ## The Final Question of the Steward The journey of stewardship always brings us back to one question: **How faithful am I with what the King has placed in my hands?** Not: “How much do I have?” Not: “How important am I?” Not: “How much recognition have I received?” Not: “How much has God given me compared with someone else?” But: **“Have I been faithful?”** The King does not ask every steward to carry the same assignment. He does not give everyone the same resources. He does not give everyone the same opportunities. He does not give everyone the same influence. But every steward receives the same responsibility: **Be faithful.** One day, everything we currently hold will pass from our hands. Money will pass. Positions will pass. Possessions will pass. Opportunities will pass. Earthly recognition will pass. But faithfulness to the King will never be wasted. So let this be the prayer of every steward: **“Lord, whatever You place in my hands, teach me to hold it with open hands, manage it with wisdom, use it with purpose, and surrender it whenever You ask.”** Because in the end, stewardship was never about proving how much we can possess. It was always about proving how faithfully we can serve the One who owns it all. **The King owns everything. The steward manages everything entrusted. And faithfulness is the measure that matters.**
