# Chapter 24 ## The Steward and Legacy > **“We will not hide them from their children, telling to the generation to come the praises of the Lord, and His strength and His wonderful works that He has done.”** > — Psalm 78:4 (NKJV) A faithful steward understands that stewardship is not only about the present. What God places in our hands today may affect people we may never meet. Our decisions can shape a family. Our character can influence a generation. Our obedience can open a path for someone who comes after us. Our faithfulness can become a testimony that continues speaking even when our own voice is no longer heard. This is part of kingdom stewardship. The question is not only: **“What am I doing with what God has entrusted to me today?”** It is also: **“What am I preparing for those who will come after me?”** --- ## Stewardship That Outlives Us Everything earthly eventually passes from one person to another. A house can be inherited. A business can be transferred. Money can be passed to another generation. A position can be handed to someone else. But the most valuable things we leave behind are not always the things that can be placed in someone's hands. Faith can be passed on. Wisdom can be passed on. Character can be demonstrated. Truth can be taught. Generosity can be modeled. A love for God can be cultivated. A kingdom mindset can be planted in the hearts of others. This is why legacy is not simply about what we leave **to** people. It is also about what we leave **within** people. A person may inherit wealth and still lack wisdom. A person may inherit a business and still lack character. A person may inherit possessions and still not know the God who provided them. Therefore, a faithful steward must think beyond inheritance. He must think about **formation**. --- ## Tell the Next Generation Psalm 78 gives us a beautiful picture of generational stewardship. The people of God were instructed not to hide the works of God from their children, but to tell the coming generation about His faithfulness. > **“That the generation to come might know them, The children who would be born, That they may arise and declare them to their children.”** > — Psalm 78:6 (NKJV) Notice the movement. One generation tells another. Then that generation tells the next. The testimony continues. This is not merely the preservation of information. It is the preservation of remembrance. Every generation needs to know: **God is faithful.** God has provided. God has corrected. God has protected. God has answered prayer. God has remained faithful even when people were not. A steward who has experienced God's faithfulness carries a responsibility to testify about it. We should not assume that the next generation will automatically know what God has done. Someone must tell them. Someone must demonstrate it. Someone must live it. --- ## Legacy Begins With Character It is tempting to think about legacy in terms of achievements. What did I build? How much did I earn? What business did I establish? What property did I acquire? How many people knew my name? But kingdom legacy asks deeper questions. **Was I faithful?** **Did I walk with integrity?** **Did I honor God when nobody was watching?** **Did I treat people with love and dignity?** **Did I use influence to serve or to control?** **Did I teach others to trust God?** These questions reach the heart of legacy. Because people may forget some of our accomplishments, but they often remember how we lived. A child may not remember every financial sacrifice a parent made. But that child may remember seeing that parent pray. A young person may forget many instructions. But they may remember the integrity of the person who taught them. A future leader may not remember every lesson. But they may remember someone who faithfully served without demanding recognition. Legacy begins long before anyone calls it legacy. It begins with ordinary faithfulness. --- ## What Are We Really Passing On? Imagine that everything material we have accumulated could suddenly disappear. What would remain? Would there still be faith? Would there still be wisdom? Would there still be people who learned how to trust God because they watched our lives? Would there still be principles that continue guiding others? Would there still be people who know that God was honored in the way we lived? This question exposes something important. **Legacy is not measured only by what remains after us. It is also measured by what remains in others because of us.** The Apostle Paul understood this principle. He instructed Timothy: > **“And the things that you have heard from me among many witnesses, commit these to faithful men who will be able to teach others also.”** > — 2 Timothy 2:2 (NKJV) Look at the multiplication: Paul taught Timothy. Timothy was instructed to teach faithful people. Those people would teach others. The truth continued moving forward. That is kingdom stewardship. We receive. We steward. We teach. We release. Others continue. --- ## Do Not Keep Everything to Yourself A steward who refuses to share what God has taught him may become a storage container instead of a channel. God did not give us wisdom merely so that we could keep it locked inside ourselves. If God has taught us something through experience, Scripture, correction, failure, or blessing, there may be someone who can benefit from what we have learned. This does not mean that we must present ourselves as having all the answers. A faithful steward can say: **“I am still learning, but this is what God has taught me.”** That kind of testimony is powerful because it points beyond the steward. It points to the King. We do not need to pretend that we have lived perfectly. We can tell the truth about God's faithfulness through imperfect people. That itself becomes part of the legacy. --- ## A Legacy of Faithfulness Consider a person who never became famous. No large platform. No public recognition. No impressive title. Yet that person faithfully loved God, served people, raised a family with wisdom, worked honestly, gave generously, prayed consistently, and taught others to trust the Lord. From the world's perspective, that life might appear ordinary. From the kingdom's perspective, it may have produced consequences that cannot be fully measured. One prayer may have strengthened a child. One act of generosity may have changed a family's circumstances. One word of encouragement may have prevented someone from giving up. One example of integrity may have taught another person how to lead. One faithful life may have influenced generations. This is why we should never underestimate ordinary faithfulness. The King sees what people do not see. --- ## Stewarding Our Influence Everyone has influence, even if that influence is small. A parent has influence. A teacher has influence. A business owner has influence. A pastor has influence. A friend has influence. A brother or sister has influence. A leader has influence. Even someone who believes they have little influence may be shaping people around them through their daily example. Influence is therefore another resource entrusted by God. And influence carries responsibility. If God gives us influence, we should not ask only: **“How many people are following me?”** We should ask: **“Where am I leading them?”** Kingdom influence does not exist primarily to make the steward greater. It exists to help point people toward what is good, true, and honoring to God. Jesus said: > **“Let your light so shine before men, that they may see your good works and glorify your Father in heaven.”** > — Matthew 5:16 (NKJV) Notice the destination of the light. People may see the steward's good works. But the glory ultimately belongs to the Father. That is the difference between influence for self and influence for the Kingdom. --- ## Teach Before You Leave A wise steward does not wait until the end to think about succession. If something matters, teach it. If a responsibility must continue, prepare someone. If wisdom has been gained through experience, share it. If a skill can bless others, train them. If a ministry, business, family responsibility, or calling has been entrusted to you, think about how it can continue according to God's purposes. Moses prepared Joshua. Paul invested in Timothy. Jesus prepared His disciples. The pattern is clear: **Faithfulness includes preparation.** We should not build something that depends entirely on our personal presence. A mature steward asks: **“Can this continue faithfully after I am gone?”** That question requires humility. Because it means accepting that we are temporary stewards. The work belongs to God. We are simply entrusted with a portion of it for a season. --- ## Legacy Requires Open Hands One of the hardest lessons in stewardship is learning to release. We may build something and become deeply attached to it. We may lead something and begin to identify ourselves with it. We may accumulate knowledge and feel that nobody can do it as well as we can. But eventually, every steward must release something. A position. A responsibility. A business. A ministry assignment. A possession. Even our own earthly life. The faithful steward therefore prepares his heart to release what God entrusted. This does not mean that we stop caring. It means we understand ownership. **God owns the work.** **God owns the future.** **God owns the next generation.** Our responsibility is to be faithful while the assignment is in our hands and to release it faithfully when the King says it is time. --- ## Do Not Build Only for Your Name There is a subtle danger in legacy. We can begin by wanting to honor God and slowly begin wanting people to remember us. We can build something for God's glory and eventually become attached to our own name. We can serve people and eventually crave their admiration. But kingdom stewardship continually returns the glory to the King. The question is not: **“Will they remember my name?”** The greater question is: **“Will they remember the God I served?”** A legacy centered on the steward eventually fades. A legacy that points people toward God can continue beyond the steward. John the Baptist demonstrated this posture when he said: > **“He must increase, but I must decrease.”** > — John 3:30 (NKJV) That is a powerful principle for every steward. The purpose of our stewardship is not to make ourselves permanent. It is to make the King known. --- ## Building for the Generation to Come Deuteronomy teaches God's people to keep His words in their hearts and to teach them diligently to their children. > **“And you shall teach them diligently to your children, and shall talk of them when you sit in your house, when you walk by the way, when you lie down, and when you rise up.”** > — Deuteronomy 6:7 (NKJV) Notice that teaching was not limited to a special event. It was woven into everyday life. This reminds us that legacy is often built through ordinary moments. A conversation. A meal. A correction. A prayer. A decision. A demonstration of forgiveness. A moment when we choose integrity instead of convenience. A moment when we choose generosity instead of selfishness. These moments may appear small. But repeated faithfully, they shape people. Legacy is often built quietly. --- ## What Will Your Hands Leave Behind? One day, our hands will no longer hold what they hold today. Someone else may manage what we built. Someone else may live in what we purchased. Someone else may continue what we started. Someone else may carry responsibilities that once belonged to us. That reality should not frighten the faithful steward. It should motivate us. Because we still have time to prepare. We can teach. We can mentor. We can forgive. We can give. We can encourage. We can demonstrate faith. We can correct what needs correction. We can prepare others to carry what God may place in their hands. So ask yourself: **What am I putting into people that will remain after I am gone?** What values? What wisdom? What faith? What example? What kingdom principles? What testimony? These may become part of the greatest legacy God allows us to leave. --- ## The Legacy of a Faithful Steward A faithful steward does not need to leave behind a monument with his name written on it. He can leave behind people who know God. He can leave behind people who understand integrity. He can leave behind people who learned generosity. He can leave behind people who learned responsibility. He can leave behind people who understand that blessings are entrusted, not merely possessed. He can leave behind people who know how to serve the King. That is kingdom legacy. And perhaps the most beautiful part is this: We may never know the full impact of our faithfulness. A lesson we teach may be remembered years later. A prayer we pray may influence a future decision. A sacrifice we make may create an opportunity for someone else. A principle we model may be repeated by people we never meet. The chain can continue far beyond our sight. That is why we should be faithful today. --- ## A Steward's Legacy Prayer **King of Kings,** Thank You for allowing me to be part of something greater than myself. Thank You for every person You have placed within my life. Thank You for every opportunity to teach, serve, encourage, guide, and give. Help me understand that my stewardship is not only about what I do with what You have placed in my hands. It is also about what I place into the lives of others. Help me leave behind faith rather than fear. Wisdom rather than confusion. Integrity rather than compromise. Generosity rather than selfishness. Humility rather than pride. Truth rather than deception. And a love for You that will continue beyond my own lifetime. Teach me to tell the next generation about Your faithfulness. Help me not to hide the lessons You have taught me. Give me wisdom to prepare others. Give me humility to teach. Give me patience to mentor. Give me courage to correct when correction is needed. Give me generosity to share what You have entrusted to me. And when the time comes to release a responsibility, help me release it without fear, knowing that Your Kingdom does not depend entirely upon me. Lord, keep me from building a legacy for my own name. Let everything I build point back to You. If people remember anything about my life, let them remember that You were faithful. If they remember my work, let it remind them of Your goodness. If they remember my words, let them lead them toward Your truth. If they remember my life, let them see evidence that serving You was worth everything. I do not know how many generations will be affected by the decisions I make today. But I ask You to make my faithfulness useful in Your hands. Let what You have taught me continue through others. Let what You have entrusted to me become a blessing beyond me. And let the legacy of my life ultimately belong to You. You are the King. Everything belongs to You. My generation belongs to You. The next generation belongs to You. And whatever comes after them belongs to You. May I faithfully steward my generation so that those who come after me may know You, trust You, serve You, and glorify You. In Jesus' name, **Amen.** --- ## The Steward's Legacy At the end of stewardship, the question is not simply: **“What did I leave behind?”** It is: **“Whom did my faithfulness help prepare?”** We are temporary stewards of eternal purposes. We may hold something for a season, but we do not hold it forever. Therefore, let us not live merely to accumulate. Let us live to cultivate. Let us cultivate faith. Cultivate wisdom. Cultivate character. Cultivate generosity. Cultivate responsibility. Cultivate people. Cultivate a kingdom mindset. Because someday, what is in our hands will be placed into someone else's hands. And when that day comes, may we be able to say: **“Lord, I did not keep everything for myself. I faithfully passed on what You entrusted to me.”** The greatest legacy of a steward is not a name carved in stone. It is a life that helped another person know the King. **We receive from the King. We steward for the King. We prepare others for the King. And ultimately, we return everything to the King.** That is the legacy of a faithful steward.
