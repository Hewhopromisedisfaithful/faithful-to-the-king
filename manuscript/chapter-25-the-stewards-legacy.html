# Chapter 25 ## The Steward’s Legacy A faithful steward does not live only for what can be seen today. He understands that his faithfulness can reach farther than his own lifetime. Every decision, every act of obedience, every resource handled with integrity, every person served with love, and every responsibility carried faithfully can leave something behind. Not always a monument. Not always a name. Not always something the world will recognize. But something that continues to point to the King. The question is not simply, “What will people remember about me?” The deeper question is: **“What will my life leave behind that belongs to the King?”** ### We Are Stewards, Not Owners Everything we have received was first placed in our hands by God. Our time. Our abilities. Our opportunities. Our relationships. Our resources. Our influence. Our work. Our position. Even the years of our lives are entrusted to us. Psalm 24:1 reminds us: > “The earth is the LORD’s, and everything in it.” If everything belongs to Him, then our lives are not ultimately about building something that carries our name. We are called to manage what belongs to Him in a way that honors Him. That changes the meaning of legacy. Legacy is not primarily about how much we accumulated. It is about how faithfully we stewarded what the King entrusted to us. ### A Legacy of Faithfulness Some people leave behind wealth. Others leave behind businesses, buildings, books, achievements, or positions. These things may have value, but they are not necessarily the greatest measure of a person's faithfulness. A person may leave very little material wealth and yet leave behind an enormous spiritual legacy. A parent who taught a child to pray. A leader who chose integrity when compromise was easier. A worker who served faithfully even when nobody was watching. A businessman who refused dishonest gain. A believer who remained faithful during a difficult season. A person who encouraged someone who was ready to give up. These things may never appear in a history book. But the King sees them. Hebrews 6:10 says: > “For God is not unjust. He will not forget how hard you have worked for him and how you have shown your love to him.” The world may forget. People may overlook. Records may disappear. But the King does not forget faithfulness. ### What Are We Passing On? Every steward is passing something to the next generation. The question is: **What are we passing on?** Are we passing on faith or fear? Integrity or compromise? Generosity or selfishness? Humility or pride? Wisdom or foolishness? Obedience or rebellion? A hunger for God or a hunger only for success? Our lives teach even when our mouths are silent. People watch how we respond when we have little. They watch how we behave when we have much. They watch how we treat people who cannot benefit us. They watch what we do when nobody is applauding. They watch whether our faith remains when circumstances do not change. This is why stewardship is never only about possessions. **Our lives themselves are a message.** ### The Legacy of Character A faithful steward understands that character is part of the inheritance we leave behind. Money can be spent. Buildings can be destroyed. Positions can be transferred. Businesses can change hands. But character can influence generations. A person who consistently chooses truth teaches others that truth matters. A person who keeps his word teaches others that integrity matters. A person who forgives teaches others that grace matters. A person who remains humble after receiving honor teaches others that promotion does not make a person greater than the King. This is why Proverbs 22:1 says: > “Choose a good reputation over great riches; being held in high esteem is better than silver or gold.” The faithful steward does not merely ask, “How much can I gain?” He also asks: **“Who am I becoming while I manage what God has given me?”** ### The Legacy of Obedience Sometimes the greatest legacy is created through obedience that nobody sees. God may ask a person to begin something small. To serve quietly. To give when it is difficult. To forgive when there is no apology. To remain faithful when there is no immediate reward. To continue when the results seem slow. The steward may wonder whether such obedience matters. It does. A seed does not look like a tree. A beginning does not look like a finished work. A single act of obedience may become part of a story that continues long after the person who obeyed is gone. This is why we should never measure obedience only by immediate results. **Our responsibility is faithfulness. The King is responsible for the fruit.** ### Do Not Build a Legacy for Your Name There is a subtle danger even in doing good things. A steward can begin by serving the King and eventually begin serving his own name. He can start with: “Lord, let this bring You glory.” And slowly change to: “Lord, let people see what I have accomplished.” The difference may look small, but the heart has changed. Jesus warned His followers about doing righteous acts merely to be seen by others. The faithful steward remembers: **The work may pass through my hands, but it belongs to the King.** If God allows us to accomplish something significant, we do not have to steal His glory. We can simply say: “Thank You, Lord. You trusted me with this.” That is stewardship. ### When the Steward Is No Longer There One day, every steward will leave the assignment. Someone else will manage the business. Someone else will occupy the position. Someone else will use the resources. Someone else will continue the work. This should humble us. We were never owners. We were entrusted for a season. There is freedom in understanding this. We do not have to control everything forever. We do not have to make sure our name remains attached to everything we touched. We simply need to be faithful while the King has placed it in our hands. Paul understood this kind of perspective. Near the end of his life, he wrote: > “I have fought the good fight, I have finished the race, and I have remained faithful.” — 2 Timothy 4:7 Notice what Paul emphasized. He did not say, “I became famous.” He did not say, “Everyone approved of me.” He did not say, “Everything went according to my plans.” He said: **“I have remained faithful.”** That is the legacy of a steward. ### The Greatest Legacy The greatest legacy we can leave is not a name that people remember. It is a life that helped people remember the King. If our children become more faithful because they watched our faithfulness, that is legacy. If someone learns integrity because they saw us choose what was right, that is legacy. If someone comes closer to God because our life reflected His character, that is legacy. If someone discovers courage because we encouraged them, that is legacy. If something we built continues serving people after we are gone, that is legacy. And if nobody remembers our name but God was honored through our obedience, **our stewardship was not wasted.** ### One Day, We Will Give an Account The reality of stewardship becomes most serious when we remember that one day we will answer to the One who entrusted everything to us. Jesus taught about servants who were entrusted with their master's resources. The faithful servants were commended because they were trustworthy with what they had received. The message is simple: **Faithfulness matters to the King.** Not comparison. Not competition. Not appearance. Faithfulness. We do not all receive the same assignment. We do not all receive the same amount of resources. We do not all have the same opportunities. But every steward has the same responsibility: **Be faithful with what the King has placed in your hands.** ### What Will Remain? Someday, many things we consider important today will no longer matter. The possessions will remain behind. The position will belong to someone else. The money will be distributed. The accomplishments will become history. The applause will become silence. But the faithfulness we offered to God will never have been meaningless. So perhaps the question we should ask today is not: **“How much will I leave behind?”** But: **“What kind of steward am I becoming?”** Am I becoming more faithful? More generous? More humble? More responsible? More obedient? More willing to serve? More willing to release what belongs to the King? More concerned with His glory than my own? These questions matter because our legacy is being formed now. Every ordinary day is part of it. ### A Legacy That Points to the King The steward's greatest desire should be that when people look at his life, they do not stop at the steward. They see the King. That is the purpose. We are not the destination. We are servants pointing toward the One who entrusted everything to us. Our work can point to Him. Our business can point to Him. Our generosity can point to Him. Our leadership can point to Him. Our relationships can point to Him. Our endurance can point to Him. Even our failures, when surrendered and redeemed by His grace, can become testimonies of His faithfulness. The legacy of a faithful steward is therefore not: **“Look what I built.”** It is: **“Look what the King was able to do through what He entrusted to me.”** And when our assignment is finally complete, may the greatest words we long to hear be the words spoken by our Master: > “Well done, my good and faithful servant.” — Matthew 25:21 That is the legacy worth pursuing. Not a legacy that makes our name great. A legacy that makes the King's name known. Not a life that says, “I owned everything.” But a life that says: **“Everything belonged to Him, and I was honored to be trusted with it.”** ### Reflection Take a moment and ask yourself: * What has the King entrusted to me in this season? * What am I teaching others through the way I live? * What kind of character am I passing to the next generation? * Am I building for my own name or for the King's glory? * If my assignment ended today, what evidence of faithfulness would remain? The goal is not to leave behind the biggest legacy. The goal is to leave behind a **faithful one**. Because in the kingdom of God, greatness is not ultimately measured by how much we possessed. It is measured by how faithfully we stewarded what belonged to the King. And when everything is finally returned to Him, may we be able to say with confidence: **“King, You entrusted it to me. I did my best to remain faithful. And now, it all belongs back to You.”** ### Prayer **King of Kings,** Thank You for trusting me with the things You have placed in my hands. Teach me not to live for my own name, recognition, or glory. Teach me to remember that everything belongs to You and that I am only a steward. Help me to leave behind a legacy of faithfulness, integrity, generosity, humility, obedience, and love. Let my life influence others not merely to remember me, but to remember You. When I receive much, keep me humble. When I have little, keep me faithful. When I am recognized, keep my heart surrendered. When nobody sees me, remind me that You see. And when my assignment is finally complete, may I be found faithful. May everything I build, everything I possess, everything I lead, and everything I leave behind ultimately point back to You. **I am not the owner. I am the steward. The resources are Yours. The opportunities are Yours. The people are Yours. The work is Yours. The glory is Yours.** And when everything is returned to the King, may my life testify: **He trusted me, and I remained faithful.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.**
