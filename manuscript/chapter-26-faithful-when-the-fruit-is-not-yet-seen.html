# Chapter 26 ## Faithful When the Fruit Is Not Yet Seen One of the hardest parts of stewardship is continuing to be faithful when we cannot yet see the fruit. We can work. We can pray. We can give. We can serve. We can obey. We can plant. And still, nothing seems to change. The result may be slow. The answer may not come. The harvest may seem far away. In those moments, the steward faces an important question: **Will I remain faithful to the King even when I cannot yet see the outcome?** This is where stewardship moves beyond responsibility and becomes trust. ### The King Does Not Require Us to See Everything God rarely shows us the entire picture at once. He often gives us enough light for the next step, not enough light to see the entire journey. Abraham was called to leave his homeland without knowing every detail of where he was going. Noah built an ark before the flood came. Joseph carried a promise long before he saw its fulfillment. David was anointed king long before he sat on the throne. In each story, there was a period between **obedience and visible fulfillment**. That period can feel uncomfortable. But it is not necessarily wasted. Sometimes God is doing His deepest work while we are waiting for visible results. ### Faithfulness Is Not Measured Only by Results The world often measures success by visible outcomes. How much did you earn? How many people followed? How large did it become? How quickly did it grow? How impressive was the result? But the King looks deeper. He looks at the heart. He looks at obedience. He looks at motives. He looks at faithfulness. A steward can produce impressive results with the wrong heart. Another steward can faithfully obey God in a season where the visible results appear very small. The second steward should not assume that his work is meaningless simply because it looks small. Jesus said: > “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.” — Luke 16:10 The King notices faithfulness in little things. ### The Hidden Season There are seasons when stewardship happens quietly. Nobody applauds. Nobody notices. There may be no public recognition. There may be no immediate financial reward. There may be no obvious evidence that what we are doing matters. But hidden does not mean meaningless. A seed is hidden before it becomes visible. Roots grow underground before branches appear above the soil. The foundation of a building is buried beneath what people eventually see. In the same way, God may be developing something beneath the surface. This is why we must not despise hidden seasons. **The King can be working where our eyes cannot see.** ### Do Not Abandon the Seed Because You Cannot See the Harvest Imagine a farmer planting a field. He places the seed in the ground. Then he covers it with soil. From the surface, it may appear that nothing happened. But the farmer does not dig up the seed every morning to check whether it is growing. He trusts the process. He waters. He waits. He continues caring for the field. Eventually, the evidence appears. Galatians 6:9 gives us this encouragement: > “So let’s not get tired of doing what is good. At just the right time we will reap a harvest of blessing if we don’t give up.” Notice the instruction: **Do not give up.** The harvest has a time. Our responsibility is not to force the harvest. Our responsibility is to remain faithful to the seed God has placed in our hands. ### Stewardship Requires Patience Patience is not passive. Biblical patience is the ability to remain faithful while waiting for God's timing. A patient steward continues working. He continues praying. He continues learning. He continues preparing. He continues serving. He continues doing what is right. He does not confuse waiting with doing nothing. Sometimes the waiting season is actually a preparation season. God may be preparing the steward for what the steward is asking Him to release. We may ask for greater responsibility while God is developing greater character. We may ask for increase while God is teaching us how to manage what we already have. We may ask for influence while God is teaching us humility. We may ask for harvest while God is strengthening our roots. ### What If the Result Is Different From What We Expected? Faithfulness becomes even deeper when the outcome is not what we expected. Sometimes we obey God and the situation still becomes difficult. Sometimes we give and do not immediately receive. Sometimes we serve and are not appreciated. Sometimes we build something and another person benefits from it. Sometimes we prepare something that someone else eventually carries forward. What then? Do we say, “It was all for nothing”? The faithful steward says: **“My obedience was not wasted simply because the outcome was different from what I expected.”** Our responsibility is obedience. God remains sovereign over the outcome. This protects our hearts from bitterness. We do not serve God only when His results match our plans. We serve because **He is worthy of our faithfulness.** ### Moses Did Not Enter the Promised Land Moses spent years leading Israel through the wilderness. He carried an enormous responsibility. Yet he did not personally enter the Promised Land. That does not make his obedience meaningless. His assignment was part of a larger story. Joshua would continue what Moses began. This teaches us something important about stewardship: **We may not always see the completion of what we faithfully begin.** Someone else may build on our work. Someone else may enjoy the fruit. Someone else may carry the vision forward. And that is okay. The King never promised that every steward would personally see the entire harvest. He asks us to be faithful with our assignment. ### Some Seeds Are for the Next Generation A parent may teach a child something that becomes valuable many years later. A mentor may invest in someone who eventually influences thousands. A business owner may establish principles that future leaders continue. A pastor may disciple someone whose ministry begins years afterward. A believer may pray for something that is answered beyond his own lifetime. We should therefore be careful about measuring our stewardship only by what happens during our own lifetime. Some seeds are planted for people we may never meet. This is why faithfulness requires vision beyond ourselves. ### The King Sees What Others Do Not See There are moments when we feel invisible. Perhaps nobody thanks us. Perhaps nobody recognizes the sacrifice. Perhaps nobody understands why we continue. But God sees. Jesus taught that the Father sees what is done in secret. That truth should give the steward peace. We do not need everyone's applause when we have the King's approval. We do not need every person to understand our obedience. We do not need to explain every sacrifice. We simply need to remain faithful. The King sees the hidden work. He sees the unseen sacrifice. He sees the private prayer. He sees the quiet generosity. He sees the decision to remain honest. He sees the moment when we could have compromised but chose integrity instead. Nothing faithful is invisible to Him. ### Do Not Let Delay Change Your Character One danger of delayed results is that disappointment can slowly change the heart. A generous person can become resentful. A faithful worker can become careless. A servant can become bitter. A patient person can become impatient. A person who once trusted God can begin trying to control everything. This is why waiting seasons require spiritual vigilance. Do not allow delayed results to turn you into someone you were never meant to become. Keep your heart clean. Keep your motives surrendered. Keep doing what is right. The delay may test the steward, but it does not have to destroy the steward. ### Faithfulness Before Increase There is a principle we must never forget: **God may increase our responsibility only after He has developed our capacity to carry it.** We often pray: “Lord, give me more.” But perhaps God is asking: “Can you faithfully manage what you already have?” Before greater resources, there may be greater discipline. Before greater influence, there may be greater humility. Before greater responsibility, there may be greater character. Before a larger platform, there may be a smaller room where nobody is watching. The small assignment is not an interruption to the big assignment. **The small assignment may be preparation for it.** ### The Steward Does Not Need to Control the Timeline One of the hardest things for a steward is surrendering the timeline. We may know what we are praying for. We may know what we are building. We may know what we hope will happen. But we do not control when everything will come together. Proverbs 3:5–6 reminds us: > “Trust in the LORD with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take.” Trust means we can obey without demanding that God explain everything first. We can move forward without knowing every detail. We can remain faithful without knowing exactly when the harvest will come. ### What If Nobody Ever Sees? This question reveals the heart. What if nobody ever sees the sacrifice? Would we still obey? What if nobody praises the work? Would we still serve? What if nobody knows how much it cost? Would we still give? What if the recognition goes to someone else? Would we still remain faithful? The answer reveals whether we are serving the King or serving the applause of people. A faithful steward can work in obscurity because his eyes are not ultimately fixed on people. His eyes are fixed on the King. ### The Harvest Belongs to God Paul wrote: > “I planted the seed, Apollos watered it, but it was God who made it grow.” — 1 Corinthians 3:6 This is a powerful picture of stewardship. Paul planted. Apollos watered. But God gave the growth. This means we should never carry a burden that belongs to God. We plant. We water. We work. We obey. We prepare. But **God gives the increase.** This brings freedom. We can work diligently without pretending that everything depends on us. ### Faithful Until the Evidence Appears The mature steward learns to say: “Even if I cannot see it yet, I will remain faithful.” Not because he knows exactly what will happen. Not because he can guarantee the outcome. But because he knows the character of the King. Faith is not merely confidence in an expected result. Faith is confidence in God. Hebrews 10:23 says: > “Let us hold tightly without wavering to the hope we affirm, for God can be trusted to keep his promise.” The foundation of our faithfulness is not the certainty that our preferred outcome will happen exactly as we imagined. The foundation is that **God is trustworthy.** ### The Question of This Season Perhaps you are in a season where you cannot see much fruit. Perhaps you have been faithful for a long time. Perhaps you are wondering whether the work matters. Perhaps you are tired of waiting. Perhaps you are tempted to stop. Before you abandon the assignment, remember: The seed may be underground. The roots may be growing. The foundation may be strengthening. The character may be developing. The next generation may be watching. And the King may be doing more than you can currently see. So remain faithful. Keep planting. Keep watering. Keep obeying. Keep preparing. Keep trusting. **Do not judge the faithfulness of your stewardship only by what is visible today.** ### A Steward's Declaration I will not measure God's faithfulness by the speed of my results. I will not abandon what God has entrusted to me simply because the harvest is delayed. I will remain faithful in little things. I will honor the King when people see me and when nobody sees me. I will plant even when I cannot yet see the harvest. I will work without demanding immediate recognition. I will trust God's timing. I will release the outcome into His hands. I will remember that my assignment is obedience, not control. I will remain faithful because the King is worthy of my faithfulness. And when the fruit finally appears, I will not take the glory for myself. I will say: **“The King gave the growth.”** ### Prayer **King of Kings,** Teach me to remain faithful when I cannot yet see the fruit. When I become tired, strengthen me. When I become discouraged, remind me that You see what others cannot see. When I am tempted to quit, remind me why I began. Help me to plant faithfully, water faithfully, serve faithfully, and wait faithfully. Keep me from measuring my worth by visible results or human recognition. Teach me to trust Your timing. If You allow me to see the harvest, let me give You the glory. If You ask me to plant a seed whose harvest will be seen by another generation, give me grace to plant it faithfully anyway. I surrender the outcome to You. Everything I have is Yours. Everything I build is Yours. Every opportunity is Yours. Every result belongs to You. I am only the steward. So while I have the assignment, help me remain faithful. And when I cannot see what You are doing, help me trust the One who can. **I will not give up. I will not abandon the seed. I will remain faithful to the King.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.**
