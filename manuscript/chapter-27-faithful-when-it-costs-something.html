# Chapter 27 ## Faithful When It Costs Something Faithfulness is easy to admire when it costs us nothing. It is easy to say we trust God when everything is going according to plan. It is easy to talk about integrity when there is nothing to gain from compromise. It is easy to say, “Everything belongs to the King,” when surrender does not require anything from us. But stewardship becomes much deeper when faithfulness becomes costly. When obedience costs comfort. When integrity costs an opportunity. When generosity costs something we wanted to keep. When truth costs approval. When patience costs convenience. When doing what is right means walking away from what appears profitable. This is where the heart of the steward is revealed. The question is no longer simply: **“Will I be faithful?”** The deeper question becomes: **“Will I still be faithful when faithfulness costs me something?”** ### The King Is Worth More Than the Reward A steward who understands the value of the King will not measure obedience only by what obedience gives him in return. He does not obey God merely because obedience produces benefits. He obeys because the King is worthy. This distinction matters. If we serve God only when serving Him makes our lives easier, then our faithfulness depends on convenience. But if we belong to the King, then our obedience remains anchored even when circumstances become difficult. Jesus said: > “If you love me, obey my commandments.” — John 14:15 Obedience is not merely a transaction. It is an expression of love. ### The Test of the Convenient Choice There will always be moments when the easier choice is available. The dishonest shortcut may look faster. The compromise may look more profitable. The lie may appear easier than the truth. The temptation may offer immediate pleasure. The wrong relationship may offer temporary comfort. The unethical decision may seem necessary to protect our position. In these moments, the steward must remember: **Not every open door comes from the King.** An opportunity is not automatically God's approval. A profitable decision is not automatically a faithful decision. A convenient path is not automatically the right path. The steward must ask: **“Does this honor the King?”** That question can protect us from many choices that look attractive in the moment but carry consequences later. ### Joseph and the Cost of Integrity Joseph provides a powerful example. He was serving in Potiphar's house when he was tempted by Potiphar's wife. Joseph had an opportunity to choose secrecy. He could have thought: “No one will know.” But Joseph understood that even when people do not see, God sees. He said: > “How could I do such a wicked thing? It would be a great sin against God.” — Genesis 39:9 Joseph chose integrity. But his obedience came with a cost. He was falsely accused and eventually imprisoned. From a human perspective, doing the right thing seemed to make his situation worse. Yet Joseph did not become unfaithful simply because obedience brought temporary hardship. His story reminds us of something important: **A faithful decision can sometimes produce a difficult season before it produces a visible blessing.** The difficulty did not mean Joseph had made the wrong choice. ### Daniel and the Cost of Prayer Daniel also understood costly faithfulness. When a decree was issued that prohibited prayer to anyone except the king, Daniel had a choice. He could have hidden his prayer life. He could have changed his routine temporarily. He could have reasoned that protecting his position was more important. Instead, Daniel continued praying. His faithfulness eventually led him into the lions' den. Daniel did not know how the story would end. He simply knew whom he served. That is the heart of stewardship. **We do not need to know the outcome before we choose obedience.** We need to know the King. ### Faithfulness When Nobody Is Watching Some of the most important tests of stewardship happen in private. What do we do with money when nobody checks? What do we do with our time when nobody supervises us? What do we do with information that could benefit us unfairly? What do we do when we can take something without being caught? What do we do when nobody would ever know? These moments reveal whether our integrity depends on human observation or God's presence. A faithful steward remembers: **The King is always present.** Integrity means doing what is right even when there is no audience. ### The Hidden Price of Integrity Integrity can sometimes be expensive. It may cost a business opportunity. It may cost a relationship. It may cost popularity. It may cost a promotion. It may cost immediate profit. It may cost convenience. But there are things money cannot replace. Peace with God. A clear conscience. A trustworthy name. A clean heart. A testimony that does not need to be defended by excuses. Proverbs 10:9 says: > “People with integrity walk safely, but those who follow crooked paths will be exposed.” The faithful steward does not trade long-term integrity for short-term advantage. ### When Generosity Costs You Stewardship is also tested through generosity. It is easy to give from abundance. It is more difficult to give when giving requires sacrifice. Yet Jesus highlighted the offering of a poor widow who gave two small coins. The value of her gift was not measured simply by its amount. Jesus said: > “This poor widow has given more than all the others.” — Mark 12:43 Why? Because her giving involved sacrifice. This does not mean that God expects every person to give beyond their ability or neglect legitimate responsibilities. Rather, it shows us that the King looks at the heart behind the gift. Faithful stewardship asks: **“Am I willing to release what belongs to God, even when holding onto it would be easier?”** ### When Forgiveness Costs You Sometimes stewardship is tested not by money but by relationships. Forgiveness can be costly. Humility can be costly. Apologizing can be costly. Admitting that we were wrong can be costly. Choosing peace instead of winning an argument can be costly. But relationships are also part of what the King entrusts to us. We cannot claim to be faithful stewards of God's resources while treating people carelessly. Jesus taught His followers to forgive. This does not mean ignoring justice, enabling abuse, or pretending that wrongdoing did not happen. It means refusing to allow bitterness to become the master of our hearts. The faithful steward asks: **“How can I respond in a way that honors the King?”** ### When Saying No Is Faithfulness A steward must sometimes say no. No to greed. No to dishonesty. No to temptation. No to unhealthy priorities. No to opportunities that require compromise. No to spending what should be saved. No to using people for personal gain. No to the pressure to become something God never called us to be. Saying no can feel like losing. But sometimes saying no is actually protecting what the King has entrusted to us. A faithful steward understands that not every opportunity deserves a yes. ### The Pressure to Compromise Pressure can make compromise look reasonable. Someone may say: “Everyone does it.” “It is only a small thing.” “Nobody will find out.” “You have to do this to succeed.” “You cannot afford to be too honest.” “If you do not take this opportunity, someone else will.” These statements may sound practical. But the steward does not determine right and wrong by popular opinion. The King determines the standard. Romans 12:2 teaches: > “Don’t copy the behavior and customs of this world.” The steward must learn to recognize the difference between what is common and what is right. Something can be common and still be wrong. Something can be difficult and still be right. ### When Obedience Feels Like Losing There may be moments when faithfulness feels like losing. Someone else may get the opportunity. Someone else may get the promotion. Someone else may make more money through dishonest means. Someone else may receive recognition. Someone else may appear to move ahead faster. The faithful steward must resist the temptation to compare. Our assignment is not to manage another person's stewardship. It is to manage ours. Jesus told Peter not to become distracted by another person's path. The principle remains: **Follow the King. Stay faithful to your assignment.** Comparison can make us resent the cost of obedience. But obedience was never supposed to be measured against another person's reward. ### Faithfulness Does Not Guarantee an Easy Life This is an important truth. Following God does not mean that every faithful decision will produce an easy outcome. Joseph still went to prison. Daniel still entered the lions' den. Paul still suffered for the Gospel. Many faithful servants throughout Scripture experienced hardship. Therefore, we should not interpret every difficulty as evidence that God has abandoned us. Sometimes difficulty comes precisely because we refused to compromise. The presence of hardship does not automatically mean the absence of God's favor. ### The King Sees the Cost When faithfulness becomes costly, remember this: **The King sees the cost.** He sees what you surrendered. He sees the opportunity you walked away from. He sees the temptation you resisted. He sees the truth you chose. He sees the generosity that required sacrifice. He sees the forgiveness that required humility. He sees the obedience nobody applauded. Nothing offered to Him in genuine faithfulness is invisible. Hebrews 6:10 reminds us: > “God is not unjust. He will not forget how hard you have worked for him.” The King remembers. ### Do Not Turn Faithfulness Into a Bargain There is another danger. We can obey God but secretly expect Him to repay every sacrifice according to our preferred timetable. “Lord, I did this, so You must give me that.” That turns stewardship into a transaction. We should certainly trust God's goodness and believe His promises. But our obedience should not become a bargaining system. We do not serve because God owes us. We serve because He is our King. The heart of true stewardship says: **“Even when I do not know what I will receive, I know whom I serve.”** ### Faithfulness Is a Long-Term Decision A single good decision is valuable. But a lifetime of faithful decisions is powerful. Every day gives us another opportunity to choose integrity. Another opportunity to choose generosity. Another opportunity to choose humility. Another opportunity to choose obedience. Character is built through repeated decisions. The steward does not ask only: **“What should I do today?”** He also asks: **“Who am I becoming through the decisions I make today?”** Every compromise shapes us. Every act of integrity shapes us. Every surrendered desire shapes us. Every faithful decision strengthens the kind of person we are becoming. ### When the Cost Is Greater Than Expected Sometimes we begin an assignment without knowing how expensive it will become. We say yes to God with limited understanding. Later, the cost becomes clearer. The responsibility grows. The sacrifice becomes greater. The road becomes harder. At that moment, we may be tempted to say: “I did not know it would cost this much.” But faithfulness is not based on complete knowledge of the future. Jesus taught about counting the cost of following Him. The steward must understand that obedience may require perseverance. We do not promise God that obedience will always be comfortable. We surrender our lives to the King. ### The Greatest Example of Costly Faithfulness Above all, we look to Jesus. He did not choose the path of least resistance. He willingly obeyed the Father even when obedience led to the cross. Philippians 2:8 says that He: > “humbled himself in obedience to God and died a criminal’s death on a cross.” The cross shows us the ultimate picture of costly obedience. Jesus did not abandon His mission when the cost became painful. He remained faithful. Because of Him, we understand that faithfulness is not merely about protecting our comfort. It is about surrendering ourselves to God's will. ### What Will We Choose? Eventually, every steward faces moments of decision. The shortcut or the right path. Compromise or integrity. Self-interest or obedience. Fear or faith. Immediate reward or long-term faithfulness. The choice may not always be dramatic. Sometimes it is a small decision nobody else notices. But those small decisions shape our lives. So when the moment comes, remember who you are. You are not merely an employee. Not merely a business owner. Not merely a leader. Not merely a parent. Not merely a worker. You are a **steward of the King.** And the steward's question is simple: **“What choice honors my King?”** ### A Steward's Declaration I will remain faithful when faithfulness is easy. I will remain faithful when faithfulness is difficult. I will choose integrity when compromise appears profitable. I will choose truth when a lie seems easier. I will choose obedience when disobedience seems more convenient. I will protect what the King has entrusted to me. I will not exchange eternal values for temporary rewards. I will not allow pressure to determine my convictions. I will not measure God's goodness only by my comfort. When obedience costs me something, I will remember that the King is worth more than what I am asked to surrender. I am not my own. I belong to the King. Everything I have belongs to Him. And whatever He asks me to surrender, I trust Him with it. **I will remain faithful—not because faithfulness is always easy, but because my King is worthy.** ### Prayer **King of Kings,** Teach me to remain faithful when obedience becomes costly. When I am tempted to compromise, give me courage to choose what is right. When the shortcut looks attractive, remind me that Your ways are higher than mine. When integrity costs me an opportunity, help me trust You. When generosity requires sacrifice, remind me that everything I have already belongs to You. When relationships require humility and forgiveness, give me a heart that reflects Yours. When pressure surrounds me, help me stand firm. When I feel that faithfulness is making me fall behind, remind me that I do not have to compete with anyone else's assignment. Keep my heart from becoming bitter when obedience is difficult. Keep me from treating my relationship with You as a transaction. I do not want to obey only when it benefits me. I want to obey because You are my King. Give me the courage of Joseph. Give me the conviction of Daniel. Give me the perseverance of the faithful servants who came before me. And above all, help me keep my eyes on Jesus, who remained obedient even when obedience led Him to the cross. May my life say: **“Whatever You entrust to me, I will steward faithfully. Whatever You ask me to surrender, I will release. Whatever You ask me to do, I will obey. Because You are worthy.”** In Jesus' name, **Amen.**
