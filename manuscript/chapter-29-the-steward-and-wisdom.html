# Chapter 29 ## The Steward and Wisdom > **“If you need wisdom, ask our generous God, and he will give it to you. He will not rebuke you for asking.”** > — James 1:5 NLT A faithful steward needs more than a willing heart. He needs wisdom. There are moments when the choice before us is clearly right or wrong. But there are also moments when both choices may appear good. There are opportunities that look promising but are not meant for us. There are doors that open quickly and doors that open slowly. There are moments when we need to speak, and moments when silence is wiser. This is where wisdom becomes essential. The King has entrusted us with many things, but He never intended for us to manage them according to our own understanding alone. We need His wisdom. ## Wisdom Is Part of Faithful Stewardship Stewardship is not simply possessing something and trying our best to use it. It is learning to handle what belongs to the King according to the King's ways. A steward may have money but lack wisdom in managing it. A steward may have influence but lack wisdom in using it. A steward may have talent but lack wisdom in where to invest it. A steward may have opportunities but lack wisdom in knowing which ones to accept. A steward may have authority but lack wisdom in knowing how to exercise it. This is why wisdom matters. The question is not only: **“What do I have?”** The deeper question is: **“How does the King want me to use what He has entrusted to me?”** That question requires wisdom. ## Wisdom Is Greater Than Knowledge Knowledge tells us what something is. Wisdom helps us know what to do with what we know. A person can have knowledge without wisdom. Someone may know how money works but still make foolish financial decisions. Someone may know many Bible verses but make unwise choices. Someone may be highly educated but handle relationships poorly. Someone may have great talent but use it at the wrong time or in the wrong place. Wisdom is not simply knowing more. Wisdom is learning to apply truth correctly. Proverbs says: > **“Getting wisdom is the most important thing you can do. And whatever else you do, get good judgment.”** > — Proverbs 4:7 NLT The steward therefore does not merely pursue information. He pursues wisdom. He wants to understand the King's principles so he can faithfully apply them in real life. ## The King Gives Wisdom One of the greatest encouragements for a steward is that wisdom is something we can ask God for. James does not tell us to pretend that we already know everything. He tells us to ask. > **“If you need wisdom, ask our generous God, and he will give it to you.”** > — James 1:5 NLT What a beautiful invitation. God does not shame us because we need wisdom. He does not rebuke us for admitting that we do not know what to do. He invites us to ask. There will be decisions in life where we simply will not know the answer. There will be situations where experience is not enough. There will be circumstances where we cannot see what is ahead. In those moments, the faithful steward does not depend entirely on himself. He goes to the King. He asks. He listens. He waits. And he obeys. ## “Trust in the Lord” One of the greatest dangers in stewardship is assuming that because something has been entrusted to us, we are now free to manage it however we want. But ownership and stewardship are different. The steward must remember that his understanding is limited. Proverbs gives us this instruction: > **“Trust in the Lord with all your heart; do not depend on your own understanding. Seek his will in all you do, and he will show you which path to take.”** > — Proverbs 3:5–6 NLT This does not mean that we stop thinking. It means we stop treating our own understanding as the highest authority. We think. We plan. We examine. We seek counsel. But above everything else, we seek the will of the King. A steward can say: **“Lord, I have considered what I can see, but You see what I cannot see. Lead me.”** That is wisdom. ## Wisdom Knows When to Wait Not every opportunity must be taken immediately. Sometimes faithfulness means moving forward. Sometimes faithfulness means waiting. This can be difficult because we often assume that an open door must automatically be God's door. But an opportunity is not always an assignment. Something can be available without being appropriate. Something can be profitable without being God's will. Something can look impressive without being what the King has asked us to steward. Wisdom teaches us to ask before we act. **“Lord, is this something You are entrusting to me, or is this something I simply desire?”** That question can protect a steward from many unnecessary burdens. Waiting is not always inactivity. Sometimes waiting is preparation. Sometimes God is developing our character while we wait for the right time. Sometimes the delay is protecting us from something we cannot yet see. The wise steward does not force the timing. He trusts the King. ## Wisdom Counts the Cost Jesus taught about counting the cost before beginning a project. > **“But don’t begin until you count the cost.”** > — Luke 14:28 NLT This principle applies to stewardship. Before committing resources, we should consider the cost. Before making a major financial decision, we should consider the consequences. Before accepting a responsibility, we should consider whether we can faithfully carry it. Before beginning a project, we should consider whether we have the resources, time, ability, and commitment required. Faith does not eliminate wisdom. Faith and wisdom work together. We trust God, but we also count the cost. We pray, but we also plan. We believe, but we also prepare. We surrender the outcome to God while faithfully managing the responsibility He has placed before us. ## Wisdom Seeks Counsel A steward should not be afraid to ask for wise counsel. > **“Plans go wrong for lack of advice; many advisers bring success.”** > — Proverbs 15:22 NLT There is humility in saying: **“I may not see everything. Help me see what I am missing.”** Sometimes another person's perspective can reveal something we could not see ourselves. This is especially important when the decision involves money, leadership, relationships, business, ministry, or major responsibilities. Wise counsel does not replace prayer. And prayer does not make wise counsel unnecessary. God can use people to help us see clearly. The wise steward therefore does not surround himself only with people who agree with him. He values truthful counsel. He welcomes correction. He listens before deciding. ## Wisdom Knows What to Say No To Stewardship is also about what we refuse. Every yes consumes something. A yes to one responsibility may require a no to another. A yes to unnecessary spending may become a no to future generosity. A yes to every opportunity may become a no to the assignment God has already given us. A yes to every request for our time may leave little time for the things that matter most. Wisdom helps us recognize that we cannot steward everything. We have limited time. Limited energy. Limited resources. Limited attention. Therefore, we need wisdom to determine what deserves our yes. Sometimes the most faithful sentence a steward can say is: **“No, because I need to remain faithful to what the King has already entrusted to me.”** ## Wisdom Protects What Has Been Entrusted A wise steward does not only seek increase. He also protects. If the King gives us a resource, we should not carelessly waste it. If He gives us a relationship, we should not neglect it. If He gives us an opportunity, we should prepare for it. If He gives us a position, we should carry it responsibly. If He gives us money, we should manage it carefully. If He gives us influence, we should recognize its weight. Wisdom asks: **“How can I protect this trust?”** Because stewardship includes preservation. Sometimes faithfulness means growth. Sometimes faithfulness means protection. Sometimes faithfulness means saying, **“This is valuable, and I must guard it.”** ## Wisdom in Money Money is one of the clearest places where wisdom is needed. The question is not simply whether we have money. The question is whether money is being managed according to the King's purposes. Wisdom teaches us to distinguish between: **need and want,** **investment and impulse,** **generosity and irresponsibility,** **contentment and greed,** **preparation and fear,** **opportunity and temptation.** A steward should not be controlled by money. Neither should he treat money as something unimportant. Money is a resource. And every resource placed in our hands should be handled faithfully. The wise steward asks: **“King, how do You want this resource to serve Your purposes?”** ## Wisdom in Relationships Stewardship also includes people. People are not possessions. We do not own them. But God can entrust relationships to us. A spouse. A child. A parent. A friend. A brother or sister in Christ. A mentor. A person we are called to serve. Wisdom teaches us how to love people properly. There are times to speak truth. Times to encourage. Times to forgive. Times to establish boundaries. Times to listen. Times to confront gently. Times to remain silent. Wisdom helps us understand that being faithful to God does not mean treating people carelessly. The King cares about how we handle those He places around us. ## Wisdom in Leadership The greater the responsibility, the greater the need for wisdom. This is why leadership should never be separated from humility. When a steward is given authority, he must remember: **Authority is not ownership.** A position does not make someone the owner of the people beneath that position. Leadership is another form of stewardship. The higher the responsibility, the more carefully the steward must ask: **“How would the King want me to lead?”** The wise leader does not use authority merely to be served. He understands that authority creates responsibility. He protects. He develops. He corrects. He listens. He serves. He makes decisions with the good of the assignment in mind. The King is watching not only what the steward accomplishes, but how the steward handles the authority entrusted to him. ## Wisdom Does Not Mean Fear Sometimes people confuse wisdom with excessive caution. Wisdom does not mean refusing every opportunity because something could go wrong. Wisdom is not fear. Wisdom is discernment. There are moments when God calls us to step out. There are moments when faith requires courage. There are moments when the wise decision is actually the bold decision. The difference is that wisdom does not act merely because something feels exciting. It asks whether the action is aligned with God's direction. The wise steward can say: **“I am not moving because I am afraid, and I am not moving because I am excited. I am moving because I have sought the King.”** That is a different kind of confidence. ## Solomon Asked for Wisdom When Solomon became king, he could have asked God for many things. He could have asked for wealth. Long life. Victory over enemies. Greater influence. But he asked for wisdom. He understood the weight of the responsibility placed upon him. He needed to lead God's people. He needed to distinguish between right and wrong. He needed wisdom because the assignment was bigger than his own ability. This is a powerful picture of stewardship. When God gives us greater responsibility, our first response should not be: **“I can handle this.”** It should be: **“King, give me the wisdom to handle this faithfully.”** ## Wisdom Must Become Action Wisdom that is never practiced remains only knowledge. The wise steward must act on what God reveals. If God gives wisdom about money, apply it. If He gives wisdom about relationships, apply it. If He gives wisdom about time, apply it. If He gives wisdom about priorities, apply it. If He gives wisdom about an opportunity, respond accordingly. Wisdom becomes visible through obedience. A person can say all the right things and still make foolish choices. But when wisdom enters the life of a steward, it begins to shape decisions. The calendar changes. The spending changes. The relationships change. The priorities change. The conversations change. The choices change. Wisdom becomes stewardship in motion. ## When You Do Not Know What to Do There will still be moments when you do not know. That does not mean you have failed. It means you are human. When you do not know what to do: **Pray.** **Search God's Word.** **Seek wise counsel.** **Examine your motives.** **Consider the consequences.** **Wait when necessary.** **Move when God makes the direction clear.** And remember: You are not the King. You are the steward. You do not have to know everything. You simply need to remain close to the One who does. That truth brings peace. ## The Wisdom to Remain a Steward Perhaps one of the greatest forms of wisdom is remembering who we are. We are stewards. God is the King. Everything begins with Him. Everything belongs to Him. Everything we have is ultimately received from Him. And everything we manage should eventually point back to Him. So when we gain wisdom, we should not become proud. When we succeed, we should not forget the Giver. When people recognize us, we should remember the King. When resources increase, we should remain faithful. When responsibilities become greater, we should become more dependent on God, not less. The wiser we become, the more clearly we should see our need for the King. ## A Steward's Prayer for Wisdom King of Kings, You have entrusted things to me that I could never have created or controlled by myself. You have given me time, resources, relationships, opportunities, abilities, and responsibilities. Teach me how to steward them wisely. When I do not know what to do, give me wisdom. When I am tempted to rush, teach me patience. When I am tempted to delay through fear, give me courage. When I am tempted to follow my own understanding, remind me to seek Your will. Give me wisdom in my decisions. Wisdom in my finances. Wisdom in my relationships. Wisdom in my work. Wisdom in my leadership. Wisdom in my opportunities. And wisdom to recognize what I must release. Do not allow knowledge to make me proud. Make wisdom make me humble. Help me remember that I am not the owner. I am Your steward. Everything in my hands belongs to You. Therefore, teach me to manage everything according to Your heart and Your ways. May my decisions honor You. May my choices reflect Your wisdom. May my life prove that what You entrust to me is safe in the hands of a faithful steward. In Jesus' name, **Amen.** ## Declaration of a Wise Steward **I belong to the King, and everything entrusted to me belongs to Him.** **I will not depend solely on my own understanding.** **I will seek the wisdom of God before making important decisions.** **I will listen to His Word, receive wise counsel, and remain teachable.** **I will not confuse every opportunity with an assignment.** **I will learn to recognize God's timing.** **I will count the cost, manage resources carefully, and protect what has been entrusted to me.** **I will use wisdom in my money, relationships, time, work, influence, and responsibilities.** **I will not allow fear to control me, and I will not allow excitement to control me.** **I will seek the King.** **I will listen to the King.** **I will obey the King.** **And as He gives me greater responsibility, I will ask for greater wisdom to steward it faithfully.** **I am a steward.** **He is the King.** **And I will faithfully manage what He has placed in my hands.**
