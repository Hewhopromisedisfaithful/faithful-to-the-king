# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 3 — What the King Entrusts

A steward cannot be faithful with something he does not recognize as entrusted.

Before we can become better stewards, we must learn to see what God has placed in our hands.

When people hear the word **stewardship**, they often think only about money.

Money is certainly part of stewardship.

But biblical stewardship is much bigger.

God can entrust us with time.

He can entrust us with abilities.

He can entrust us with opportunities.

He can entrust us with relationships.

He can entrust us with influence.

He can entrust us with leadership.

He can entrust us with resources.

And He can entrust us with spiritual gifts and responsibilities in His Kingdom.

The question is not simply:

**“What do I own?”**

The better question is:

**“What has the King placed in my hands?”**

---

## 1. Money and Resources

Money is one of the most obvious areas of stewardship.

Yet money is also one of the easiest things to misunderstand.

We work for it.

We receive it.

We save it.

We spend it.

We invest it.

Because we worked for it, we can easily begin to think:

**“This is mine.”**

But the Kingdom perspective is different.

God teaches us to remember that He is the source of our ability to produce wealth.

> “Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”
>
> — Deuteronomy 8:18

Money therefore carries responsibility.

How we earn it matters.

How we use it matters.

How we manage it matters.

How we give matters.

How we respond when we have little matters.

And how we respond when we have much matters.

Jesus also taught:

> “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”
>
> — Luke 16:10

The amount may change.

The principle does not.

**Money is not merely something to possess. It is something to steward.**

---

## 2. Time

One of the greatest things God entrusts to every person is time.

Unlike money, we cannot store tomorrow's time in a bank.

Once a moment passes, it cannot be recovered.

That is why Scripture teaches us to use our time wisely.

> “Make the most of every opportunity in these evil days.”
>
> — Ephesians 5:16

Psalm 90:12 says:

> “Teach us to realize the brevity of life, so that we may grow in wisdom.”

Every day is an opportunity entrusted by God.

We may not know how many days we have, but we know that every day we receive is valuable.

This means stewardship includes our schedule.

What receives our attention?

What receives our energy?

What consumes our hours?

Are we spending our time only on things that benefit ourselves?

Or are we also using our time to honor God, serve others, grow, work, rest, and fulfill our responsibilities?

A steward understands that **time is a trust.**

---

## 3. Talents and Abilities

God also entrusts abilities.

Some people can teach.

Some can lead.

Some can create.

Some can organize.

Some can communicate.

Some can build.

Some can encourage.

Some can serve quietly behind the scenes.

Not everyone receives the same abilities.

But different does not mean less valuable.

Peter wrote:

> “God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts. Use them well to serve one another.”
>
> — 1 Peter 4:10

Notice the purpose of the gift.

**Use them well.**

The gift is not merely for personal recognition.

It is meant to serve.

This means developing an ability can also be an act of stewardship.

If God has given you an ability, neglecting it is not faithfulness.

We should learn.

We should practice.

We should improve.

We should use what God has placed in us.

The question is not:

**“Do I have the talent someone else has?”**

The question is:

**“Am I faithfully using what God has given me?”**

---

## 4. Opportunities

Sometimes God entrusts us with something that cannot be held in our hands.

An opportunity.

A door.

A moment.

A chance to serve.

A chance to help someone.

A chance to learn.

A chance to lead.

A chance to speak.

A chance to begin something new.

Paul encouraged believers to make the most of their opportunities.

> “Make the most of every opportunity.”
>
> — Colossians 4:5

An opportunity is temporary.

A door can open and later close.

A moment to encourage someone may come only once.

A chance to serve may not always return.

This is why a steward must learn to recognize opportunities.

Not every opportunity comes from God simply because it is available.

But when God places a genuine opportunity before us, faithfulness requires wisdom to recognize it and courage to act.

Sometimes stewardship means saying:

**“Lord, I will use this opportunity for Your purpose.”**

---

## 5. Relationships

People are also part of stewardship.

God places people in our lives for seasons, responsibilities, encouragement, service, and growth.

Parents are entrusted with children.

Spouses are entrusted with one another.

Leaders are entrusted with people.

Friends are entrusted with friendships.

Church members are entrusted with opportunities to care for one another.

This means relationships should never be treated casually.

Paul taught:

> “Be kind to each other, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, just as God through Christ has forgiven you.”
>
> — Ephesians 4:32

If God has placed people in our lives, we have a responsibility to treat them with love, honesty, patience, and respect.

A relationship is not something to exploit.

It is something to steward.

---

## 6. Influence

Influence is another form of stewardship.

You do not need to be famous to have influence.

A parent influences a child.

A teacher influences students.

A leader influences a team.

A friend influences a friend.

A church member influences younger believers.

Even our daily behavior can influence people watching us.

Jesus said:

> “You are the light of the world—like a city on a hilltop that cannot be hidden.”
>
> — Matthew 5:14

Light exists to shine.

Influence exists to affect.

The question is therefore not whether we have influence.

The question is:

**What are we doing with the influence we have?**

Our words can encourage or discourage.

Our example can strengthen or weaken someone.

Our decisions can affect people around us.

Our influence is therefore a responsibility before the King.

---

## 7. Authority and Leadership

Leadership is one of the greatest forms of stewardship because leadership affects other people.

The world often views leadership as power over others.

The Kingdom views leadership differently.

Jesus taught:

> “Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”
>
> — Matthew 20:26

Kingdom leadership is therefore not primarily about position.

It is about service.

A leader is entrusted with authority so that others may be helped, protected, guided, and developed.

The greater the authority, the greater the responsibility.

This is why leadership should never produce pride.

It should produce humility.

A position is not a personal possession.

It is an assignment.

A title is not an ownership certificate.

It is a responsibility.

---

## 8. Spiritual Gifts and Ministry

God also entrusts spiritual gifts and ministry responsibilities to His people.

Paul explained that different believers receive different gifts and functions within the body of Christ.

> “A spiritual gift is given to each of us so we can help each other.”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 12:7

The purpose is clear:

**so we can help each other.**

A spiritual gift is therefore not a trophy.

It is a tool for service.

A ministry position is not a platform for personal importance.

It is an opportunity to serve the King and His people.

When God entrusts ministry to someone, the proper response is not:

**“Look what I have achieved.”**

It is:

**“Lord, help me faithfully serve with what You have entrusted.”**

---

# One King, Many Trusts

When we put all of these together, we begin to see how comprehensive stewardship really is.

The King may entrust:

**Money.**

**Time.**

**Talents.**

**Opportunities.**

**Relationships.**

**Influence.**

**Authority.**

**Spiritual gifts.**

These things may look different from one person to another.

But they all have one thing in common:

**They are entrusted.**

The five-talent servant received something different from the two-talent servant.

But both were accountable.

The widow had very little money.

But what she had still mattered.

A person with great influence carries a different responsibility from someone with little influence.

A leader carries responsibilities that may not belong to someone who is not leading.

God does not ask every person to carry the same assignment.

He asks each steward to be faithful with what has been entrusted.

---

# The Question Every Steward Must Ask

At the end of the day, stewardship is not about counting everything we have.

It is about recognizing everything we have been entrusted with.

So perhaps the question we should ask is not:

**“What do I have?”**

but:

**“What has the King entrusted to me?”**

What has He placed in your hands?

What has He placed in your heart?

What abilities has He given you?

What opportunities are before you?

Who has He placed around you?

What resources are under your care?

What influence do you carry?

What responsibility has He given you?

These are not merely possessions.

They are trusts.

And every trust carries responsibility.

---

## Bible Foundation

The central teaching of this chapter is supported by these passages:

- **Deuteronomy 8:18** — God gives the ability to produce wealth.
- **Luke 16:10** — Faithfulness begins with little things.
- **Ephesians 5:15–16** — We are called to use our time and opportunities wisely.
- **1 Peter 4:10** — God's gifts are to be used to serve others.
- **Ephesians 4:32** — Relationships should be handled with kindness, forgiveness, and love.
- **Matthew 5:14–16** — Our influence should shine before others.
- **Matthew 20:25–28** — Kingdom leadership is expressed through serving.
- **1 Corinthians 12:7** — Spiritual gifts are given for the benefit of others.
- **Matthew 25:14–30** — Different amounts can be entrusted, but every steward is responsible for what he receives.

---

## Kingdom Principle

**The King may entrust different things to different people, but every trust carries responsibility.**

Do not compare what you have been given with what someone else has received.

Ask instead:

**“Am I being faithful with what the King has placed in my hands?”**

---

## Heart Check

What are the things God has entrusted to me right now?

Have I recognized them as trusts from the King, or have I treated them only as personal possessions?

Which area of stewardship do I need to become more faithful in?

## Faithful Response

Lord, open my eyes to recognize everything You have entrusted to me.

Teach me to steward my money, time, abilities, opportunities, relationships, influence, authority, and spiritual gifts with wisdom and faithfulness.

Help me not to compare my assignment with someone else's.

Give me grace to faithfully use what You have placed in my hands.

**Whatever You entrust, I will steward.

Whatever You give, I will honor.

Whatever responsibility You place before me, help me remain faithful to the King.**
