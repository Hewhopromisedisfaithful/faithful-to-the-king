# Chapter 30 ## The Steward and Obedience > **“So why do you keep calling me ‘Lord, Lord!’ when you don’t do what I say?”** > — Luke 6:46 NLT A steward can have a good heart. A steward can seek wisdom. A steward can understand what the King desires. But there comes a moment when understanding must become action. That moment is called **obedience**. Wisdom tells us what is right. Obedience chooses to do it. And this is where stewardship becomes visible. The King does not entrust His resources, responsibilities, opportunities, and assignments to us merely so that we can understand His principles. He entrusts them so that we can faithfully carry them out. ## Knowing Is Not the Same as Obeying It is possible to know what God says and still refuse to do it. We can hear Scripture without practicing it. We can recognize the right decision without making it. We can know what forgiveness requires and still hold on to resentment. We can know what generosity requires and still remain controlled by possessions. We can know that honesty is right and still choose deception when telling the truth becomes costly. Knowledge has value. But knowledge alone does not produce faithfulness. James gives us a direct warning: > **“Remember, it is sin to know what you ought to do and then not do it.”** > — James 4:17 NLT The steward must therefore ask more than: **“Do I know what God wants?”** He must ask: **“Am I willing to do what God wants?”** That is the difference between knowledge and obedience. ## Obedience Is the Response of a Steward A steward does not create the King's instructions. He receives them. The King establishes the standard. The steward responds. This is the foundation of stewardship. If God is truly King, then His Word is not merely advice. It is direction. It is instruction. It is the standard by which the steward learns how to manage what belongs to Him. Obedience therefore begins with recognizing authority. We cannot faithfully serve the King while constantly negotiating whether His commands are convenient for us. There will be commands we understand immediately. There will also be commands whose purpose we do not fully understand. But the steward's responsibility is not to understand everything before obeying. Sometimes the order is clear even when the reason is not. ## Abraham Obeyed Without Seeing Everything Abraham gives us a powerful picture of obedience. God called him to leave his country, his relatives, and his father's household and go to a land God would show him. > **“So Abram departed as the Lord had instructed.”** > — Genesis 12:4 NLT Notice the simplicity of the response. God instructed. Abraham departed. He did not have the entire map. He did not know every detail of the journey. He did not receive every answer before taking the first step. He trusted the One who gave the instruction. That is often how stewardship works. God may show us the next step without showing us the entire journey. The steward must learn to say: **“I may not see everything, but I trust the One who does.”** ## Noah Obeyed Before the Rain Noah provides another powerful example. God instructed him to build an ark. The assignment required time, effort, resources, and perseverance. More importantly, Noah had to obey before he could see the result. > **“So Noah did everything exactly as God had commanded him.”** > — Genesis 6:22 NLT Imagine the faithfulness required to continue working on an assignment when the evidence of its necessity was not yet visible. Noah built before the rain came. That is obedience. Sometimes the steward must prepare before the provision becomes visible. Sometimes he must build before anyone understands. Sometimes he must remain faithful before the fruit appears. Obedience does not always begin with visible evidence. Sometimes obedience is the evidence of faith. ## Partial Obedience Is Still Disobedience One of the most important lessons in stewardship is that obedience cannot be measured only by how much we did. It must also be measured by whether we followed the instruction faithfully. King Saul learned this painfully. God gave Saul a clear command. Saul obeyed partially but kept what God had instructed him to destroy. When Samuel confronted him, Saul defended himself by saying that the people had spared the best animals to sacrifice to the Lord. Samuel responded: > **“Obedience is better than sacrifice.”** > — 1 Samuel 15:22 NLT This is a serious lesson. We can sometimes perform religious activities while avoiding actual obedience. We can give offerings while refusing to surrender something God has told us to release. We can serve publicly while ignoring private instructions. We can speak spiritual language while resisting God's correction. The King is not impressed by activity that replaces obedience. He wants a faithful steward. ## Delayed Obedience There is also another danger: **delayed obedience.** Sometimes we know what we should do, but we keep postponing it. “I will do it later.” “I am not ready yet.” “I will obey when circumstances improve.” “I will obey when it becomes easier.” But every delay creates another opportunity for disobedience. There are times when waiting is wisdom. But there are also times when waiting is simply resistance. The wise steward learns to distinguish the two. If the King has clearly instructed us to act, we should not create excuses to avoid obedience. When God makes the direction clear, the faithful response is: **Yes, Lord.** ## Obedience When It Costs Something True obedience is revealed when obedience becomes costly. It is easy to obey when the command benefits us. It is easy to obey when everyone agrees. It is easy to obey when the sacrifice is small. But what happens when obedience costs comfort? What happens when obedience costs money? What happens when obedience costs an opportunity? What happens when obedience requires us to forgive? What happens when obedience requires us to say no? What happens when obedience makes us unpopular? That is when the steward discovers whether the King truly has first place. Jesus said: > **“If you love me, obey my commandments.”** > — John 14:15 NLT Obedience is not merely a rule. It is a response of love. ## Obedience and Trust At the heart of obedience is trust. Why would a steward obey when he cannot see everything? Because he trusts the King. Why would Abraham leave? Because he trusted God. Why would Noah build? Because he trusted God's word. Why would a steward surrender something valuable? Because he trusts the One who owns everything. Sometimes disobedience happens because we believe our way will protect us better than God's way. We think: **“If I obey, what will happen to me?”** But faith asks a different question: **“If God is my King, can I trust Him with the consequences of obedience?”** The answer is yes. We may not know every consequence. We may not control every outcome. But we can trust the character of the King. ## Obedience Does Not Require Complete Understanding There are commands from God that we understand immediately. There are others whose wisdom becomes clear only later. Sometimes we obey first and understand afterward. That is not blind faith. It is relational trust. A child may not understand every decision a loving parent makes. Likewise, a steward may not understand every direction the King gives. But the steward knows the character of the One giving the instruction. This is why the relationship with God matters. The more we know the King, the more willing we become to trust His ways. Isaiah reminds us: > **“My thoughts are nothing like your thoughts,” says the Lord. “And my ways are far beyond anything you could imagine.”** > — Isaiah 55:8 NLT The steward does not need to become equal to the King in understanding. He needs to trust the King. ## Obedience in Small Things Faithfulness is often revealed in ordinary things. How we handle a small amount of money. How we speak when nobody important is listening. Whether we keep a promise. Whether we return something that does not belong to us. Whether we arrive when we said we would. Whether we tell the truth when lying would be easier. Whether we treat someone with respect when they cannot benefit us. These things may appear small. But small decisions train the heart for greater responsibilities. Jesus said: > **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”** > — Luke 16:10 NLT The King does not need to wait for a large assignment to see our faithfulness. He can see it in the small things already in our hands. ## Obedience Protects the Assignment When the King gives an assignment, obedience protects that assignment from our own preferences. Every steward is tempted to change the instructions. We may think: **“I have a better way.”** Sometimes our idea may even appear more efficient. But stewardship is not about improving the King's instructions according to our preferences. It is about faithfully carrying them out. There may be room for creativity. There may be room for wisdom. There may be room for adaptation. But the foundation remains obedience. A steward must never become so confident in his own ability that he forgets the authority of the One who gave the assignment. ## Jesus: The Perfect Example of Obedience The greatest example of obedience is Jesus Christ. He did not merely teach obedience. He lived it. Even when obedience meant suffering, He remained faithful to the Father's will. Paul writes: > **“He humbled himself in obedience to God and died a criminal’s death on a cross.”** > — Philippians 2:8 NLT The cross shows us the depth of obedience. Jesus did not choose the easiest path. He chose the Father's will. His obedience was not convenient. It was costly. It was sacrificial. And it accomplished what God had purposed. When we struggle with obedience, we should look to Christ. He reminds us that faithfulness is sometimes costly, but the will of the Father is worthy of our obedience. ## Obedience and Stewardship Everything we have discussed in this book comes together here. If God owns everything, then obedience matters. If we are stewards, then obedience matters. If our resources belong to Him, then obedience matters. If our time belongs to Him, then obedience matters. If our talents belong to Him, then obedience matters. If our relationships are entrusted to us, then obedience matters. If our opportunities come from Him, then obedience matters. The question is not simply: **“What has God given me?”** It is also: **“What has God instructed me to do with what He has given me?”** That question transforms stewardship. We stop asking only what we want to do with our lives. We begin asking what the King wants done with what He has entrusted to us. ## The Cost of Saying Yes Every genuine yes to God may require us to say no to something else. Yes to generosity may mean no to unnecessary spending. Yes to integrity may mean no to dishonest gain. Yes to forgiveness may mean no to revenge. Yes to God's timing may mean no to impatience. Yes to God's assignment may mean no to distractions. Yes to humility may mean no to pride. Yes to obedience may mean no to comfort. But every faithful no protects a greater yes. The steward learns that obedience is not about losing everything. It is about refusing to allow lesser things to control what belongs to the King. ## Obedience Produces Freedom At first, obedience may feel restrictive. But God's ways often protect us from the consequences of our own foolishness. His boundaries are not designed to destroy our freedom. They are designed to protect what He has entrusted to us. A steward who follows the King's instructions does not need to carry the burden of constantly defending his own choices. He can say: **“I followed the King.”** There is peace in that. We cannot control every outcome. But we can control our response to God's instruction. We can choose faithfulness. We can choose obedience. And when we have done what God asked us to do, we can leave the results in His hands. ## When Obedience Is Difficult There will be moments when obedience feels difficult. When that happens, do not pretend that it is easy. Bring the struggle to God. Pray honestly. Ask for strength. Ask for wisdom. Ask for courage. Remember His faithfulness. Remember His Word. Remember that the King is not asking you to carry the assignment without His help. He is with you. Sometimes the prayer of a faithful steward is simply: **“Lord, I want to obey You, but I need Your strength to do it.”** That prayer is not weakness. It is dependence. And dependence on the King is one of the marks of a faithful steward. ## The Steward's Final Question At the end of the day, the faithful steward should not ask only: **“Did I accomplish many things?”** He should ask: **“Did I obey the King?”** Because a person can accomplish much and still miss the assignment. A person can become successful and still be unfaithful. A person can become influential and still misuse what was entrusted. But the faithful steward understands that success in the kingdom is measured differently. The goal is not simply to accomplish more. The goal is to remain faithful. And faithfulness requires obedience. ## A Steward's Prayer of Obedience King of Kings, Thank You for everything You have entrusted to me. Forgive me for the times when I knew what was right but chose my own way. Forgive me for delayed obedience. Forgive me for partial obedience. Forgive me when I allowed comfort, fear, pride, or personal desire to become more important than Your instruction. Teach me to obey You completely. Give me courage when obedience is difficult. Give me patience when You tell me to wait. Give me wisdom when I do not understand. Give me strength when obedience becomes costly. Help me remember that You are the King and I am the steward. I do not need to understand everything before I trust You. I do not need to see everything before I take the next faithful step. I only need to know that You are faithful. May my life become a living response to Your Word. May my stewardship reflect my obedience. May everything You have placed in my hands be handled according to Your will. And when You ask me to move, let my answer be: **“Yes, Lord.”** In Jesus' name, **Amen.** ## Declaration of an Obedient Steward **I belong to the King.** **Everything I have belongs to Him.** **I will not merely hear God's Word; I will obey it.** **I will not substitute activity for obedience.** **I will not choose partial obedience when God has given clear instruction.** **I will not allow fear, comfort, pride, or convenience to rule my decisions.** **When God says move, I will move.** **When God says wait, I will wait.** **When God says release, I will release.** **When God says remain faithful, I will remain faithful.** **I will trust the King even when I cannot see the whole path.** **I will obey Him in small things and in great things.** **I will remember that obedience is not about proving myself worthy.** **It is my response to the One who has always been faithful to me.** **I am a steward.** **He is my King.** **His Word is my instruction.** **His will is my direction.** **And my answer will be:** **Yes, Lord.**
