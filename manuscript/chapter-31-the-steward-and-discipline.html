# Chapter 31 ## The Steward and Discipline > **“For physical training is of some value, but godliness has value for all things, holding promise for both the present life and the life to come.”** > — 1 Timothy 4:8 NIV A steward may have a sincere heart. He may seek wisdom. He may understand what the King requires. He may even decide to obey. But faithfulness cannot be sustained by decisions alone. There must be discipline. Discipline is what helps a steward continue doing what is right when the excitement has faded, when circumstances become difficult, and when nobody is watching. A faithful steward does not live only by inspiration. He learns to live by conviction. ## Discipline Turns Intention Into Practice Many people have good intentions. They intend to pray more. They intend to manage money better. They intend to become more responsible. They intend to use their time wisely. They intend to become more generous. They intend to take better care of what God has entrusted to them. But intention alone does not produce transformation. There must be action. And action must become consistent. This is where discipline enters. Discipline takes the statement: **“I should.”** and turns it into: **“I will.”** It takes a desire and gives it structure. It takes a decision and gives it consistency. It takes obedience and helps it become a lifestyle. ## Discipline Is Not Punishment Sometimes the word *discipline* sounds negative. People may associate it with punishment, restriction, or deprivation. But biblical discipline is much more than punishment. Discipline is training. It is learning to bring our desires, habits, time, resources, and actions under proper direction. An athlete disciplines the body because the goal is greater than temporary comfort. A student disciplines his time because the goal is greater than entertainment. A steward disciplines his life because what has been entrusted to him is more valuable than temporary convenience. Discipline says: **“I will not allow what I feel in this moment to determine everything I do.”** ## The Steward Cannot Live by Feelings Alone Feelings are real, but feelings are not always reliable guides. There will be days when we feel motivated. There will also be days when we do not. There will be mornings when prayer feels easy. There will be mornings when getting out of bed feels easier than seeking God. There will be days when generosity feels joyful. There will be moments when holding on feels safer. There will be times when work is exciting. There will be times when responsibility feels exhausting. If faithfulness depended entirely on how we feel, our stewardship would constantly change. Discipline helps us remain faithful even when our emotions fluctuate. We can say: **“I may not feel like doing this today, but I know that this is part of my responsibility before the King.”** That is maturity. ## Discipline Protects the Steward's Priorities Every steward has limited resources. We have only twenty-four hours in a day. We cannot recover yesterday's time. We cannot personally pursue every opportunity. We cannot give our attention to everything. Therefore, discipline is necessary because it protects our priorities. Without discipline, whatever is loudest may become most important. Whatever is most entertaining may consume our time. Whatever is most urgent may replace what is most valuable. Whatever feels easiest may become our default choice. A disciplined steward learns to ask: **“What has the King entrusted to me, and what deserves my attention today?”** That question brings order. ## Discipline in Time Time is one of the greatest trusts God gives us. Money can sometimes be earned again. Material possessions can sometimes be replaced. But yesterday cannot be recovered. That is why a steward must learn to respect time. This does not mean living under constant pressure. It means refusing to waste the gift of time carelessly. A disciplined steward learns to plan. He creates priorities. He makes room for God. He makes room for important relationships. He makes room for work. He makes room for rest. He makes room for service. He understands that rest is not necessarily laziness, and busyness is not necessarily faithfulness. The goal is not to fill every minute. The goal is to steward every season wisely. ## Discipline in Money Financial stewardship also requires discipline. It is easy to spend when we have money. It is easy to desire something simply because it is available. It is easy to confuse what we want with what we need. Discipline gives us the ability to pause. Before spending, we can ask: **“Do I need this?”** **“Can I afford this?”** **“Is this wise?”** **“Does this support my responsibilities?”** **“Am I being controlled by desire?”** The disciplined steward does not need to prove that he can buy something. He understands that having the ability to purchase something does not automatically make the purchase wise. Money is a tool. Discipline keeps the tool from becoming the master. ## Discipline in Giving Generosity also benefits from discipline. If we only give when we feel emotionally moved, our generosity may become inconsistent. But when generosity becomes part of our stewardship, we learn to give intentionally. We prepare. We plan. We remain sensitive to God's leading. We become willing to help when an opportunity arises. Discipline does not make generosity less genuine. It can make generosity more dependable. A faithful steward does not simply say: **“I want to be generous.”** He develops a life that actually allows generosity to happen. ## Discipline in Work Work is another area where discipline matters. A steward should not wait for motivation before fulfilling responsibility. There will always be tasks we enjoy and tasks we do not. There will always be days when productivity feels natural and days when it requires effort. Proverbs repeatedly praises diligence. > **“Lazy people do not plow in season; they harvest when it is too late.”** > — Proverbs 20:4 NLT The disciplined steward understands seasons. There is a time to prepare. A time to work. A time to persevere. A time to harvest. If we refuse to work during preparation, we should not be surprised when there is little to harvest later. ## Discipline in Spiritual Life Perhaps the most important discipline is our relationship with God. Prayer. Reading Scripture. Worship. Fellowship. Reflection. Serving. These things should not become religious performance. They are ways we remain connected to the King. Jesus Himself often withdrew to pray. > **“But Jesus often withdrew to the wilderness for prayer.”** > — Luke 5:16 NLT If Jesus intentionally made room for prayer, how much more should we recognize our need for time with God? The disciplined steward does not wait until there is a crisis before seeking God. He develops a relationship with the King before the crisis comes. ## Discipline Builds Strength Before the Test One reason discipline matters is that we often discover our strength only when pressure arrives. But strength is usually developed before the test. Daniel did not suddenly become faithful when he was thrown into the lions' den. His faithfulness had already been developed through his daily walk with God. When the pressure came, his established discipline helped him remain faithful. That is why daily habits matter. Small choices prepare us for larger moments. Private faithfulness prepares us for public responsibility. Daily obedience prepares us for extraordinary tests. ## Discipline in the Hidden Place Some of the most important stewardship decisions happen where nobody sees us. What do we do with our time when no one is watching? What do we do with money when nobody asks? What do we watch? What do we say? What do we think? What do we do when there is no applause? The hidden place reveals whether discipline has become part of our character. Jesus taught about doing things for God rather than for human recognition. The disciplined steward does not need an audience to remain faithful. He remembers: **The King sees.** That is enough. ## Discipline Requires Saying No Every disciplined life contains no's. No to unnecessary distractions. No to wasteful spending. No to unhealthy habits. No to procrastination. No to pride. No to temptation. No to opportunities that would pull us away from our assignment. A no is not always rejection. Sometimes a no is protection. When we say no to something lesser, we create room for something greater. The steward must therefore become comfortable with boundaries. Not everything deserves our time. Not everything deserves our money. Not everything deserves our attention. Not every invitation deserves a yes. Wisdom helps us recognize what matters. Discipline helps us protect it. ## Discipline Is Consistency, Not Perfection A disciplined steward is not a perfect steward. There will be failures. There will be missed days. There will be mistakes. There will be moments when we become distracted or discouraged. The answer is not to give up. It is to return. If you fail today, begin again tomorrow. If you wasted time yesterday, steward today's time well. If you made a poor financial decision, learn from it and make a wiser decision next time. If you missed an opportunity, do not spend your entire future grieving over the past. Discipline does not demand perfection. It demands a willingness to return to the right path. ## The Danger of Starting and Stopping One of the greatest enemies of stewardship is inconsistency. Start. Stop. Start again. Stop again. A person can become exhausted not because the assignment is impossible, but because he constantly abandons his own commitments. Discipline creates stability. It teaches us to continue. Paul wrote: > **“So I run with purpose in every step.”** > — 1 Corinthians 9:26 NLT There is purpose in disciplined living. We are not simply moving. We are moving in a direction. ## Discipline and Delayed Results Discipline becomes especially important when results are delayed. It is easy to remain consistent when the harvest is immediate. It is harder when we cannot yet see the fruit. But Scripture encourages us: > **“So let’s not get tired of doing what is good. At just the right time we will reap a harvest of blessing if we don’t give up.”** > — Galatians 6:9 NLT The disciplined steward keeps planting. Keeps working. Keeps praying. Keeps preparing. Keeps obeying. Not because every day produces visible results, but because the King is faithful. Faithfulness does not become unnecessary simply because the harvest is delayed. ## Discipline and Freedom Some people think discipline removes freedom. In reality, healthy discipline can create freedom. A person who disciplines his spending has greater financial freedom. A person who disciplines his time has greater freedom to focus on what matters. A person who disciplines his body may have greater capacity to serve. A person who disciplines his words may avoid unnecessary conflict. A person who disciplines his spiritual life becomes more prepared to recognize God's direction. Discipline is not the enemy of freedom. Uncontrolled desires can become the enemy of freedom. A steward is free when his desires are no longer controlling him. ## The Steward Must Train Paul used the language of training when describing the Christian life. > **“All athletes are disciplined in their training.”** > — 1 Corinthians 9:25 NLT An athlete does not train only on the day of competition. Training happens beforehand. Repeatedly. Consistently. Sometimes when nobody is watching. The same is true for stewardship. We prepare before responsibility increases. We develop discipline before opportunity arrives. We establish good habits before pressure comes. We strengthen our spiritual life before the storm. The time to build discipline is not when the crisis arrives. It is now. ## Discipline When Nobody Applauds There may be seasons when your faithfulness receives no recognition. You may work hard and receive little praise. You may manage resources wisely and nobody notices. You may choose integrity and lose an opportunity. You may continue serving while others receive attention. Do not confuse recognition with reward. The King sees what people do not see. Faithfulness does not become meaningless because people fail to notice it. The steward's ultimate audience is the King. One day, what matters will not be how loudly people applauded. What matters will be whether we were faithful with what was entrusted to us. ## Discipline Produces Readiness A disciplined steward is ready. Ready for opportunity. Ready for responsibility. Ready for difficulty. Ready for increase. Ready for correction. Ready for change. When opportunity comes, he does not have to begin preparing from zero. He has already been preparing. When greater responsibility arrives, he is not completely overwhelmed because he has developed habits of faithfulness in smaller things. Discipline creates readiness. And readiness allows the steward to respond when the King opens a door. ## Discipline and the King's Trust Imagine a king entrusting a valuable responsibility to two servants. One servant is talented but inconsistent. The other may have fewer abilities, but he is dependable. Who would the king trust with greater responsibility? The lesson is not that talent is unimportant. It is that talent without discipline can become unreliable. The King is not only looking at what we can do. He is also developing who we are. Character determines how safely responsibility can be carried. Discipline strengthens character. And strong character makes a steward more trustworthy. ## The Daily Yes Faithfulness is often built through many small yeses. Yes to prayer. Yes to honesty. Yes to responsibility. Yes to preparation. Yes to generosity. Yes to forgiveness. Yes to diligence. Yes to rest when rest is needed. Yes to saying no when no is necessary. Yes to obedience. One day of discipline may not seem significant. But thousands of faithful choices can shape an entire life. The steward should therefore stop waiting for one great moment to prove faithfulness. Faithfulness is being formed today. In ordinary choices. In ordinary responsibilities. In ordinary moments. ## A Steward's Prayer for Discipline King of Kings, Thank You for everything You have placed in my hands. Teach me not only to begin well, but to continue faithfully. Give me discipline when I lack motivation. Give me strength when I become tired. Give me wisdom when I need to prioritize. Help me steward my time. Help me steward my money. Help me steward my work. Help me steward my relationships. Help me steward my spiritual life. Teach me to say no to distractions that pull me away from my assignment. Teach me to say yes to the responsibilities You have entrusted to me. When I fail, help me return instead of giving up. When results are delayed, help me remain faithful. When nobody sees me, remind me that You see. When nobody applauds, remind me that Your approval is enough. Build discipline in me—not so I can become proud of my own strength, but so I can become more dependable in Your hands. Make me a steward who can be trusted with little and with much. Let my daily choices honor You. Let my habits reflect Your priorities. Let my consistency become evidence of my faithfulness. I do not want to live only by inspiration. I want to live by conviction. I want to remain faithful when it is easy and when it is difficult. In Jesus' name, **Amen.** ## Declaration of a Disciplined Steward **I belong to the King.** **Everything entrusted to me belongs to Him.** **I will not depend only on feelings or motivation.** **I will develop discipline that protects my calling and responsibilities.** **I will steward my time wisely.** **I will manage my money responsibly.** **I will work diligently.** **I will make room for prayer and God's Word.** **I will say no to distractions that weaken my faithfulness.** **I will remain consistent even when nobody applauds.** **I will not give up because results are delayed.** **I will not demand perfection from myself, but I will continue pursuing faithfulness.** **When I fall, I will rise again.** **When I fail, I will learn.** **When I become tired, I will seek God's strength.** **When the King entrusts more to me, I will be ready.** **I will build my life through faithful daily choices.** **I am not merely starting.** **I am continuing.** **I am not merely hearing.** **I am practicing.** **I am not merely desiring faithfulness.** **I am developing a life that demonstrates it.** **I am a steward.** **He is the King.** **And by His grace, I will remain faithful—day after day, choice after choice, responsibility after responsibility.**
