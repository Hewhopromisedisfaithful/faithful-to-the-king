# Chapter 32 ## The Steward and Responsibility > **“When someone has been entrusted with much, even more will be required.”** > — Luke 12:48 NLT Every gift carries responsibility. Every opportunity carries responsibility. Every position carries responsibility. Every resource carries responsibility. Every relationship carries responsibility. When the King places something in our hands, He is not merely giving us something to enjoy. He is entrusting something to manage. That is the heart of stewardship. A steward does not simply ask: **“What have I received?”** He also asks: **“What responsibility came with what I received?”** ## Responsibility Is Part of the Trust To be entrusted with something means that someone believes it can be placed in your care. A business owner entrusts responsibilities to an employee. A parent is entrusted with the care of a child. A leader is entrusted with people. A manager is entrusted with resources. A believer is entrusted with gifts, opportunities, time, relationships, and resources. And ultimately, everything we have comes from God. Therefore, stewardship carries responsibility. The King does not ask us to own everything. He asks us to faithfully care for what He has placed in our hands. ## The Difference Between Ownership and Responsibility Ownership says: **“This is mine.”** Stewardship says: **“This has been entrusted to me.”** Responsibility says: **“Therefore, I must take care of it.”** This distinction changes everything. If we believe something belongs completely to us, we may feel free to use it however we want. But if we understand that it belongs to the King, our attitude changes. We become more careful. More intentional. More accountable. More grateful. We recognize that one day we will answer to the One who entrusted it to us. ## The King Expects Faithfulness Jesus told a parable about servants who were entrusted with different amounts. The servants did not all receive the same amount. But they were each given something to manage. The lesson is important. The King does not compare one steward's assignment with another steward's assignment. He looks for faithfulness. The question is not: **“Did I receive as much as someone else?”** The question is: **“What did I do with what I received?”** This protects the steward from comparison. Someone may have more money. Someone may have a larger platform. Someone may have greater influence. Someone may have a different opportunity. That does not change our responsibility. We are accountable for our own assignment. ## Responsibility Begins With What Is Already in Your Hands Sometimes we are so focused on what we want next that we neglect what we already have. We want a bigger opportunity. A larger business. More money. Greater influence. A higher position. A larger ministry. But the King may be asking: **“What are you doing with what I have already given you?”** Before asking for more, we should examine our present stewardship. How are we handling our current responsibilities? How are we managing our current resources? How are we treating the people already in our lives? How are we using the time we already have? How are we caring for the opportunities already before us? Faithfulness begins where we are. ## Responsibility in Small Things Jesus taught: > **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”** > — Luke 16:10 NLT Small responsibilities matter. A small amount of money can reveal financial character. A small task can reveal work ethic. A small opportunity can reveal preparation. A small responsibility can reveal reliability. A small promise can reveal integrity. We should never despise small assignments simply because they do not look impressive. The King may be using small responsibilities to prepare us for greater ones. The size of the assignment does not determine its importance. The faithfulness with which we carry it does. ## Responsibility Before Promotion Sometimes people desire promotion without desiring responsibility. They want authority without accountability. They want recognition without sacrifice. They want influence without the burden of caring for others. But kingdom stewardship works differently. Greater responsibility comes with greater accountability. Jesus said: > **“When someone has been entrusted with much, even more will be required.”** > — Luke 12:48 NLT The higher the position, the greater the responsibility. The more resources entrusted, the greater the accountability. The more influence given, the greater the need for wisdom. The more people affected by our decisions, the more carefully those decisions must be made. Promotion is not simply receiving more. It is being trusted with more. ## Responsibility and Accountability A faithful steward understands accountability. Accountability means that our actions matter. Our decisions have consequences. Our choices affect other people. Our use of resources produces results. And eventually, we answer to God. Paul wrote: > **“Yes, each of us will give a personal account to God.”** > — Romans 14:12 NLT This should not make us live in constant fear. It should make us live intentionally. We are not managing God's resources in a universe without accountability. The King sees. The King knows. The King cares. And the faithful steward lives with that awareness. ## Adam and the Failure of Responsibility The story of Adam gives us an early picture of what happens when responsibility is avoided. God had given Adam a clear command. But when Adam and Eve sinned, Adam attempted to shift responsibility. He pointed toward the woman. The woman pointed toward the serpent. Instead of simply owning the failure, responsibility was passed to someone else. This is one of the oldest human tendencies: **blame someone else.** When something goes wrong, we may immediately look for someone to blame. The economy. The situation. Our parents. Our coworkers. Our circumstances. Someone else's decision. Sometimes other people truly contribute to a problem. But the steward must still ask: **“What is my responsibility in this situation?”** That question requires maturity. ## Responsibility Does Not Mean Taking Blame for Everything Taking responsibility does not mean accepting blame for things that were never ours to carry. A faithful steward understands boundaries. We are responsible for our own decisions. We are responsible for our own obedience. We are responsible for how we respond. We are responsible for what God has entrusted to us. But we cannot control everything other people do. This distinction is important. Healthy responsibility does not say: **“Everything is my fault.”** It says: **“I will faithfully handle what is actually mine to handle.”** That is stewardship. ## Joseph and Responsibility Joseph gives us another powerful picture. Even when circumstances were unfair, Joseph continued to act faithfully. He was sold by his brothers. He became a servant. He was falsely accused. He was imprisoned. Yet wherever Joseph was placed, he handled responsibility faithfully. In Potiphar's house, Joseph was entrusted with responsibility. In prison, he was entrusted with responsibility. Eventually, Pharaoh entrusted Joseph with responsibility over Egypt. Joseph did not wait for perfect circumstances before becoming faithful. He was faithful where he was. That is an important lesson. You do not need perfect circumstances to practice faithful stewardship. You can be responsible in the place where you are today. ## Responsibility During Difficult Seasons Some people become responsible only when life is easy. But character is revealed when circumstances become difficult. What happens when business is slow? What happens when money is limited? What happens when people disappoint you? What happens when the opportunity disappears? What happens when the work becomes exhausting? The faithful steward does not say: **“Because life is difficult, I no longer need to be responsible.”** He asks: **“What is still mine to faithfully carry in this season?”** Even difficult seasons contain responsibilities. Sometimes the responsibility is to continue. Sometimes it is to wait. Sometimes it is to rebuild. Sometimes it is to learn. Sometimes it is to ask for help. Sometimes it is to release something that God is no longer asking us to carry. Wisdom determines what responsibility looks like in each season. ## Responsibility for Our Words Words are also entrusted resources. We can build with them. We can destroy with them. We can encourage. We can discourage. We can tell truth. We can deceive. We can heal. We can wound. Jesus taught that our words matter. Therefore, a steward must take responsibility for what comes out of his mouth. Before speaking, wisdom asks: **Is it true?** **Is it necessary?** **Is it loving?** **Is it helpful?** **Is this the right time?** Not every thought needs to become a sentence. Not every emotion needs to become a public statement. The disciplined steward learns to manage his words because words carry influence. ## Responsibility for Our Influence Influence is another form of stewardship. You may influence family members. Friends. Coworkers. Employees. Customers. Church members. Young people. People who look up to you. You may not realize how closely someone is watching your decisions. That is why influence must be handled carefully. The question is not only: **“Can I influence people?”** It is: **“What am I influencing them toward?”** A steward should never use influence merely to make himself important. Influence should be used to serve. To encourage. To protect. To guide. To point people toward what is good and true. ## Responsibility for Opportunities Opportunities are not always permanent. Sometimes God gives us a door for a particular season. That means opportunities require attention. When an opportunity appears, the steward should ask: **“Why has this opportunity been placed before me?”** Perhaps it is for growth. Perhaps it is for service. Perhaps it is for preparation. Perhaps it is for blessing others. Perhaps it is simply a temporary assignment. Not every opportunity must be maximized for personal gain. Some opportunities exist so that we can serve someone else. The wise steward therefore seeks God's purpose, not merely personal advantage. ## Responsibility for Failure One of the strongest signs of maturity is learning how to handle failure. When we fail, we have choices. We can hide. We can blame. We can deny. We can become bitter. Or we can take responsibility, learn, repent where necessary, and grow. A faithful steward does not pretend to be perfect. He becomes teachable. If we make a mistake with money, we learn. If we mishandle a relationship, we seek reconciliation. If we make a poor decision, we examine what went wrong. If we fail in an assignment, we learn what the experience can teach us. Failure does not have to become our identity. It can become instruction. ## Responsibility and Repentance When our failure involves sin, responsibility must go deeper. We do not merely analyze what happened. We repent. Repentance means turning away from what is wrong and returning to God. A steward cannot faithfully continue while refusing to acknowledge what needs correction. David's life reminds us that even when someone has failed seriously, honest repentance matters. The faithful response is not: **“I will protect my image at all costs.”** It is: **“Lord, I was wrong. Forgive me. Change me. Teach me to walk differently.”** That is responsibility before the King. ## Responsibility for What We Do Not Know A responsible steward also knows when to say: **“I don't know.”** Pretending to know everything is not wisdom. Sometimes responsibility means asking for help. Sometimes it means seeking counsel. Sometimes it means admitting that we need training. Sometimes it means bringing someone more qualified into the situation. Humility protects the assignment. A steward does not need to prove that he can do everything. He needs to make sure the responsibility is handled faithfully. ## Responsibility and Delegation There are times when responsibility includes delegating. Delegation is not abandoning responsibility. It is recognizing that some assignments are too broad for one person to carry alone. Moses learned this when Jethro advised him to share responsibility with capable people. A wise steward understands: **“I am responsible for making sure the assignment is handled well, even if I am not personally doing every task.”** This is especially important as responsibilities grow. Greater stewardship often requires greater organization. Greater organization often requires trusted people. And trusted people require clear expectations, accountability, and communication. ## Responsibility Is Not Control A steward can become so concerned about responsibility that he begins controlling everything. But responsibility does not mean control over every detail. There is a difference. Control says: **“Everything must happen exactly according to me.”** Responsibility says: **“I will faithfully manage what has been entrusted to me.”** The first can produce fear and exhaustion. The second produces healthy stewardship. We are responsible for our assignment. God remains sovereign over the outcome. ## The Responsibility to Rest Even rest can be a stewardship responsibility. Some people believe that working constantly proves faithfulness. It does not. The human body has limits. The mind has limits. The steward must recognize those limits. Rest can restore strength. Sleep can restore the body. Quiet time can restore perspective. Sabbath principles remind us that our value does not come merely from productivity. We are not machines. A faithful steward knows when to work and when to rest. Rest does not mean abandoning responsibility. Sometimes rest is what allows us to carry responsibility faithfully for the long term. ## The Responsibility to Prepare Faithful stewardship includes preparation. Noah prepared before the flood. Joseph prepared during years of abundance for years of famine. Nehemiah prepared before rebuilding Jerusalem's walls. Preparation is a form of responsibility because it respects what is coming. We should not always wait until a problem appears before preparing. If we know a responsibility is coming, prepare. If we know a season will change, prepare. If we know an opportunity may require new skills, prepare. If we know greater responsibility may come, develop character now. Preparation says: **“I respect the assignment enough to prepare for it.”** ## Responsibility and the Next Generation What we steward today can affect people tomorrow. Our children may learn from how we manage money. Others may learn from how we handle pressure. Employees may learn from how we lead. Younger believers may learn from how we respond to failure. People may learn from how we treat those who cannot benefit us. Our stewardship can become an inheritance of wisdom. That is why responsibility is bigger than ourselves. We are often preparing something that will outlive us. ## The Weight of “Much” Jesus said: > **“When someone has been entrusted with much, even more will be required.”** > — Luke 12:48 NLT This principle should change how we view increase. More is not simply more comfort. More can mean more responsibility. More money may mean greater financial stewardship. More influence may mean greater accountability. More employees may mean greater responsibility for people. More opportunities may mean greater responsibility for time. More authority may mean greater responsibility for decisions. Therefore, when we pray for increase, we should also be willing to pray: **“Lord, prepare me to carry the responsibility that comes with it.”** ## Becoming Trustworthy Ultimately, responsibility is about trustworthiness. Can the King trust us with what He places in our hands? Can He trust us with small things? Can He trust us with resources? Can He trust us with influence? Can He trust us with people? Can He trust us with success? Can He trust us with disappointment? Can He trust us when nobody is watching? Can He trust us when things do not go according to our plans? Trustworthiness is not built in one dramatic moment. It is built through repeated faithfulness. One responsibility at a time. One decision at a time. One season at a time. ## A Steward's Responsibility Before the King At the end of the day, the faithful steward remembers: **I am responsible, but I am not the owner.** **I am accountable, but I am not the King.** **I have duties, but I also have grace.** **I must work faithfully, but I must trust God with the outcome.** This balance is important. We should never use God's sovereignty as an excuse for irresponsibility. And we should never use responsibility as an excuse to live as though everything depends entirely on us. The steward works faithfully. The King remains sovereign. The steward carries the assignment. The King carries the ultimate outcome. The steward obeys. The King provides. The steward prepares. The King directs. The steward remains faithful. The King receives the glory. ## A Steward's Prayer of Responsibility King of Kings, Thank You for trusting me with the things You have placed in my hands. Teach me to recognize every responsibility as a sacred trust. Help me not to complain about what You have entrusted to me. Help me not to compare my assignment with someone else's. Help me to faithfully manage what I have today. Give me wisdom to know what is mine to carry and what I must release. Teach me to take responsibility for my decisions. Teach me to admit my mistakes. Give me humility to receive correction. Give me courage to make things right when I have done wrong. Help me care for people. Help me manage resources. Help me protect opportunities. Help me use influence wisely. Help me prepare for greater responsibility without becoming proud. And when You entrust more to me, remind me that more is not merely a greater blessing. It is also a greater responsibility. Make me trustworthy. Make me dependable. Make me faithful. May You be able to place something in my hands and know that I will seek Your wisdom, follow Your instruction, and handle it according to Your will. I am not the owner. You are. I am not the King. You are. I am Your steward. And I want to be found faithful. In Jesus' name, **Amen.** ## Declaration of a Responsible Steward **I belong to the King.** **Everything I have is ultimately His.** **I will faithfully care for what He has entrusted to me.** **I will not compare my assignment with someone else's.** **I will take responsibility for my decisions.** **I will not use excuses to escape my responsibilities.** **I will admit my mistakes and learn from them.** **I will seek wisdom when I do not know what to do.** **I will ask for help when help is needed.** **I will protect the people, resources, opportunities, and responsibilities placed in my care.** **I will be faithful in little things.** **I will prepare for greater responsibility without seeking greater pride.** **I will remember that greater authority means greater responsibility.** **I will not confuse responsibility with ownership.** **I will faithfully carry what belongs to me while trusting God with what belongs to Him.** **When I receive much, I will remember that much will be required.** **When I succeed, I will remain humble.** **When I fail, I will repent, learn, and rise again.** **When no one is watching, I will still be faithful.** **I am a steward.** **He is the King.** **What He places in my hands is a trust.** **And by His grace, I will handle that trust faithfully.**
