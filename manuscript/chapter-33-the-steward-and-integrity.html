# Chapter 33 ## The Steward and Integrity A steward may be given responsibility, authority, resources, opportunities, and influence—but none of these things can replace integrity. Responsibility asks, **“Will I carry what the King has entrusted to me?”** Integrity asks, **“How will I carry it when no one is watching?”** A person may appear faithful in public while being careless in private. A person may speak about honesty while making dishonest decisions when money is involved. A person may be trusted with authority and yet use that authority for personal gain. But the King does not merely look at what is visible. He looks at the heart. > **“Whoever walks in integrity walks securely, but whoever takes crooked paths will be found out.”** > — Proverbs 10:9 Integrity is not perfection. Integrity is wholeness. It means that the person we are in private is not completely different from the person people see in public. It means our words and actions are moving in the same direction. It means we do not change our principles simply because the situation has changed. For the steward of the King, integrity is not optional. It is part of faithful stewardship. --- ## 1. Integrity Means Being Whole The word integrity carries the idea of being complete, undivided, and morally sound. A steward with integrity does not live with two separate standards: One standard for church. Another standard for business. One standard when people are watching. Another standard when nobody is watching. One standard when there is something to gain. Another standard when honesty becomes costly. The King desires a heart that is whole. David prayed: > **“May integrity and honesty protect me, for I put my hope in you.”** > — Psalm 25:21 Integrity becomes a protection because it keeps the steward from constantly having to manage different versions of himself. When we tell the truth, we do not need to remember which version of the story we told. When we keep our commitments, we do not need to constantly explain why we broke them. When we handle money honestly, we do not need to hide what we have done. Integrity brings simplicity to the soul. --- ## 2. The King Sees What Others Cannot See Human beings often judge what they can see. God sees deeper. > **“People look at the outward appearance, but the LORD looks at the heart.”** > — 1 Samuel 16:7 This is important for every steward. There may be things that nobody else knows about us. Private decisions. Private conversations. Private financial choices. Private habits. Private compromises. Private victories. The world may never know some of these things. But the King knows. And this should not merely make us afraid. It should make us desire to live honestly before Him. Integrity is choosing what is right not because someone might discover us, but because **the King already sees us**. --- ## 3. Integrity Is Tested in Small Things Many people imagine that integrity is only tested in major situations. But Scripture teaches us that faithfulness is often revealed in small things. Jesus said: > **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”** > — Luke 16:10 A small dishonest amount is still dishonesty. A small broken promise still reveals something about our character. A small misuse of someone else's trust still matters. A small compromise may seem harmless, but repeated compromises can shape a person's character. This is why the steward must learn to honor the King in the ordinary. How we handle a small amount of money. How we return something that does not belong to us. How we speak about someone who is not present. How we respond when nobody will reward us. How we fulfill a commitment that has become inconvenient. How we treat people who cannot give us anything in return. These moments may appear small to people. But they are not small to the King. --- ## 4. Integrity With Money Money is one of the clearest places where integrity is tested. It is possible to speak beautifully about stewardship while being dishonest with finances. But the King cares about how His resources are handled. A steward must resist: * deception, * manipulation, * hidden transactions, * dishonest reporting, * taking what does not belong to him, * exploiting another person's weakness, * and using entrusted resources for selfish purposes. The issue is not merely how much money we have. The issue is whether the King can trust us with what He has placed in our hands. Proverbs says: > **“The LORD detests lying lips, but he delights in people who are trustworthy.”** > — Proverbs 12:22 A faithful steward understands that money is not merely currency. It is also a test of character. --- ## 5. Integrity in Business and Work Integrity must also enter our work. A Christian steward should not need to abandon biblical principles simply because he enters the marketplace. Our business practices should reflect the King we serve. That means being honest about what we sell. Being truthful about quality. Keeping agreements. Treating workers fairly. Paying what is owed. Correcting mistakes honestly. Refusing to deceive customers. Giving accurate information. Not taking advantage of someone simply because we have more knowledge or bargaining power. Excellence without integrity is incomplete. Success without integrity is dangerous. Profit without integrity can become a trap. The steward must remember: **The business may be in my hands, but it ultimately belongs to the King.** --- ## 6. Integrity in Words Our words are also part of our stewardship. A steward's mouth can build trust or destroy it. Promises should not be made casually. Truth should not be adjusted simply to make ourselves look better. Gossip should not become entertainment. Secrets entrusted to us should not become stories we tell others. James reminds us that the tongue has great power. Therefore, integrity requires us to ask: **Are my words trustworthy?** Can people believe what I say? Do my words match my actions? Do I speak differently about a person when they are absent? Do I exaggerate to make myself appear more successful? Do I tell the truth even when the truth makes me look less impressive? These questions matter. A steward does not only manage possessions. A steward manages influence. And words carry influence. --- ## 7. Daniel: Integrity Under Pressure Daniel gives us a powerful picture of integrity. His enemies searched for a reason to accuse him, but they could not find corruption in his conduct. > **“Finally, the men said, ‘We will never find any basis for charges against this Daniel unless it has something to do with the law of his God.’”** > — Daniel 6:5 Think about the significance of that. His enemies examined his life. They looked for weakness. They searched for corruption. They wanted evidence against him. But they found no ordinary corruption to use. The only thing they could attack was his devotion to God. Daniel's integrity did not come from having an easy environment. He lived in a culture that did not necessarily share his convictions. He served under powerful rulers. He faced pressure. Yet his character remained consistent. That is stewardship. Integrity is not proven when everything is easy. It is proven when compromise would be easier. --- ## 8. Joseph: Integrity When Nobody Was Watching Joseph also demonstrates integrity in a difficult environment. When Potiphar's wife tempted him, Joseph could have reasoned that nobody would know. But Joseph understood something deeper. He was not merely accountable to Potiphar. He was accountable to God. He said: > **“How could I do such a wicked thing? It would be a great sin against God.”** > — Genesis 39:9 Joseph's decision was not based merely on consequences. It was based on relationship with God. That is the heart of integrity. The question is not only: **“What will happen if I get caught?”** The better question is: **“Is this pleasing to the King?”** That question can protect a steward from many compromises. --- ## 9. Integrity When It Costs You Integrity becomes especially valuable when honesty costs something. It may cost money. It may cost an opportunity. It may cost popularity. It may cost convenience. It may require admitting that we were wrong. It may require returning something we could have kept. It may require saying no when everyone else says yes. This is where integrity becomes more than a word. It becomes obedience. A steward must sometimes choose: **character over profit, truth over image, obedience over opportunity, and faithfulness over convenience.** The King is not looking for stewards who remain honest only when honesty is profitable. He is forming people who remain faithful even when faithfulness is costly. --- ## 10. Integrity After Failure Integrity does not mean that a steward will never make a mistake. We are still human. We may fail. We may make wrong decisions. We may say something we should not have said. We may mishandle something entrusted to us. The question is what we do afterward. A person without integrity may hide the mistake, blame someone else, manipulate the story, or pretend nothing happened. A person walking in integrity can say: **“I was wrong.”** **“I need to make this right.”** **“Please forgive me.”** **“I need to correct what I did.”** That kind of humility is not weakness. It is strength of character. Repentance is also part of faithful stewardship. When we discover that we have handled something wrongly, we should not protect our image at the expense of truth. We should protect what matters more: **our relationship with God and the integrity of our character.** --- ## 11. Integrity and Trust Trust takes time to build. It can take years to establish a reputation for honesty. But one act of dishonesty can damage what took years to build. This is why Proverbs says: > **“The integrity of the upright guides them, but the unfaithful are destroyed by their duplicity.”** > — Proverbs 11:3 Integrity becomes a guide. When the steward knows his values, decisions become clearer. When temptation comes, he already knows his answer. When an opportunity requires compromise, he does not need to negotiate with himself. He has already decided: **“I belong to the King.”** That decision settles many other decisions. --- ## 12. Integrity in Secret Places One of the greatest tests of stewardship is what we do in secret. What happens when nobody applauds? What happens when nobody checks? What happens when there is no recognition? What happens when we could take advantage of a situation without being discovered? This is where the true condition of the heart is often revealed. Jesus taught His followers to practice righteousness without seeking human applause. The steward must learn to live before the audience of One. Not because people do not matter. They matter deeply. But ultimately, our faithfulness is before the King. A hidden act of obedience may never receive public recognition. But it is still seen by God. A private decision to remain honest may never appear in a testimony. But it still matters. A secret refusal to compromise may never be celebrated. But the King knows. --- ## 13. Integrity Is More Valuable Than Image Image asks: **“What do people think about me?”** Integrity asks: **“Who am I before God?”** Image can be managed. Integrity must be lived. Image depends on appearance. Integrity depends on character. Image wants applause. Integrity wants faithfulness. This does not mean reputation is unimportant. Scripture teaches us to live in such a way that our conduct gives a good testimony. But the steward must never sacrifice integrity just to protect an image. If protecting our image requires hiding the truth, then the image has become more important than the King. The steward must choose truth. --- ## 14. The Integrity of the King Ultimately, our integrity is a response to the character of God Himself. God is faithful. God is truthful. God keeps His promises. God does not change according to circumstances. > **“God is not a man, so he does not lie. He is not human, so he does not change his mind.”** > — Numbers 23:19 The King we serve is faithful. Therefore, His stewards should desire to reflect His faithfulness. We do not become people of integrity merely by trying harder. We grow in integrity as we surrender ourselves to God and allow His character to shape ours. The closer we walk with the King, the more we should desire that our lives reflect the values of His kingdom. --- ## 15. Integrity Is Part of the Steward's Witness People may forget our words. They may forget our accomplishments. They may forget what we owned. But they may remember whether they could trust us. A faithful steward becomes a testimony through character. Our integrity can make the Gospel visible. When a Christian refuses dishonest profit, people notice. When a Christian admits a mistake, people notice. When a Christian keeps his word, people notice. When a Christian treats the weak with dignity, people notice. When a Christian refuses to compromise even when compromise would benefit him, people notice. Our lives become a message. Not because we are perfect. But because we are committed to walking faithfully before the King. --- # The Steward's Integrity Checklist Before moving forward, every steward can ask: **Am I the same person in private that I appear to be in public?** **Can the people who trust me depend on my word?** **Do I handle money honestly?** **Do I keep commitments even when they become inconvenient?** **Do I tell the truth even when it makes me look bad?** **Do I speak about others with the same respect whether they are present or absent?** **Do I refuse opportunities that require me to compromise my convictions?** **When I fail, am I willing to admit it and make things right?** **Do I value character more than appearance?** **Can the King trust me with what He has placed in my hands?** These are not questions designed to condemn us. They are questions designed to examine us. Because the goal of stewardship is not merely to look faithful. It is to **be faithful**. --- # The King's Standard The world may measure success through wealth, influence, recognition, position, or achievement. The King measures differently. He looks for faithfulness. He looks for truth. He looks for a heart that can be trusted. He looks for a steward who will not sell integrity for temporary gain. And one day, what will matter most will not be how impressive our lives appeared to others. What will matter is whether we were faithful with what He entrusted to us. The steward does not need to be famous. He needs to be faithful. He does not need to be seen by everyone. He needs to be known by the King. He does not need to possess everything. He needs to honor the One who owns everything. --- # Reflection Take a moment and ask the Lord: **Where is my integrity being tested right now?** Is it in money? In business? In relationships? In my words? In my private life? In an opportunity? In something nobody else knows about? Then ask: **“Lord, what would faithfulness look like here?”** Do not merely ask God to give you a better situation. Ask Him to give you a faithful heart within the situation you already have. Because sometimes the King changes the circumstances. But sometimes He uses the circumstances to change the steward. --- # Declaration of the Faithful Steward I belong to the King. Everything entrusted to me belongs ultimately to Him. Therefore, I will walk in integrity. I will choose truth over deception. I will choose character over profit. I will choose faithfulness over convenience. I will keep my word. I will handle resources honestly. I will honor people whether they can benefit me or not. I will do what is right even when nobody is watching. When I fail, I will not hide. I will humble myself, repent, make things right, and continue walking with God. I will not trade my integrity for temporary gain. My character is part of my stewardship. My words are part of my stewardship. My decisions are part of my stewardship. My private life is part of my stewardship. I desire to be the same person before God in secret and before people in public. May my life reflect the character of the King I serve. And when my stewardship is examined, may I be found trustworthy. **I am not the owner.** **I am the steward.** **And I will steward everything with integrity before my King.** --- # Prayer **King of Kings,** Thank You for everything You have entrusted to me. Forgive me for the times when I have valued appearance more than character, convenience more than obedience, or temporary gain more than faithfulness. Search my heart and reveal anything in me that needs to change. Teach me to walk in integrity. Help me to be truthful in my words, honest in my dealings, faithful with money, trustworthy in relationships, and consistent in both public and private life. Give me courage to choose what is right when doing what is right becomes costly. When nobody is watching, remind me that You see me. When temptation comes, remind me that I belong to You. When I fail, give me humility to admit it, wisdom to correct it, and grace to move forward faithfully. Make my character worthy of the trust You have placed in me. May my life become a testimony of Your faithfulness. I do not want merely to appear faithful. I want to be faithful. I do not want merely to manage what You have given me. I want to honor You with how I manage it. Everything belongs to You. My life belongs to You. My resources belong to You. My opportunities belong to You. My influence belongs to You. And I belong to You. **Make me a steward of integrity—faithful before people, but above all, faithful before You.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.**
