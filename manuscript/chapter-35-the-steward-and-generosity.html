# Chapter 35 ## The Steward and Generosity A steward who understands that everything belongs to the King eventually learns another important truth: **What God places in our hands is not meant only to stay in our hands.** Some things are meant to be shared. Some resources are meant to be given. Some opportunities are meant to open doors for others. Some gifts are meant to serve. Some words are meant to encourage. Some time is meant to be spent helping someone who cannot repay us. Generosity is therefore not merely about money. It is about recognizing that the King can use what He has entrusted to us to become a blessing to others. Paul reminded the believers: > **“You should remember the words of the Lord Jesus: ‘It is more blessed to give than to receive.’”** > — Acts 20:35 The generous steward understands: **I am not the final destination of everything God puts in my hands.** Sometimes I am the channel. --- ## 1. The Owner Gives; the Steward Distributes If God is the Owner, then everything we possess has ultimately come from Him. Our money. Our time. Our abilities. Our knowledge. Our opportunities. Our influence. Our possessions. Our relationships. Our strength. Even our ability to work and produce comes from Him. Therefore, generosity begins with recognition: **“What I have is not entirely mine.”** This changes the way we hold things. Instead of gripping everything tightly, we become willing to release what God asks us to release. The steward does not say: **“This is mine, and nobody can touch it.”** The steward says: **“Lord, how do You want me to use what You have placed in my hands?”** --- ## 2. Generosity Begins in the Heart Generosity is not first a financial issue. It is a heart issue. A person can give money and still have a selfish heart. Another person may have very little money but be incredibly generous with time, encouragement, service, hospitality, and compassion. Jesus said: > **“Wherever your treasure is, there the desires of your heart will also be.”** > — Matthew 6:21 Generosity reveals what is happening inside us. If we are always thinking: **“How can I get more for myself?”** our heart may be moving toward self-centeredness. But if we begin asking: **“How can what God gave me bless someone else?”** our heart is learning the King's way. --- ## 3. Generosity Is a Response to Grace We do not give because God needs our resources. God owns everything already. > **“The earth is the LORD’s, and everything in it.”** > — Psalm 24:1 God does not become richer because we give. We give because **we have already received**. God gave us life. God gave us salvation. God gave us grace. God gave us opportunities. God gave us people. God gives daily provision. The greatest example is Jesus Christ. > **“For this is how God loved the world: He gave his one and only Son.”** > — John 3:16 Christian generosity flows from receiving God's grace. We give because we have first been given to. --- ## 4. The Generous Steward Does Not Give to Impress Generosity can become another way to seek recognition. Someone may give because they want people to praise them. Someone may help because they want everyone to know what they have done. Someone may post every act of generosity publicly because recognition has become part of the reward. Jesus warned against this kind of motivation. He taught: > **“When you give to someone in need, don’t do as the hypocrites do—blowing trumpets in the synagogues and on the streets to call attention to their acts of charity.”** > — Matthew 6:2 Then Jesus taught His followers to give quietly. The principle is clear: **The King cares not only about what we give, but why we give.** Generosity should not become a performance. It should become worship. --- ## 5. Giving in Secret Jesus said: > **“When you give to someone in need, don’t let your left hand know what your right hand is doing.”** > — Matthew 6:3 The point is not that every act of giving must literally remain secret. There are legitimate situations where generosity is public for practical reasons or where testimony encourages others. The deeper principle is this: **Our motivation should not depend on applause.** A steward should be willing to help even when nobody knows. A hidden act of kindness is still seen by God. A meal given quietly. A bill helped privately. A person encouraged through a difficult day. A worker treated generously. A struggling family supported without humiliation. A person forgiven when revenge would have been easier. These acts may never become public stories. But the King sees them. --- ## 6. Generosity Is More Than Money Money is one expression of generosity, but it is not the only one. A steward can be generous with: ### Time Some people need our presence more than our possessions. ### Knowledge What we have learned can help someone else avoid unnecessary mistakes. ### Skills A skill that God developed in us can become a blessing to another person. ### Encouragement A timely word can strengthen someone who is close to giving up. ### Hospitality Opening our home and making people feel welcome can be an act of generosity. ### Forgiveness Sometimes generosity means releasing a debt of offense. ### Opportunity Sometimes the most generous thing we can do is open a door for someone else. ### Attention In a distracted world, simply listening can be a powerful gift. Generosity asks: **“What has God placed in me or around me that can become a blessing to someone else?”** --- ## 7. The Good Samaritan Jesus told the story of the Good Samaritan to demonstrate what loving one's neighbor looks like. The Samaritan encountered a wounded man who had been ignored by others. He stopped. He cared for him. He used his own resources. He brought him to safety. He paid for his care. He did not merely feel compassion. **He acted on it.** That is important. Generosity is not merely having good intentions. Generosity eventually becomes action. We can say: **“I care about people.”** But generosity asks: **“What am I willing to do?”** The Samaritan's generosity cost him time, resources, attention, and inconvenience. Yet he became an example of practical love. --- ## 8. Generosity Can Be Inconvenient Sometimes we imagine generosity as giving away something we do not need. But genuine generosity can sometimes cost us. It may require giving time when we are busy. Serving when we are tired. Helping when there is no immediate benefit. Sharing when we would rather keep everything. Forgiving when we feel justified. Opening a door for someone else when we could have taken the opportunity ourselves. This does not mean we should ignore healthy boundaries or become irresponsible. A steward must still use wisdom. But generosity should have enough room in our lives that it sometimes costs us something. --- ## 9. The Widow's Offering Jesus once watched people giving at the temple. Many wealthy people gave large amounts. Then a poor widow gave two small coins. Jesus said: > **“This poor widow has given more than all the others who are making contributions.”** > — Mark 12:43 Why? Because Jesus was not measuring the gift merely by its numerical amount. He saw the heart behind it. This teaches us something important: **God does not compare our generosity by earthly measurements.** A large gift may come from abundance. A small gift may represent enormous sacrifice. The King sees what people cannot see. Therefore, never despise a small act of generosity. A small gift given with love can matter deeply. --- ## 10. Generosity Without Wisdom However, generosity does not mean giving without wisdom. A steward must still exercise discernment. We should not allow manipulation to control our giving. We should not create unnecessary financial irresponsibility in the name of generosity. We should not neglect legitimate responsibilities simply to appear generous. Paul taught believers to give thoughtfully and willingly. > **“You must each decide in your heart how much to give. And don’t give reluctantly or in response to pressure.”** > — 2 Corinthians 9:7 Generosity and wisdom belong together. A faithful steward asks: **“Lord, what are You asking me to give?”** not merely: **“What will make me look generous?”** --- ## 11. Generosity and Cheerfulness Paul continues: > **“For God loves a person who gives cheerfully.”** > — 2 Corinthians 9:7 There is a difference between giving and being generous in spirit. A person can give while complaining. A person can give while feeling forced. A person can give while keeping score. But the Kingdom invites us into something deeper. A cheerful giver understands: **“It is a privilege to participate in God's work.”** Generosity becomes joyful when we stop seeing ourselves as losing something and start seeing ourselves as participating in something greater. --- ## 12. Generosity and the Principle of Sowing Paul also wrote: > **“Remember this—a farmer who plants only a few seeds will get a small crop. But the one who plants generously will get a generous crop.”** > — 2 Corinthians 9:6 This is not a promise that every financial gift will return to us as financial wealth. The principle is broader. Generosity creates opportunities for blessing. A generous person often creates environments where others can flourish. Generous encouragement can produce courage. Generous teaching can produce wisdom. Generous service can produce strength. Generous resources can meet needs. Generous opportunities can change someone's future. We should not give merely to get. We give because **the King is generous**, and we want our lives to reflect Him. --- ## 13. Generosity and the Local Community A steward should notice the needs around him. There may be people in our own community who need help. Someone may need food. Someone may need work. Someone may need transportation. Someone may need a listening ear. Someone may need practical assistance. Someone may need an opportunity. The generous steward does not need to solve every problem. But he refuses to become blind to every problem. We should ask: **“King, what needs around me have You equipped me to respond to?”** Sometimes the answer will be yes. Sometimes it will be no. Wisdom determines the assignment. But generosity keeps our hearts open. --- ## 14. Generosity and Business Business owners and leaders also have opportunities to practice generosity. Generosity can influence how we treat employees. How we compensate people. How we respond when someone makes a mistake. How we support someone during a difficult season. How we create opportunities for others. How we handle customers. How we use profits. How we invest in the community. A business does not need to become financially irresponsible to become generous. But if God gives someone greater resources, those resources create greater opportunities for stewardship. Remember: **Greater resources do not merely create greater comfort.** They can create **greater responsibility**. --- ## 15. Generosity and the Next Generation One of the greatest forms of generosity is investing in people who will come after us. Teaching. Mentoring. Training. Encouraging. Giving opportunities. Sharing wisdom. Correcting with love. Preparing someone else to carry responsibility. This is generosity because we are giving something that cannot always be measured in money. Moses invested in Joshua. Elijah invested in Elisha. Paul invested in Timothy. The pattern continues. A faithful steward does not ask only: **“What can I build for myself?”** He also asks: **“Who can I help prepare for what God will entrust to them?”** --- ## 16. Generosity and the Gospel The greatest treasure we have received is the Gospel. We should never treat it as something to keep exclusively for ourselves. Jesus commanded His followers: > **“Go and make disciples of all the nations.”** > — Matthew 28:19 The message of Christ is meant to be shared. We share through our words. We share through our lives. We share through love. We share through service. We share through compassion. Generosity therefore includes giving people an opportunity to encounter the truth and love of Christ. --- ## 17. Generosity and Sacrifice There is a deeper level of generosity where giving becomes sacrifice. Jesus is the greatest example. Paul wrote: > **“Though he was rich, yet for your sakes he became poor, so that by his poverty he could make you rich.”** > — 2 Corinthians 8:9 Christ gave Himself. He did not merely give something He possessed. He gave His life. This is the highest picture of kingdom generosity. Christian generosity ultimately points back to Jesus. We do not give in order to earn salvation. We give because salvation has already been given to us through Christ. Grace produces generosity. --- ## 18. Generosity and Boundaries Being generous does not mean allowing everyone to control our resources. Jesus Himself demonstrated boundaries. He did not respond to every demand in the same way. He withdrew to pray. He chose where to go. He entrusted responsibility to His disciples. He did not allow crowds to dictate His entire schedule. A faithful steward must learn that generosity requires discernment. Sometimes the most generous response is **yes**. Sometimes it is **not yet**. Sometimes it is **no**. Saying no to one request may allow us to say yes to the assignment God actually gave us. Generosity without wisdom can become disorder. Generosity guided by God becomes stewardship. --- ## 19. Generosity When We Have Little We should never think: **“I will become generous when I become rich.”** Generosity can begin now. A kind word costs little. A listening ear costs time. Sharing food may be possible. Helping someone with a skill may cost nothing. Praying for someone is a gift. Encouraging someone can change a day. A small act can become significant in God's hands. The widow's offering reminds us that generosity is not reserved for people with abundance. Generosity begins with willingness. --- ## 20. Generosity When We Have Much At the same time, abundance creates responsibility. If the King entrusts more resources to us, we should not assume that every increase is meant to increase our lifestyle. Some increase may be for greater giving. Some may be for investment. Some may be for building something that serves others. Some may be for helping a family member. Some may be for creating employment. Some may be for supporting ministry. Some may be for future responsibilities. The question is not: **“How much more can I consume?”** The better question is: **“How much more can I faithfully steward?”** --- ## 21. Generosity Protects the Heart From Possessions Contentment teaches us not to be controlled by what we lack. Generosity teaches us not to be controlled by what we possess. Together, they create freedom. Contentment says: **“I do not need everything.”** Generosity says: **“Therefore, I can release some things.”** When we give, we remind ourselves that possessions are tools, not masters. We can hold resources with an open hand. If God says keep, we keep. If God says give, we give. If God says invest, we invest. If God says wait, we wait. The steward's hand remains open to the King. --- ## 22. The Blessing of Being a Channel A river remains fresh because water flows through it. There is a spiritual principle in becoming a channel rather than a container. God blesses us so that we can become a blessing. He gives us wisdom so we can teach. He gives us strength so we can serve. He gives us resources so we can help. He gives us influence so we can open doors. He gives us comfort so we can comfort others. Paul wrote: > **“He comforts us in all our troubles so that we can comfort others.”** > — 2 Corinthians 1:4 What we receive from God can become something we pass to others. That is the beauty of stewardship. --- # The Generosity Check Before giving, ask: **Is my heart willing?** **Am I giving because God is leading me or because I want recognition?** **Can I give without keeping score?** **Can I give wisely rather than emotionally?** **Will this generosity help the person or situation appropriately?** **Am I neglecting responsibilities that God has already given me?** **Can I give my time, not just my money?** **Can I give encouragement, knowledge, skills, forgiveness, or opportunity?** **Am I becoming a channel of what God has placed in my hands?** These questions help us practice generosity without losing wisdom. --- # The Steward's Open Hand A closed hand says: **“I must protect everything.”** An open hand says: **“I trust the King.”** The open hand does not mean carelessness. It means surrender. It means we understand that the King can place something into our hands and can also ask us to release it. Sometimes He gives. Sometimes He asks us to give. Sometimes He takes us into another season. But the faithful steward remains faithful because his security is not in the possession. His security is in the King. --- # Reflection Ask yourself: **What has God placed in my hands that could become a blessing to someone else?** Maybe it is money. Maybe it is time. Maybe it is knowledge. Maybe it is a skill. Maybe it is an opportunity. Maybe it is encouragement. Maybe it is forgiveness. Maybe it is a connection. Maybe it is a door that you can open for someone else. Now ask: **Am I holding it too tightly?** And finally: **“Lord, how do You want me to use what You have entrusted to me?”** Do not rush the answer. Listen. Pray. Use wisdom. Then obey. --- # Declaration of the Generous Steward I belong to the King. Everything I have ultimately comes from Him. Therefore, I will not live with a closed hand. I will receive with gratitude. I will steward with wisdom. I will give with love. I will serve with humility. I will not give merely to be seen. I will not give merely to receive praise. I will not allow greed to control what God has entrusted to me. I will remember that resources are tools, not masters. I will be generous with my time. Generous with my gifts. Generous with encouragement. Generous with compassion. Generous with opportunities. Generous with resources according to God's wisdom. I will not despise small acts of generosity. I will not wait until I have much before I begin to give. When God increases what is in my hands, I will remember that increase carries responsibility. When God asks me to release something, I will trust Him. I will become a channel of blessing. What God has placed in me can strengthen someone else. What God has placed in my hands can help someone else. What God has taught me can prepare someone else. I am not the owner. I am the steward. And I will use what the King has entrusted to me to bless others and bring glory to Him. **My hands are open.** **My heart is willing.** **My resources are surrendered.** **My life belongs to the King.** --- # Prayer **King of Kings,** Thank You for every good thing You have placed in my life. Thank You for the resources, abilities, opportunities, relationships, knowledge, time, and strength You have entrusted to me. Forgive me for the times I have held too tightly to what You placed in my hands. Forgive me when fear made me selfish. Forgive me when comparison made me unwilling to give. Teach me to become generous. Give me wisdom to know when to give, how to give, and where my giving can truly help. Protect me from giving for recognition. Protect me from manipulation. Protect me from foolishness. Give me a generous heart that is also guided by wisdom. Teach me to see opportunities to bless people. Help me notice those who need encouragement. Help me recognize where my skills can serve. Help me use my resources responsibly. Help me invest in people. Help me create opportunities for others. Help me become a blessing without seeking the glory for myself. Remind me that everything belongs to You. When You place something in my hands, help me steward it. When You ask me to release something, help me trust You. When You give me more, help me become more generous—not merely more comfortable. Make my life a channel through which Your goodness can reach others. May my generosity point people back to You. I do not want to live only asking: **“What can I receive?”** Teach me to ask: **“What can I give?”** Not because I am trying to earn Your favor. But because I have already received Your grace. You gave first. You loved first. You saved me through Christ. Therefore, I want my life to reflect the generosity of my King. **My hands are Yours.** **My resources are Yours.** **My time is Yours.** **My gifts are Yours.** **My opportunities are Yours.** And whatever You place in my hands, I will seek to use it faithfully for Your glory and for the blessing of others. In Jesus' name, **Amen.**
