# Chapter 36 ## The Steward and Service > **“For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others and to give his life as a ransom for many.”** > — Mark 10:45 A faithful steward does not ask only, **“What has the King given me?”** A faithful steward also asks: **“Who can I serve with what the King has placed in my hands?”** This question changes the way we see everything. Our gifts are not merely for our advancement. Our position is not merely for our benefit. Our influence is not merely for our recognition. Our resources are not merely for our comfort. Everything entrusted to us can become an instrument of service. The King does not entrust something to a steward merely so the steward can possess it. He entrusts it with purpose. And one of the clearest purposes of stewardship is **service**. --- ## Stewardship Is Not About Being Served The world often measures success by how many people serve us. The kingdom measures greatness differently. Jesus said: > **“Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”** > — Matthew 20:26 Jesus completely reversed the world's understanding of greatness. In the kingdom, leadership is not primarily about position. It is about responsibility. Authority is not primarily about being above people. It is about being entrusted with the opportunity to help people. A title may place someone in front, but a servant's heart causes that person to bend down and help. Jesus had every right to be served. He was the Son of God. Yet He served. He touched the untouchable. He welcomed the overlooked. He fed the hungry. He taught the confused. He strengthened the weak. He washed His disciples' feet. And ultimately, He gave His life. If the King Himself chose the path of service, then no faithful steward should consider service beneath them. --- ## Jesus Showed Us What Service Looks Like One of the most powerful pictures of kingdom service happened when Jesus washed His disciples' feet. John 13 shows us something remarkable. Jesus knew who He was. He knew where He came from. He knew where He was going. And knowing His identity did not make Him unwilling to serve. It made Him willing to serve. This is important because sometimes people think that serving others means becoming less important. Jesus demonstrated the opposite. **True security in identity produces humility.** When we know that our worth comes from the King, we no longer need to use position to prove our importance. We can serve without feeling diminished. We can help without needing applause. We can bend down without believing that we have become smaller. The King does not lose His authority when He serves. Neither does a faithful steward lose his dignity by serving others. --- ## The Steward Serves With What He Has Been Given Peter wrote: > **“God has given each of you a gift from his great variety of spiritual gifts. Use them well to serve one another.”** > — 1 Peter 4:10 Notice the connection: **Gift → Stewardship → Service.** God gives. We receive. We steward. We serve. Your ability may be different from mine. Your opportunities may be different from mine. Your resources may be different from mine. Your personality may be different from mine. But the question remains the same: **What am I doing with what the King gave me?** If you can teach, teach. If you can encourage, encourage. If you can organize, organize. If you can lead, lead. If you can create, create. If you can build, build. If you can provide, provide. If you can listen, listen. If you can pray, pray. If you can open a door for someone, open it. If you can carry someone else's burden for a moment, carry it. The value of a gift is not only found in possessing it. Its kingdom value is revealed when it becomes useful in the hands of love. --- ## Service Begins Where God Has Already Placed You Sometimes we imagine that serving God requires a large platform. It does not. Service can begin in the place where you already are. At home. At work. In business. In church. In your neighborhood. Among friends. With family. Among people who can never repay you. A parent serves by raising a child faithfully. A worker serves by doing honest work. A business owner serves by providing something valuable with integrity. A leader serves by protecting and developing those under his care. A friend serves by being present when someone is struggling. A believer serves by encouraging someone who is losing hope. A mature steward understands that **ordinary places can become kingdom assignments.** You do not need a microphone to serve. You do not need a title. You do not need a crowd. You only need faithfulness with the place and people the King has entrusted to you. --- ## Service Without Recognition One of the greatest tests of service is what happens when nobody notices. It is easy to serve when people applaud. It is harder to serve when nobody says thank you. But the King sees what people do not see. Jesus warned His followers not to make their acts of righteousness into performances for human recognition. This means we should not measure the value of service by the size of the audience. Some of the most important acts of service happen in hidden places. A prayer nobody hears. A sacrifice nobody knows about. A task quietly completed. A person patiently helped. A burden quietly carried. A difficult person treated with grace. A responsibility fulfilled when nobody would have known if it had been neglected. The faithful steward learns to say: **“If the King sees it, that is enough.”** --- ## Service Is Not People-Pleasing There is an important distinction here. Serving others does not mean trying to make everyone happy. A servant of the King is not controlled by the approval of people. Sometimes faithful service requires saying **yes**. Sometimes faithful service requires saying **no**. Sometimes love means helping. Sometimes love means allowing someone to take responsibility for their own choices. Sometimes serving someone means giving them what they need. Sometimes serving them means refusing to give them what would enable harmful behavior. A steward must therefore combine **love with wisdom**. We are not called to become slaves to people's expectations. We are called to remain faithful to the King. Our first loyalty is to Him. When service becomes people-pleasing, our motivation can quietly shift from: **“How can I honor the King?”** to: **“How can I make people approve of me?”** Those are not the same thing. The faithful steward serves from obedience, not from the hunger for approval. --- ## Leadership Is Stewardship Through Service Leadership carries authority. But authority without humility can become dangerous. The King never gives authority so that a steward can treat people as personal possessions. Authority is entrusted for responsibility. A leader should therefore ask: **“Who has the King placed under my care?”** A manager has people entrusted to him. A parent has children entrusted to him. A pastor has people entrusted to his care. A business owner has employees, customers, suppliers, and resources entrusted to him. A ministry leader has people and responsibilities entrusted to him. A person with influence has an audience entrusted to him. The question is not simply: **“How many people are under me?”** The better question is: **“How faithfully am I caring for the people entrusted to me?”** Kingdom leadership does not ask, *“How can they serve me?”* It asks: **“How can I help them fulfill what God has placed in them?”** --- ## The Servant Does Not Need to Control Everything Service also requires humility about our limitations. A steward cannot do everything. There are times when the most faithful thing is to delegate. There are times when someone else is more gifted. There are times when another person should lead. There are times when we need to step back so someone else can grow. A faithful steward does not need to control every outcome. Why? Because the kingdom belongs to the King. We are servants, not saviors. We are stewards, not owners. We are participants in God's work, not the source of everything He is doing. This frees us to serve faithfully without carrying responsibilities that God never assigned to us. --- ## Service Requires Excellence Serving does not mean doing things carelessly. Paul instructed believers: > **“Whatever you do, work at it with all your heart, as working for the Lord.”** > — Colossians 3:23 If we serve people while remembering that ultimately we are serving the King, excellence becomes an act of worship. A steward should not say: **“It is only service, so anything is good enough.”** Instead: **“Because this belongs to the King, I will do it faithfully.”** Excellence does not mean perfection. It means giving sincere effort, paying attention, honoring commitments, correcting mistakes, and refusing unnecessary negligence. The King deserves faithful service. And the people we serve deserve to be treated with dignity. --- ## Service Can Be Inconvenient Real service is not always convenient. Sometimes it interrupts our plans. Sometimes it requires patience. Sometimes it costs time. Sometimes it requires using our strength when we are tired. Sometimes it requires forgiving. Sometimes it means helping someone who cannot repay us. Sometimes it means doing something that will never advance our own reputation. This is where service becomes more than an idea. It becomes a test of the heart. A steward may say: **“I will serve when it is convenient.”** But kingdom service asks something deeper: **“Will I remain faithful when serving costs me something?”** Chapter 27 taught us about faithfulness when obedience costs something. Service brings that same principle into our relationships with people. The King may sometimes ask us to serve beyond convenience. And when He does, our response reveals whether we are truly serving Him or merely serving when it benefits us. --- ## Service and Humility Philippians 2 points us toward the attitude of Christ. Jesus did not use His position as an excuse to exalt Himself. He humbled Himself and became obedient. This gives us a powerful kingdom pattern: **Position should produce responsibility, not pride.** The greater the influence, the greater the opportunity to serve. The greater the resources, the greater the opportunity to bless. The greater the authority, the greater the responsibility to protect. The greater the platform, the greater the responsibility to use it well. This connects directly with the principle we have carried throughout this book: **Bigger power means bigger responsibility.** The King does not increase our influence merely to make our name bigger. He may increase our influence so that more people can be helped through what He has entrusted to us. --- ## Serving the Next Generation A faithful steward does not only serve the people of today. He also prepares the people of tomorrow. Parents teach children. Mentors develop younger people. Leaders raise new leaders. Experienced workers teach those who are beginning. Older believers encourage younger believers. This is stewardship beyond the present moment. If everything we know dies with us, then we have not fully considered what the King may have wanted us to pass on. Our knowledge can become someone else's foundation. Our experience can become someone else's preparation. Our testimony can become someone else's encouragement. Our mistakes, when honestly learned from, can become warnings for those coming after us. A faithful steward asks: **“Who will be stronger because I served them faithfully?”** That is part of legacy. --- ## The Joy of Being Useful to the King There is a beautiful joy in realizing that God can use our ordinary lives. You may not stand before thousands. You may never have a famous name. You may never see the full effect of your service. But if the King has used your life to strengthen one person, provide for one family, encourage one discouraged heart, teach one child, restore one relationship, or open one door, your service has meaning. The goal is not to become impressive. The goal is to become **faithful and useful in the King's hands.** A vessel does not receive glory because it is beautiful by itself. Its value is revealed when it faithfully carries what the Master intends to pour through it. That is the heart of kingdom service. --- ## When Service Becomes Worship Eventually, the steward begins to understand something deeper. Service is not merely something we do **for God**. Service can become something we do **as worship to God**. When we clean with integrity, work honestly, care for people, lead humbly, teach faithfully, provide responsibly, encourage sincerely, and serve without needing applause, we are acknowledging: **“King, everything I have came from You, and I want everything I do to honor You.”** This is the heart of stewardship. The King owns the gift. The King owns the opportunity. The King owns the influence. The King owns the resources. And the steward says: **“Use what You entrusted to me for Your purposes.”** --- # Questions for the Steward Take time to ask yourself honestly: 1. **What has the King placed in my hands that could become a blessing to others?** 2. **Am I using my gifts mainly for myself, or also to serve others?** 3. **How do I respond when my service is not recognized?** 4. **Am I serving from obedience to God or from a desire for people's approval?** 5. **How do I use my position and influence to help those entrusted to me?** 6. **Is there someone God may be asking me to serve in this season?** 7. **Am I willing to serve when it becomes inconvenient?** 8. **Who am I preparing or helping for the generation that comes after me?** Then ask the deepest question: **“King, how do You want me to use what You have placed in my hands to serve You and others?”** --- # Declaration of the Faithful Steward **I am a steward of the King.** What I have is not ultimately mine. My gifts belong to Him. My resources belong to Him. My opportunities belong to Him. My influence belongs to Him. My position belongs to Him. Therefore, I will not live merely to be served. I will serve. I will use what God has entrusted to me to bless others, strengthen others, encourage others, and help others fulfill their God-given purpose. I will serve without needing applause. I will serve with wisdom. I will serve with excellence. I will serve with humility. I will serve with boundaries and discernment. I will not be controlled by people's approval. I will remain faithful to the King. When service is easy, I will serve. When service is inconvenient, I will remain faithful. When nobody notices, I will remember that the King sees. When I am given greater authority, I will accept greater responsibility. When I am given greater influence, I will use it for greater service. I will never forget that I am a steward, not an owner. My life is entrusted to me for the King's purposes. **I am here to serve the King by faithfully serving others.** And when the King looks upon my stewardship, may He find a servant who was willing to use everything entrusted to him for His glory. --- # Prayer of the Steward **King and Master,** Thank You for everything You have entrusted to me. Thank You for my gifts, abilities, opportunities, resources, relationships, responsibilities, and influence. Forgive me for the times I have wanted to be served more than I wanted to serve. Teach me to have the heart of Christ. Help me to recognize the people You have placed around me. Show me how to use what You have given me to bless them. Give me wisdom so that I will know when to help, when to lead, when to encourage, when to teach, when to give, and when to step back. Protect me from pride. Protect me from people-pleasing. Protect me from seeking recognition. Teach me to serve even when nobody applauds. Teach me to serve with excellence without becoming controlled by perfection. Teach me to serve with love without abandoning the responsibilities You have given me. May my position become an opportunity to serve. May my resources become an opportunity to bless. May my influence become an opportunity to strengthen others. May my gifts become instruments in Your hands. And may my entire life become an offering of faithful service to You. I do not want to build my own kingdom. I want to be faithful in Yours. **Everything I have is from You. Everything I steward belongs to You. And everything I do, I want to do for You.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.** --- ## A Steward's Final Thought The world asks: **“How many people serve you?”** The King asks: **“How faithfully did you serve with what I entrusted to you?”** That is the question of the steward. For the faithful steward understands that greatness in the kingdom is not measured merely by how high we rise, how much we possess, or how many people know our name. It is measured by faithfulness. And sometimes the greatest expression of faithfulness is simply this: **To take what the King placed in our hands and use it to serve someone else.** Because the King we serve first chose to serve. And if we belong to Him, then our stewardship should carry the same heart. **Received from the King. Faithfully stewarded. Freely used to serve. Returned for His glory.**
