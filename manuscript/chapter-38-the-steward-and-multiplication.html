# Chapter 38 ## The Steward and Multiplication > **“Well done, my good and faithful servant. You have been faithful in handling this small amount, so now I will give you many more responsibilities. Let’s celebrate together!”** > — Matthew 25:21 A steward does not merely receive. A steward does not merely protect. A steward does not merely preserve. A faithful steward also asks: **“How can what the King entrusted to me become more useful for His purposes?”** This is the heart of multiplication. Multiplication is not simply about making something bigger. It is about **developing what has been entrusted so that it can accomplish more of what the King intended.** A talent can be developed. A skill can be sharpened. A business can become more useful. A ministry can reach more people. A resource can serve more needs. A lesson learned can be passed to another generation. A faithful steward does not bury what the King has entrusted. He seeks to make it fruitful. --- ## The Parable of the Talents Jesus told the story of a master who entrusted different amounts of money to three servants before leaving on a journey. Two servants put what they received to work. One servant buried what he had received. When the master returned, he asked each servant to give an account. The first two had increased what was entrusted to them. The master responded with the same essential commendation: **“Well done, my good and faithful servant.”** The issue was not that both servants produced exactly the same amount. They did not. They had received different amounts. What mattered was what they did with what had been entrusted to them. This is crucial for stewardship. **The King does not compare your assignment with someone else's assignment.** He looks at your faithfulness with what He placed in your hands. --- ## Multiplication Is Not Comparison One of the easiest ways to misunderstand multiplication is to compare our results with someone else's. One person may have one talent. Another may have five. Another may have ten. One business may be small. Another may become large. One ministry may reach hundreds. Another may faithfully serve a few. One person may have a public platform. Another may serve almost entirely in hidden places. The kingdom does not require identical assignments. Faithfulness is personal. Your responsibility is not to multiply what God gave someone else. Your responsibility is to steward what **He** gave you. Therefore, multiplication should never become an excuse for comparison. Instead of asking: **“Why don't I have what they have?”** ask: **“What can I faithfully develop with what God has given me?”** --- ## The King Expects Faithfulness With What He Entrusts The parable teaches us something uncomfortable but important. The master expected his servants to do something with what they received. The third servant did not lose the money. He preserved it. He returned it. But preservation alone was not considered faithful stewardship. Why? Because the master had entrusted it for a purpose. This gives us an important principle: **There are times when preserving is wise, but there are also times when failing to develop what God gave us becomes another form of unfaithfulness.** A gift that is never developed remains limited. A skill that is never practiced becomes unused potential. Knowledge that is never shared benefits only the person who possesses it. An opportunity that is never acted upon may disappear. A resource that is never deployed cannot fulfill its intended purpose. The steward must therefore learn the difference between **careful preservation** and **fearful burial**. --- ## The Danger of Burying What God Gave The third servant buried his master's money. His action was driven by fear. Fear can cause a steward to bury many things. Fear of failure. Fear of criticism. Fear of rejection. Fear of making a mistake. Fear of losing what we have. Fear of not being good enough. Fear of what people will say. Fear can make a person protect a gift so carefully that the gift never gets used. But a gift is not given merely to be admired. It is given to be used. Sometimes the greatest risk to stewardship is not reckless behavior. Sometimes it is **doing nothing because we are afraid of doing something wrong.** Wisdom does not mean avoiding every risk. Wisdom means taking faithful, prayerful, responsible action. --- ## Multiplication Begins With Development Before something multiplies externally, it often develops internally. A person may have natural ability, but ability requires training. A business may have potential, but potential requires systems. A ministry may have vision, but vision requires preparation. A leader may have influence, but influence requires character. A writer may have ideas, but ideas require discipline. A young person may have talent, but talent requires mentorship. God may give the seed. The steward must cultivate it. This is why discipline, wisdom, responsibility, integrity, contentment, generosity, service, and endurance all matter. The previous chapters have been preparing us for this idea. **Multiplication is not an isolated principle.** It is the fruit of faithful stewardship applied consistently. --- ## Multiplication Requires Faithfulness Before Expansion Many people want increase before they have learned stewardship. They want more money before learning how to manage what they have. They want greater influence before learning how to handle small responsibilities. They want a larger business before learning how to serve customers well. They want leadership before learning humility. They want a platform before developing character. But the kingdom often works differently. Jesus said: > **“If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”** > — Luke 16:10 Small faithfulness becomes preparation for greater responsibility. The question is not merely: **“How much more could God give me?”** It is: **“How faithfully am I handling what He has already given me?”** --- ## Multiplication Can Happen Through People Multiplication is not always about producing more possessions. Sometimes the greatest multiplication happens through people. A teacher teaches another teacher. A leader develops another leader. A parent teaches a child. A mentor trains a younger person. A business owner develops employees. A mature believer disciples someone who is just beginning. One person's faithful investment can continue through another person's life. This is multiplication with generational impact. Paul told Timothy to entrust what he had learned to faithful people who would be able to teach others. That is multiplication. One life strengthens another. That life strengthens another. The investment continues. A faithful steward therefore asks: **“What can I teach that will continue producing fruit after I am gone?”** --- ## Multiplication Through Knowledge Knowledge is one of the easiest things to keep for ourselves. We learn something valuable and keep it inside. But knowledge becomes more powerful when it is shared wisely. What has God taught you through your experience? What lessons have you learned through failure? What wisdom have you gained through difficult seasons? What skills have you developed through years of practice? Those lessons can become seeds in another person's life. Sharing wisdom does not mean pretending to know everything. It means recognizing that what we have learned can become useful to someone else. A steward who multiplies knowledge does not create dependency. He helps others become capable. --- ## Multiplication Through Resources Money and material resources can also be multiplied through wise stewardship. This does not mean that every investment will produce financial profit. Nor does it mean that greater wealth automatically means greater faithfulness. The kingdom question is deeper: **“How can this resource accomplish more good?”** A business can create employment. A tool can improve someone's work. A house can become a place of hospitality. A vehicle can help serve others. Money can provide for family, support ministry, meet needs, or create opportunities. Technology can help communicate truth. A property can become useful for a community. Resources become more meaningful when they are intentionally directed toward purpose. The steward does not worship the resource. The steward deploys the resource. --- ## Multiplication Through Business Business can be stewardship. A business is not merely a machine for generating personal income. It can become a platform for service. It can provide useful products. It can solve real problems. It can provide dignified employment. It can create opportunities for others. It can demonstrate integrity. It can contribute to the community. But multiplication in business should never become an excuse for compromising character. Growth without integrity is not kingdom multiplication. More customers cannot compensate for dishonest practices. More revenue cannot justify exploitation. More influence cannot excuse arrogance. The faithful steward seeks **fruitfulness without abandoning faithfulness**. --- ## Multiplication Through Influence Every person has some degree of influence. The question is how we use it. Influence can be used to draw attention toward ourselves. Or it can be used to strengthen others. A parent influences a child. A teacher influences students. A manager influences employees. A friend influences friends. A creator influences an audience. A leader influences a community. Influence is therefore another entrusted resource. The faithful steward asks: **“After people encounter my influence, are they stronger, wiser, more encouraged, and more prepared to do what is right?”** That is a different goal from simply becoming popular. Popularity measures attention. Faithful influence seeks fruit. --- ## Multiplication Requires Patience Growth does not always happen quickly. Seeds require time. Children require years of development. Businesses require seasons of learning. Skills require practice. Relationships require investment. Leaders require formation. Therefore, multiplication must be connected with endurance. Chapter 37 taught us to remain faithful over time. Now we see why endurance matters. Sometimes multiplication is invisible before it becomes visible. A teacher may not immediately see the impact of a lesson. A parent may not immediately see the fruit of instruction. A mentor may not immediately see the result of investment. A business owner may spend years building systems before growth becomes visible. The steward must therefore resist the temptation to measure everything by immediate results. Some fruit takes time. --- ## Multiplication Is Not Always Bigger This is an important correction. Multiplication should not be reduced to numerical growth. Bigger is not automatically better. More is not automatically more faithful. A larger platform can create greater responsibility. A larger business can create greater complexity. More money can create greater accountability. More influence can create greater temptation. Therefore, the goal is not simply **expansion**. The goal is **fruitfulness according to the King's purpose.** Sometimes multiplication means more. Sometimes it means deeper. Sometimes it means stronger. Sometimes it means reaching a different group. Sometimes it means preparing others. Sometimes it means improving quality rather than increasing quantity. The faithful steward asks the King what kind of fruitfulness He desires. --- ## Multiplication Requires Systems As something grows, stewardship becomes more complex. A small responsibility may be managed personally. A larger responsibility may require systems. This is true in families, businesses, ministries, organizations, and communities. Systems can help protect what has been entrusted. Good systems can create consistency. Clear processes can reduce confusion. Training can prepare people. Documentation can preserve knowledge. Delegation can distribute responsibility. Accountability can protect integrity. The steward who wants to multiply must learn that **growth without structure can eventually become disorder.** Faithful multiplication therefore includes preparation for increase. Do not only pray for greater responsibility. Prepare for it. --- ## Multiplication Through Delegation Moses learned that he could not carry every responsibility alone. Jethro advised him to appoint capable leaders to help carry the burden. This was not weakness. It was wise stewardship. A steward who insists on doing everything personally can become a bottleneck. Healthy delegation allows other people to grow. It also recognizes that the King's work is larger than one person's capacity. Delegation requires trust. It requires clear expectations. It requires accountability. It requires allowing others to learn. Sometimes people will make mistakes while learning. That does not mean delegation has failed. It means development is happening. A faithful leader does not merely accomplish tasks. He develops people who can faithfully carry responsibility. --- ## Multiplication and Character There is a danger in multiplying results faster than character. A person's influence can grow faster than wisdom. A business can grow faster than systems. A ministry can grow faster than leadership capacity. Money can increase faster than maturity. When that happens, growth itself can become dangerous. Therefore, character must grow alongside capacity. Integrity must grow with influence. Wisdom must grow with responsibility. Humility must grow with recognition. Generosity must grow with resources. Service must grow with authority. The bigger the stewardship, the greater the need for character. **Multiplication without maturity can become destruction.** The King is not merely interested in how much grows. He cares about what kind of steward is carrying the growth. --- ## Multiplication and the Next Generation Perhaps one of the greatest forms of multiplication is what continues after us. A faithful steward should think beyond his own lifetime. What values will remain? What wisdom will be passed on? What systems will continue serving people? What businesses will continue operating with integrity? What children will carry the lessons forward? What leaders will rise because someone invested in them? What seeds will continue producing fruit? Legacy is not simply what people remember about us. Legacy is also what continues because we faithfully invested while we were here. A steward should therefore think: **“I am not only managing something for today. I may be preparing something for tomorrow.”** --- ## Multiplication and the Gospel Ultimately, kingdom multiplication points beyond material success. The greatest multiplication is the spread of God's truth and the transformation of lives. Jesus commanded His followers to make disciples. The Gospel moves from one person to another. One testimony encourages another. One disciple becomes someone who helps another disciple. One faithful servant can influence generations. This is why multiplication should never become merely a strategy for personal advancement. The greatest question remains: **“Does this help fulfill the King's purposes?”** If increase brings more people toward truth, service, righteousness, and love, it can become a powerful stewardship opportunity. But if increase only makes the steward more proud, distracted, greedy, or self-centered, then the multiplication has lost its kingdom purpose. --- ## The Steward's Responsibility After Increase What happens when multiplication actually occurs? The responsibility becomes greater. More resources require more accountability. More influence requires more humility. More people require more care. More opportunities require more wisdom. More income requires more responsible management. More authority requires more servant leadership. Increase is not permission to relax stewardship. **Increase increases responsibility.** This returns us to one of the central principles of this book: **Bigger power means bigger responsibility.** If the King expands the steward's assignment, the steward must expand his faithfulness as well. --- ## The Goal Is the Master's Joy At the end of the parable, the faithful servants were invited into the master's joy. This is important. The ultimate reward was not merely the multiplied amount. It was relationship with the master. The steward's greatest reward is not simply: **“I built something big.”** It is: **“I was faithful to the One who trusted me.”** That changes our motivation. We do not multiply merely to prove ourselves. We do not grow merely to impress others. We do not develop our gifts merely to become famous. We do not pursue increase merely to possess more. We multiply because the King entrusted something to us and we want to return it having served His purposes faithfully. --- # Questions for the Steward Take time to ask yourself: 1. **What has the King entrusted to me that needs further development?** 2. **Am I faithfully using my gifts, or have I buried them because of fear?** 3. **What skill should I develop in this season?** 4. **Who could I teach, mentor, or prepare?** 5. **How can my resources become more useful for God's purposes?** 6. **Am I pursuing growth for the King's purposes or merely for personal recognition?** 7. **Is my character growing alongside my influence and responsibility?** 8. **What can I build, teach, develop, or invest in that could continue beyond me?** And ask: **“King, what have You placed in my hands that You now want me to develop and multiply?”** --- # Declaration of the Multiplying Steward **I am a steward of what belongs to the King.** I will not bury what He has entrusted to me because of fear. I will develop my gifts. I will sharpen my skills. I will grow in wisdom. I will strengthen my character. I will use my resources intentionally. I will invest in people. I will teach what I have learned. I will develop those who come after me. I will use influence to strengthen rather than control. I will pursue growth without sacrificing integrity. I will pursue excellence without worshiping success. I will welcome increase with greater responsibility. I will not compare my assignment with another person's assignment. I will faithfully multiply what the King has placed in my hands. If He gives me little, I will be faithful with little. If He gives me much, I will be faithful with much. If He gives me influence, I will use it to serve. If He gives me resources, I will use them wisely. If He gives me knowledge, I will share it. If He gives me opportunity, I will act with courage and wisdom. If He gives me people to lead, I will serve them faithfully. My goal is not simply to become bigger. My goal is to become more fruitful for the King. I will not bury my calling. I will not waste my opportunities. I will not hide my gifts. I will not allow fear to become my master. **I will faithfully develop, deploy, and multiply what the King has entrusted to me for His glory.** And when the King asks what I did with what He placed in my hands, may I be able to answer with a faithful heart: **“King, I used what You gave me for Your purposes.”** --- # Prayer of the Multiplying Steward **Faithful King,** Everything I have came from You. Every gift. Every ability. Every opportunity. Every resource. Every relationship. Every lesson. Every influence. Every responsibility. Thank You for trusting me with them. Forgive me for the times I have buried what You gave me because of fear, insecurity, comparison, or comfort. Give me courage to develop what You have placed inside me. Give me discipline to sharpen my abilities. Give me wisdom to know where to invest. Give me humility to learn from others. Give me patience to allow growth to take place. Help me recognize that increase is not merely a blessing. It is also a greater responsibility. If You increase my influence, increase my humility. If You increase my resources, increase my generosity. If You increase my authority, increase my servant's heart. If You increase my opportunities, increase my wisdom. If You increase my responsibility, increase my faithfulness. Teach me to multiply without becoming proud. Teach me to grow without losing my integrity. Teach me to build without forgetting the Builder. Teach me to develop others instead of simply developing myself. And when something grows because of what You entrusted to me, help me remember that the glory belongs to You. I do not want to build a name for myself. I want to build something that serves Your purposes. May everything that grows through my stewardship point back to the King. **You gave the seed. You gave the opportunity. You gave the ability. And I want to be faithful with what You placed in my hands.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.** --- ## A Steward's Final Thought The faithful steward does not ask only: **“What did I receive?”** He asks: **“What did I do with what I received?”** The King does not require every steward to produce the same result. He asks each one to be faithful with what was entrusted. Therefore, do not compare your five with someone else's ten. Do not despise your one because someone else received more. Do not bury your gift because you are afraid it is too small. Start with what is in your hands. Develop it. Use it. Serve with it. Invest it. Teach others. Build wisely. And when the King gives increase, receive it with humility and greater responsibility. Because multiplication is not ultimately about making the steward bigger. It is about making the **King's entrusted resources more fruitful for the King's purposes.** The seed is entrusted. The steward cultivates. The King gives the growth. And the fruit returns to His glory. **Received from the King. Faithfully developed. Wisely multiplied. Returned for His glory.**
