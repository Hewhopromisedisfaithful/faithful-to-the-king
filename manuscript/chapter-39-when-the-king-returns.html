# Chapter 39 ## When the King Returns > **“So you, too, must keep watch! For you don’t know what day your Lord is coming.”** > — Matthew 24:42 Every steward lives with a future appointment. There will come a day when the King returns. And when He returns, stewardship will no longer be theoretical. The questions we have carried throughout this book will become personal: **What did I do with what He entrusted to me?** How did I use my time? How did I handle my resources? How did I treat people? What did I do with my gifts? How did I use my influence? Was I faithful when nobody was watching? Did I serve? Did I endure? Did I multiply what was entrusted? The faithful steward does not live only for today. He lives with eternity in view. --- # The Steward Will Give an Account Paul wrote: > **“Yes, each of us will give a personal account to God.”** > — Romans 14:12 This is one of the most serious truths of stewardship. We may be accountable to parents. We may be accountable to employers. We may be accountable to leaders. We may be accountable to governments. We may be accountable to customers, families, churches, and communities. But ultimately, every steward is accountable to the King. People may not know everything we have done. People may misunderstand our motives. People may see only the public part of our lives. But the King sees completely. He sees the hidden places. He sees the decisions nobody witnessed. He sees the sacrifices nobody recognized. He sees the opportunities we used. He sees the opportunities we neglected. He knows what He entrusted. And He knows how we responded. --- # The Return of the King Changes the Way We Live If the King is returning, then today matters. The return of the King is not merely a future event to think about. It changes how we live in the present. A steward who knows the master may return does not treat the assignment casually. Jesus repeatedly taught His disciples to remain watchful. Watchfulness is not panic. It is preparedness. A prepared steward does not spend every moment staring at the door. He continues doing what the master assigned. He works. He serves. He cares for others. He manages what was entrusted. He remains faithful. That is what biblical readiness looks like. **Readiness is faithfulness while waiting.** --- # The Difference Between Waiting and Wasting There is a difference between waiting for the King and wasting the time before His return. Waiting says: **“The King is coming, so I will remain faithful.”** Wasting says: **“The King has not returned yet, so what I do does not matter.”** But everything we do with what God has entrusted matters. Our days matter. Our choices matter. Our relationships matter. Our character matters. Our work matters. Our service matters. Our generosity matters. Our obedience matters. The present season is not meaningless simply because eternity is coming. It is the very season in which stewardship is being demonstrated. --- # The Faithful Servant Keeps Working Jesus told a parable about servants waiting for their master to return from a wedding feast. The servants were expected to be ready when he came. The message was clear: **Do not stop being faithful simply because the master is not visibly present.** This is one of the greatest tests of stewardship. What do we do when the King is not visibly standing in front of us? Do we still obey? Do we still serve? Do we still tell the truth? Do we still manage money responsibly? Do we still protect our integrity? Do we still forgive? Do we still work with excellence? Do we still honor God when nobody is watching? The absence of visible supervision reveals something about the heart. The faithful steward understands: **The King may not be visibly present, but He is never unaware.** --- # Faithfulness Is More Important Than Appearance A steward can create an impressive appearance while secretly neglecting the assignment. A person can look successful while being irresponsible. A person can appear generous while seeking recognition. A person can appear spiritual while ignoring obedience. A leader can appear powerful while failing to serve. A business can appear successful while compromising integrity. But the King is not fooled by appearances. The King looks at faithfulness. This is why stewardship must begin in the heart. Chapter 28 taught us about the heart. Chapter 33 taught us about integrity. Now we see why those lessons matter. When the King returns, the question will not simply be: **“How impressive did your life look?”** The deeper question is: **“Were you faithful with what I entrusted to you?”** --- # The King Knows What He Entrusted The parables of stewardship show that the master knew what he had given his servants. The servants were not accountable for something they had never received. This gives us both seriousness and comfort. We are responsible for our own assignment. You do not have to carry someone else's assignment. You do not have to reproduce someone else's life. You do not have to possess someone else's gifts. You do not have to achieve someone else's results. You must faithfully steward what the King placed in your hands. The King knows the difference between one talent and five. He knows the opportunities you had. He knows the limitations you faced. He knows what resources were available. He knows what responsibilities were actually yours. Therefore, stewardship is not a competition. It is an assignment. --- # The King Will Examine Faithfulness Paul wrote: > **“Now, a person who is put in charge as a manager must be faithful.”** > — 1 Corinthians 4:2 Notice what Paul does not say. He does not say that the steward must be famous. He does not say successful according to worldly standards. He does not say wealthy. He does not say popular. He says: **Faithful.** This word should settle many of our anxieties. The goal of stewardship is not to become someone else. The goal is to become faithful with what God has given us. You may not be able to control how large the harvest becomes. But you can control whether you faithfully plant. You may not control how many people recognize your service. But you can serve faithfully. You may not control how quickly an opportunity grows. But you can prepare faithfully. You may not control another person's response. But you can obey God faithfully. Faithfulness places our focus where it belongs. --- # The King Will Also See the Hidden Some of the most beautiful stewardship may never be publicly recognized. A parent quietly raising children. A person caring for an aging family member. A worker faithfully doing honest work. A believer praying consistently for others. A person giving generously without telling anyone. A leader developing people without taking credit. A servant cleaning, organizing, preparing, and helping behind the scenes. These things may never become famous. But they are not invisible to the King. Jesus taught that the Father sees what is done in secret. This should encourage the faithful steward. You do not need to make everything visible. You do not need to announce every sacrifice. You do not need to prove your faithfulness to everyone. **The King sees.** And His seeing is enough. --- # The King Will Ask About Our Relationships Stewardship is not only about money, work, or possessions. People are also entrusted to us. How did we treat them? Did we use people? Did we manipulate them? Did we honor them? Did we forgive? Did we listen? Did we encourage? Did we protect those under our care? Did we use authority responsibly? Did we become proud when people trusted us? Did we remember that people belong to God before they ever became part of our responsibility? These questions matter because kingdom stewardship always involves people. A faithful steward understands: **People are not possessions.** They are lives entrusted to our care for a season. --- # The King Will Ask About Our Influence Influence is another form of stewardship. What did we do with the voice God gave us? Did we use it to encourage or destroy? To teach or mislead? To serve or manipulate? To point people toward God or toward ourselves? Influence can be powerful. That is why influence must be handled with humility. The greater the audience, the greater the responsibility. The more people listen, the more carefully we must speak. The more authority we have, the more carefully we must act. The King will not merely ask: **“How many people listened?”** He will care about what we did with the influence entrusted to us. --- # The King Will Ask About Our Resources Money is one of the clearest areas of stewardship. But the question is not simply: **“How much did you have?”** The question is: **“How faithfully did you manage what you had?”** Did you live responsibly? Did you provide for legitimate responsibilities? Did you practice generosity? Did you avoid dishonest gain? Did you allow money to become your master? Did you use resources to serve others? Did you manage increase wisely? Did you remain content? The King is not impressed by numbers alone. A large amount can be managed faithfully or unfaithfully. A small amount can also be managed faithfully or unfaithfully. The amount is not the whole story. **The heart and the stewardship matter.** --- # The King Will Ask About Our Time Every person receives the same basic gift of time each day. But time cannot be stored for later. Once a day passes, it cannot be recovered. This makes time one of our most valuable entrusted resources. How did we use it? Did we invest time in what mattered? Did we make room for God? Did we care for people? Did we fulfill responsibilities? Did we rest wisely? Did we spend our lives only consuming, or did we also create, serve, build, teach, and bless? The goal is not to become endlessly busy. Busyness is not the same as faithfulness. The question is whether our time was aligned with what the King entrusted us to do. --- # The King Will Ask About Our Gifts Every gift carries responsibility. If God gave you the ability to teach, what did you do with it? If He gave you the ability to create, how did you use it? If He gave you leadership, how did you lead? If He gave you compassion, whom did you encourage? If He gave you business ability, how did you use it? If He gave you wisdom, did you share it? If He gave you resources, did you steward them? A gift buried remains unused. A gift developed becomes useful. A gift surrendered to God can become a blessing to many. The King gave the gift. The steward determines whether he will faithfully use it. --- # The King Will Ask About Our Endurance Chapter 37 taught us that faithfulness must continue through time. The King sees the long journey. He sees the seasons when you wanted to quit. He sees the moments when obedience was difficult. He sees the times when you continued even without immediate results. He sees the times when you waited. He sees the times when you had to begin again. Endurance matters because faithfulness is not only measured by how we respond to one difficult moment. It is also measured by whether we continue walking with God through many seasons. The faithful steward does not need every season to be easy. He needs to remain connected to the King. --- # The King Will Ask What We Did With Opportunity Opportunity is also stewardship. Some opportunities arrive only once. Others return in different forms. A conversation. A business idea. A person needing help. A chance to learn. An invitation to serve. An opportunity to reconcile. A moment to encourage. A door to begin something new. The steward must learn to recognize opportunity. Not every opportunity is from God. That is why wisdom is necessary. But when the right opportunity comes, fear should not automatically close the door. Sometimes obedience requires courage. The question is not whether every opportunity became successful. The question is whether we faithfully responded to the opportunities God entrusted to us. --- # What About the Things We Failed to Steward Well? This is where grace becomes important. When we look honestly at stewardship, none of us can say: **“I managed everything perfectly.”** We have all made mistakes. We have wasted time. We have missed opportunities. We have mishandled resources. We have said wrong things. We have failed people. We have neglected responsibilities. We have allowed fear to influence decisions. The answer is not denial. The answer is repentance. The Gospel does not teach us to pretend that our failures never happened. It teaches us to bring them to Christ. There is forgiveness. There is restoration. There is growth. There is another opportunity to become faithful. The King does not call us to perfection before we can serve Him. He calls us to surrender, repentance, obedience, and faithfulness. --- # Accountability Should Produce Readiness, Not Fear Thinking about accountability can be serious. But the purpose is not to make the steward live in constant terror. For those who belong to Christ, the return of the King is also a source of hope. The One who returns is not a stranger. He is our Lord. He is the One we have trusted. He is the One who called us. He is the One who gave Himself for us. Therefore, Christian readiness is not simply: **“I am afraid of being caught.”** It is: **“I am waiting for the One I belong to.”** That changes everything. We prepare because we love the King. We remain faithful because we trust the King. We serve because we belong to the King. We endure because we know the King is faithful. --- # The Steward's Greatest Hope The greatest hope of the faithful steward is not merely receiving a reward. It is hearing the Master's approval. In Matthew 25, the faithful servants hear: > **“Well done, my good and faithful servant.”** Those words reveal something beautiful. The Master noticed faithfulness. He noticed responsibility. He noticed what was done with what had been entrusted. The steward's deepest desire should not be: **“I hope everyone remembers my name.”** It should be: **“I hope the King finds me faithful.”** Human recognition fades. Earthly achievements change. Possessions remain behind. Positions eventually end. But faithfulness before the King has eternal significance. --- # When the King Returns, There Will Be No Need for Comparison Imagine standing before the King. At that moment, comparison loses its power. You will not need to explain why someone else had more. You will not need to prove why someone else's life was easier. You will not need to defend why your assignment was different. The question will be personal: **What did you do with what I entrusted to you?** This is both sobering and freeing. Sobering because our stewardship matters. Freeing because we do not need to live someone else's life. We only need to faithfully live the life the King entrusted to us. --- # Live Today With That Day in Mind The return of the King should affect today. It should affect how we handle money today. How we speak today. How we work today. How we serve today. How we treat people today. How we respond to temptation today. How we use our time today. How we handle opportunities today. How we lead today. How we rest today. How we repent today. How we forgive today. Eternity does not make today meaningless. It makes today significant. Every ordinary day becomes another opportunity to demonstrate faithfulness. --- # The Steward's Final Preparation How do we prepare for the King's return? Not by trying to predict the exact day. Jesus Himself taught His followers to remain watchful because the timing would not be known to them. We prepare by living faithfully now. Keep your heart surrendered. Keep your hands open. Keep your conscience clean. Keep your relationships honest. Keep your priorities aligned. Keep serving. Keep learning. Keep giving. Keep building. Keep forgiving. Keep enduring. Keep your eyes on Jesus. The faithful steward does not need to know the exact hour of the King's return. He only needs to be ready when the King comes. --- # Questions for the Steward Take time to ask yourself honestly: 1. **If the King asked me today what I did with what He entrusted to me, what would I say?** 2. **What area of my stewardship needs greater faithfulness?** 3. **What have I been neglecting because nobody is watching?** 4. **Have I used my resources according to the King's purposes?** 5. **How have I treated the people entrusted to me?** 6. **What have I done with my gifts and opportunities?** 7. **Is there anything I need to repent of and place back into the King's hands?** 8. **Am I living today in a way that reflects that the King is coming?** Then ask: **“King, if You returned today, what would You want me to change while I still have time?”** --- # Declaration of the Ready Steward **The King is coming, and I choose to live faithfully.** I will not live carelessly with what God has entrusted to me. I will remember that my life belongs to the King. My time belongs to Him. My resources belong to Him. My gifts belong to Him. My influence belongs to Him. My relationships belong to Him. My opportunities belong to Him. My responsibilities belong to Him. Therefore, I will steward them faithfully. I will not live only for temporary recognition. I will live with eternity in view. I will serve when nobody applauds. I will obey when nobody is watching. I will give when nobody knows. I will work with integrity when nobody checks. I will forgive when it is difficult. I will endure when the journey is long. I will develop what the King has entrusted. I will multiply what He has placed in my hands. I will repent when I fail. I will rise when I fall. I will remain watchful without living in fear. I will remain hopeful because my King is faithful. I do not know the day or hour of His return. But I know what I want to be doing when He comes: **I want to be faithful.** May my hands be found serving. May my heart be found trusting. May my life be found obedient. May my stewardship be found faithful. And when the King calls me to give an account, may I hear those words: **“Well done, good and faithful servant.”** --- # Prayer of the Ready Steward **King Jesus,** Thank You for entrusting my life to me for this season. Everything I have belongs ultimately to You. Teach me to remember that every day is an opportunity to be faithful. Forgive me for the times I have wasted what You entrusted. Forgive me for the times I allowed fear to bury my gifts. Forgive me for the times I placed temporary things above eternal priorities. Forgive me for the times I failed to love, serve, give, obey, or endure as I should have. Thank You for Your grace. Thank You that failure does not have to be the end. Teach me to repent quickly. Teach me to return to You. Teach me to steward the remainder of my days faithfully. Help me to live ready for Your return. Keep me from fear. Keep me from complacency. Keep me from pride. Keep me from comparison. Keep me from wasting the opportunities You place before me. Help me to serve faithfully. Help me to lead humbly. Help me to give generously. Help me to endure patiently. Help me to multiply wisely. And when the day comes when I stand before You, may I not be ashamed of how I handled what You placed in my hands. May I have no need to pretend. May I have no need to compare. May I simply stand before my King knowing that I gave my life to Him. And by Your grace, may I hear: **“Well done, good and faithful servant.”** Until that day, keep me faithful. Until that day, keep me watchful. Until that day, keep me close to You. **I am Your steward. You are my King. And I will remain faithful until You return.** In Jesus' name, **Amen.** --- # A Steward's Final Thought One day, the waiting will end. One day, the King will return. One day, every steward will stand before Him. On that day, the size of our platform will not be the ultimate question. The amount of applause will not be the ultimate question. The number of people who knew our name will not be the ultimate question. The question will be about faithfulness. **What did you do with what I entrusted to you?** So while we still have time: Serve. Give. Build. Teach. Develop. Forgive. Endure. Multiply. Love. Obey. Remain faithful. Not because we are trying to earn the King's love. But because we already know who the King is. He is faithful. And the steward's response to a faithful King is simple: **Faithfulness.** The King entrusted. The steward received. The steward served. The steward endured. The steward multiplied. And finally, the steward returned everything to the One who owned it all along. **The King is coming. Let Him find us faithful.**
