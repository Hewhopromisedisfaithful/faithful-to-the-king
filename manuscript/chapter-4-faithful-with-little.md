# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 4 — Faithful With Little

One of the greatest misunderstandings about stewardship is the belief that faithfulness only becomes important when we have something significant.

We may think:

**“When I have more, then I will become generous.”**

**“When I have a bigger opportunity, then I will serve.”**

**“When I have a better position, then I will lead well.”**

**“When I have more resources, then I will do something meaningful.”**

But the Kingdom of God teaches something different.

**Faithfulness begins with what is already in our hands.**

God does not ask us to be faithful with what He never entrusted to us.

But whatever He has entrusted to us—even if it seems small—matters.

---

## The Principle of Little Things

Jesus gave a remarkably simple principle:

> “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”
>
> — Luke 16:10

Notice that Jesus did not say:

**“If you have large things, be faithful.”**

He said:

**“If you are faithful in little things.”**

This means the small things are not merely preparation for something more important.

The small things are themselves opportunities for faithfulness.

A small responsibility is still a responsibility.

A small amount of money is still something to manage.

A small opportunity is still an opportunity.

A small influence is still influence.

A small assignment is still an assignment.

The size of the trust does not remove the responsibility to be faithful.

---

## The King Gives According to His Wisdom

In Matthew 25, Jesus told the Parable of the Talents.

A master was preparing to leave on a journey.

Before leaving, he entrusted his possessions to three servants.

One received five talents.

Another received two.

Another received one.

They did not receive equal amounts.

This is important.

The master did not give each servant the same assignment.

But each servant received something.

And each servant became responsible for what he had received.

The first two servants put what they received to work.

The servant with five gained five more.

The servant with two gained two more.

When the master returned, both were commended.

The master said:

> “Well done, my good and faithful servant.”
>
> — Matthew 25:21

Notice something powerful.

The servant who received five and the servant who received two were both called **faithful**.

The master did not say:

**“The servant with five is more faithful because he produced more.”**

The amount entrusted was different.

The faithfulness was what mattered.

---

## God Does Not Compare Your Assignment

This is one of the most freeing truths in stewardship.

You do not have to live someone else's assignment.

You do not have to produce what another person was entrusted to produce.

You do not have to possess another person's abilities.

You do not have to carry another person's responsibilities.

The servant with two talents did not have to produce five.

He simply had to be faithful with two.

This means comparison can become a distraction from stewardship.

If we constantly look at what someone else has, we may fail to recognize what God has already placed in our hands.

We may spend our lives saying:

**“If only I had what they have.”**

Meanwhile, the King is asking:

**“What are you doing with what I gave you?”**

---

## The Widow With Two Coins

Jesus gave another powerful picture of faithfulness through a poor widow.

She came to the Temple and gave two small coins.

Her offering was extremely small compared with what wealthy people were giving.

But Jesus saw something others might not have seen.

He said that her gift had great significance because of what it represented.

> “This poor widow has given more than all the rest of them.”
>
> — Luke 21:3

How could two small coins be considered greater than larger gifts?

Because God does not measure stewardship only by visible size.

He sees the heart.

He sees sacrifice.

He sees faithfulness.

The widow did not have much.

But she was faithful with what she had.

Her story reminds us:

**You do not need much to be faithful.**

You only need something entrusted to you.

---

## God Does Not Require What He Has Not Given

This principle is important for every steward.

God does not ask the servant with one talent to account for five talents.

He does not ask the widow to give what she does not possess.

He does not ask one person to fulfill another person's assignment.

God's expectations are connected to what He entrusts.

Paul wrote:

> “Now, a person who is put in charge as a manager must be faithful.”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 4:2

The requirement is faithfulness.

Not comparison.

Not competition.

Not appearance.

Not status.

**Faithfulness.**

This should bring peace to the steward.

You do not need to become someone else.

You need to faithfully become who God has called you to be with what He has entrusted to you.

---

## Little Things Reveal the Heart

Why does God care about little things?

Because little things reveal the condition of the heart.

How do we handle a small responsibility?

How do we treat a small amount of money?

How do we respond when nobody notices?

How do we serve when there is no recognition?

How do we behave when the assignment seems beneath our position?

These moments reveal whether we understand stewardship.

Anyone can desire a great position.

But a faithful steward learns to honor small responsibilities.

Anyone can want a large opportunity.

But a faithful steward learns to use today's opportunity well.

Anyone can desire influence.

But a faithful steward learns to serve the people already within reach.

---

## The Hidden Test

Sometimes what appears to be an ordinary responsibility is actually a test of faithfulness.

A small task may test our attitude.

A small amount of money may test our discipline.

A quiet season may test our patience.

A simple assignment may test our humility.

A limited opportunity may test our gratitude.

The King may use small things to prepare the heart for greater responsibility.

But even if greater responsibility never comes, faithfulness is still valuable.

Because our ultimate goal is not promotion.

Our ultimate goal is **faithfulness to the King.**

---

## Faithfulness Before Increase

There is a tendency to think:

**“When God gives me more, then I will prove myself.”**

But Kingdom stewardship works in the opposite direction.

We demonstrate faithfulness with what we have.

Then, if God chooses to entrust more, we receive greater responsibility.

Jesus said:

> “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”
>
> — Luke 16:10

This principle means that increase should not be our primary motivation for faithfulness.

We should not be faithful merely because we want God to give us more.

We should be faithful because **the King is worthy of our faithfulness.**

If He gives more, we will steward more.

If He gives little, we will steward little.

Either way:

**We remain faithful.**

---

## The King's Measure

Imagine two stewards standing before the King.

One managed a great estate.

Another managed a small field.

One handled great wealth.

Another handled very little.

One had wide influence.

Another served quietly with almost no recognition.

If both were faithful with what they received, the difference in size would not change the value of their faithfulness.

The King is not asking:

**“Why were you not given what I gave someone else?”**

He is asking:

**“What did you do with what I entrusted to you?”**

That is the question every steward must eventually face.

---

## Do Not Despise Small Beginnings

Scripture says:

> “Do not despise these small beginnings, for the Lord rejoices to see the work begin.”
>
> — Zechariah 4:10

A beginning can be small and still be significant.

A small business can be a beginning.

A small ministry can be a beginning.

A small act of generosity can be a beginning.

A small responsibility can be a beginning.

A small step of obedience can be a beginning.

We should not despise what is small simply because it is not yet large.

The King sees what we cannot always see.

And our responsibility is not to control the size of the outcome.

Our responsibility is to faithfully steward the assignment.

---

## When the King Gives More

If the King eventually increases what He has entrusted to us, we should remember the lesson of the small things.

More resources mean more stewardship.

More money means more responsibility.

More influence means more responsibility.

More authority means more responsibility.

More opportunities mean more responsibility.

More leadership means more responsibility.

Increase should never make us forget the foundation.

**The same faithfulness required with little is required with much.**

The size of the assignment changes.

The character of the steward should not.

---

# Faithfulness Is the Goal

At the end of our stewardship journey, perhaps the greatest question will not be:

**“How much did you have?”**

It will not be:

**“How famous were you?”**

It will not be:

**“How powerful were you?”**

It will not even be:

**“How much did you accomplish compared with others?”**

The deeper question is:

**“Were you faithful with what I entrusted to you?”**

That is why we should not despise little.

We should not be ashamed of a small beginning.

We should not become frustrated because someone else has more.

We should not wait for a greater opportunity before becoming faithful.

**Start with what is in your hands.**

Use what you have.

Serve where you are.

Honor what has been entrusted.

And trust the King with the results.

Because the faithful steward does not live for the size of the assignment.

**The faithful steward lives for the approval of the King.**

---

## Bible Foundation

The central teaching of this chapter is supported by these passages:

- **Luke 16:10** — Faithfulness in little things reveals faithfulness in greater things.
- **Matthew 25:14–30** — The Parable of the Talents shows that servants receive different amounts but are accountable for what they receive.
- **Luke 21:1–4** — The widow's small offering demonstrates that God sees beyond the size of a gift.
- **1 Corinthians 4:2** — Stewards are required to be faithful.
- **Zechariah 4:10** — God's people are warned not to despise small beginnings.
- **Luke 12:48** — Greater trust carries greater responsibility.

---

## Kingdom Principle

**God does not require what He has not entrusted to us.

But whatever He has entrusted to us, He calls us to steward faithfully.**

The assignment may be small.

The resources may be limited.

The opportunity may seem insignificant.

But faithfulness is never insignificant before the King.

If You entrust me with much, I will honor You with much.

I will not wait for greater resources before becoming faithful.

I will begin where I am, with what I have, because what is in my hands has first passed through Yours.

**I do not need another person's assignment.

I need faithfulness in my own.

Whatever You entrust to me, help me steward it faithfully for You.**
