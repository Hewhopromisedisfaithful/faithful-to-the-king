# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 6 — The Test of Stewardship

Stewardship is easy to talk about when everything is going well.

It is easy to say we trust the King when resources are sufficient, opportunities are open, and responsibilities are manageable.

But stewardship is truly revealed when our trust is tested.

What happens when resources become limited?

What happens when an opportunity disappears?

What happens when we are given responsibility but receive little recognition?

What happens when doing what is right costs us something?

These moments reveal something important:

**Faithfulness is not proven by what we say we will do. It is proven by what we do when we are tested.**

### 1. The Test Reveals What Is in the Heart

The Bible teaches that testing can reveal the quality of our faith.

> “These trials will show that your faith is genuine.”  
> — 1 Peter 1:7

A test does not necessarily mean that God has abandoned us.

Sometimes a test reveals what has already been developing inside us.

When pressure comes, patience is revealed.

When money becomes tight, contentment is revealed.

When authority is given, character is revealed.

When nobody is watching, integrity is revealed.

When success comes, humility is revealed.

The test exposes whether we truly see ourselves as stewards or whether we have slowly begun to think like owners.

The owner says:

**“This is mine, and I can do whatever I want.”**

The steward says:

**“This belongs to the King. How does He want me to handle it?”**

### 2. The Test of Little Things

Jesus taught that faithfulness is first demonstrated in small things.

> “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”  
> — Luke 16:10

The Kingdom often begins with something that looks small.

A small amount of money.

A simple responsibility.

A limited opportunity.

A few people to serve.

A talent that nobody notices.

A task that does not receive applause.

The question is not whether the assignment looks important.

The question is whether the King entrusted it.

A steward who is faithful with little is already demonstrating the character required for greater responsibility.

The King is not measuring the size of the assignment.

**He is examining the faithfulness of the steward.**

### 3. The Test of Another Person's Property

Jesus continued His teaching by saying:

> “And if you are not faithful with other people’s things, why should you be trusted with things of your own?”  
> — Luke 16:12

This is a powerful stewardship principle.

Sometimes the King allows us to manage things that are not permanently ours.

A position.

A business.

A ministry.

A responsibility.

Someone else's resources.

Someone else's property.

Someone else's trust.

These become opportunities to demonstrate character.

A steward must never confuse **access** with **ownership**.

Just because something is placed in our hands does not mean it belongs to us.

A person may be given authority without owning the people.

A manager may control resources without personally owning them.

A ministry leader may carry responsibility without possessing the ministry.

The King remains the Owner.

The steward remains accountable.

### 4. The Test of Pressure

Pressure can make people reveal what they truly value.

When resources are limited, the temptation is to become selfish.

When recognition is absent, the temptation is to become discouraged.

When others appear to receive more, the temptation is to compare.

When obedience becomes costly, the temptation is to compromise.

But Scripture calls us to remain faithful.

Paul wrote:

> “So let’s not get tired of doing what is good.”  
> — Galatians 6:9

Faithfulness sometimes means continuing to do what is right even when the immediate result is not visible.

The steward does not stop being faithful simply because the season is difficult.

Why?

Because the steward's responsibility is not based on circumstances.

**It is based on the King's trust.**

### 5. The Test of Temptation

Every trust carries the possibility of misuse.

Money can be used selfishly.

Influence can become manipulation.

Authority can become control.

Talent can become pride.

Opportunity can become selfish ambition.

Blessing can become entitlement.

This is why stewardship requires self-control.

Paul instructed Timothy:

> “Run from anything that stimulates youthful lusts. Instead, pursue righteous living, faithfulness, love, and peace.”  
> — 2 Timothy 2:22

The faithful steward does not simply ask:

**“What can I do?”**

He also asks:

**“What should I do?”**

Not everything we have the power to do is something the King wants us to do.

Kingdom stewardship requires restraint.

### 6. The Test of Delay

Sometimes the greatest test is not pressure.

It is waiting.

We may faithfully do what God has entrusted to us and still not see immediate results.

The temptation is to conclude that faithfulness is useless.

But Scripture reminds us:

> “Those who wait on the Lord will renew their strength.”  
> — Isaiah 40:31

Faithfulness does not require immediate reward.

The steward continues because the King is worthy of trust.

A farmer does not abandon the field simply because the harvest is not visible today.

In the same way, we must not abandon faithful stewardship simply because the result has not yet appeared.

There is a season for sowing.

There is a season for growing.

And there is a season for harvest.

The steward's responsibility is to remain faithful through every season.

### 7. The Test of Success

Failure can test us.

But success can test us too.

When the King gives greater resources, greater influence, or greater responsibility, we must remember where everything came from.

Deuteronomy says:

> “Remember the Lord your God. He is the one who gives you power to be successful.”  
> — Deuteronomy 8:18

Success must never cause the steward to forget the King.

Greater blessing should produce greater gratitude.

Greater influence should produce greater responsibility.

Greater resources should produce greater generosity.

Greater authority should produce greater service.

**Greater trust should produce greater faithfulness.**

The danger is not simply having more.

The danger is forgetting the Owner when we receive more.

## Kingdom Principle

**The test of stewardship is not how much we possess, but whether we remain faithful when what is entrusted to us is tested.**

Pressure tests our faithfulness.

Little things test our diligence.

Another person's property tests our integrity.

Temptation tests our self-control.

Delay tests our patience.

Success tests our humility.

Every season provides an opportunity to demonstrate that we still recognize the King as Owner.

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

**What happens to my stewardship when I am under pressure?**

Do I remain honest when resources are limited?

Do I remain faithful when nobody notices?

Do I remain obedient when obedience becomes difficult?

Do I remain humble when I succeed?

Do I continue serving when the reward is delayed?

Do I remember that what I hold still belongs to the King?

A test does not change our identity.

It reveals it.

And when the test comes, may the King find us faithful.

## Faithful Response

King, when my stewardship is tested, help me remain faithful.

When resources are limited, teach me contentment.

When responsibility becomes difficult, give me endurance.

When temptation comes, give me self-control.

When success comes, keep me humble.

When the reward is delayed, teach me to wait.

When I am entrusted with what belongs to another, help me handle it with integrity.

Remind me that everything in my hands belongs to You.

I am not the owner.

I am Your steward.

And whatever season I am in, my desire is the same:

**to remain faithful to the King.**

## Bible Foundation

- Matthew 25:14–30 — The servants are tested through what the master entrusts to them.
- Luke 16:10–12 — Faithfulness is demonstrated in little things and in handling what belongs to another.
- 1 Peter 1:6–7 — Trials test and reveal genuine faith.
- Galatians 6:9 — Believers are encouraged not to become tired of doing what is good.
- 2 Timothy 2:22 — Faithfulness includes fleeing temptation and pursuing righteousness.
- Isaiah 40:31 — Those who wait on the Lord receive renewed strength.
- Deuteronomy 8:18 — God is the source of the ability to produce wealth and success.
