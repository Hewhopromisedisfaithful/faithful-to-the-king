# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 7 — The Steward's Accountability

Every steward eventually answers to the King.

This is one of the most important truths about stewardship.

When something is entrusted to us, we are not given unlimited ownership. We are given responsibility.

And responsibility carries accountability.

Jesus illustrated this clearly in the Parable of the Talents. A master entrusted different amounts to three servants and then went away. When he returned, he called the servants to give an account of what they had done with what he had entrusted to them.

The servants were not accountable for what they were **not** given.

They were accountable for what they **were** given.

This reveals a foundational Kingdom principle:

**The King does not compare assignments. He examines faithfulness.**

### 1. Every Steward Will Give an Account

Paul wrote:

> “Yes, each of us will give a personal account to God.”  
> — Romans 14:12

There will come a time when the steward stands before the King.

The question will not simply be:

**“How much did you have?”**

The deeper question will be:

**“What did you do with what I entrusted to you?”**

The King knows what He placed in our hands.

He knows the opportunities we received.

He knows the resources available to us.

He knows the abilities He gave us.

He knows the responsibilities assigned to us.

Therefore, accountability is personal.

We cannot stand before the King and answer for someone else's assignment.

We are responsible for our own stewardship.

### 2. The King Knows What He Entrusted

In the Parable of the Talents, the master gave one servant five talents, another two, and another one.

Their assignments were different.

Their opportunities were different.

Their starting amounts were different.

But the master did not judge them by comparison with one another.

The servant who received five gained five more.

The servant who received two gained two more.

Both were commended for their faithfulness.

> “Well done, my good and faithful servant.”  
> — Matthew 25:21

This is deeply encouraging.

The King knows the difference between what He entrusted to one person and what He entrusted to another.

He does not require us to produce what He never gave us.

He asks us to be faithful with what is already in our hands.

### 3. Accountability Removes Comparison

Comparison can easily distort stewardship.

We look at another person's resources and think:

“I wish I had what they have.”

We look at another person's opportunities and think:

“Why did they receive more?”

We look at another person's influence and think:

“Why am I not there?”

But the Kingdom does not call us to manage another person's assignment.

It calls us to faithfully manage our own.

Peter once asked Jesus about another disciple:

> “Lord, what about him?”  
> — John 21:21

Jesus answered:

> “What is that to you? As for you, follow me.”  
> — John 21:22

The principle is simple.

**Do not let another person's assignment distract you from your own obedience.**

The King knows what He has entrusted to each steward.

Our responsibility is faithfulness.

### 4. Accountability Includes Motives

Stewardship is not only about visible results.

God also examines the heart.

A person may perform impressive works while having wrong motives.

Jesus warned against practicing righteousness merely to be seen by others.

> “Watch out! Don’t do your good deeds publicly, to be admired by others.”  
> — Matthew 6:1

Kingdom stewardship is therefore not a performance for people.

We do not serve merely for applause.

We do not give merely to be praised.

We do not lead merely to become important.

We do not use our gifts merely to build our own name.

We serve because the King entrusted something to us.

The question is not only:

**“What did I accomplish?”**

It is also:

**“Why did I do it?”**

### 5. Accountability Is According to What Was Entrusted

Jesus taught:

> “When someone has been given much, much will be required in return.”  
> — Luke 12:48

This principle protects us from two opposite mistakes.

The first is **entitlement**.

We may think that having more means we have greater privileges.

But in the Kingdom, greater trust brings greater responsibility.

The second is **comparison**.

We may think that because someone else has more, God expects the same from us.

But the King knows what each person has received.

The principle is not:

**“Everyone must produce the same amount.”**

The principle is:

**“Everyone must be faithful with what the King entrusted.”**

### 6. Accountability Gives Meaning to Ordinary Work

Sometimes we underestimate ordinary responsibilities.

Taking care of a family.

Doing honest work.

Managing a small amount of money.

Serving a few people.

Using a simple talent.

Showing kindness.

Keeping a promise.

Praying faithfully.

These things may seem small in the eyes of people.

But they are not small when they are part of the King's trust.

Paul wrote:

> “Whatever you do, do it all for the glory of God.”  
> — 1 Corinthians 10:31

The ordinary can become Kingdom work when it is done faithfully before the King.

A steward does not need a large platform to have a significant assignment.

Faithfulness can happen quietly.

The King sees it.

### 7. Accountability Should Change How We Live Today

Knowing that we will give an account should not make us live in fear.

It should make us live intentionally.

Every day becomes an opportunity to manage God's gifts well.

Every resource becomes a trust.

Every opportunity becomes an assignment.

Every relationship becomes a responsibility.

Every ability becomes something that can be used for God's glory.

Every decision becomes an opportunity to demonstrate faithfulness.

We do not know exactly how long our assignment will last.

But we know that the King will eventually call His stewards to account.

Therefore, today matters.

**What we do with what is in our hands today matters.**

## Kingdom Principle

**The steward is accountable to the King for how faithfully he manages what the King has entrusted.**

The King does not ask us to manage what belongs to someone else.

He does not require what He never entrusted.

He does not judge us by another person's assignment.

He asks us to faithfully manage our own trust.

Therefore:

**No comparison.**

**No entitlement.**

**No excuse.**

**No pretending.**

Just faithful stewardship before the King.

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

**If the King asked me today, “What did you do with what I entrusted to you?” what would I say?**

What have I done with my time?

What have I done with my resources?

What have I done with my abilities?

What have I done with my opportunities?

What have I done with my influence?

What have I done with the people He placed around me?

And perhaps most importantly:

**Have I managed these things for myself, or for the King?**

The answer to these questions reveals the direction of our stewardship.

## Faithful Response

King, everything I have comes from You.

One day, I will give an account of how I handled what You entrusted to me.

Help me not to compare my assignment with another person's.

Help me not to complain about what I was not given.

Help me not to become proud because I received more.

Help me not to neglect what seems small.

Teach me to faithfully manage every trust You place in my hands.

May my time honor You.

May my resources honor You.

May my abilities honor You.

May my relationships honor You.

May my influence honor You.

And when my stewardship is finally examined, may I hear the words every faithful steward longs to hear:

**“Well done, my good and faithful servant.”**

## Bible Foundation

- Matthew 25:14–30 — The servants give an account for what the master entrusted to them.
- Romans 14:12 — Each person will give a personal account to God.
- 2 Corinthians 5:10 — We will all appear before Christ and be accountable for what we have done.
- John 21:21–22 — Jesus teaches Peter not to compare his assignment with another disciple's.
- Matthew 6:1 — God cares about the motives behind our actions.
- Luke 12:48 — Greater trust carries greater responsibility.
- 1 Corinthians 10:31 — Everything we do can be done for God's glory.
- 1 Corinthians 4:2 — Stewards must be found faithful.
