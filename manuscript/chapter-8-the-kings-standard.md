# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 8 — The King's Standard

A faithful steward does not create his own standard.

The steward follows the standard of the King.

If everything belongs to the King, then the King has the right to determine how what He entrusts should be handled.

This means stewardship is not simply about being active, productive, or successful.

It is about managing what we have received according to the values and will of the One who entrusted it.

The question is not merely:

**“Did I do something with what God gave me?”**

The deeper question is:

**“Did I handle what God gave me according to His standard?”**

### 1. The King's Standard Begins With His Word

God has not left His stewards without instruction.

His Word gives us wisdom for how to live, work, lead, give, serve, and relate to others.

The psalmist said:

> “Your word is a lamp to guide my feet and a light for my path.”  
> — Psalm 119:105

A steward who ignores the King's Word is attempting to manage the King's property without consulting the King.

The Bible becomes our guide.

When we handle money, His Word gives principles.

When we handle relationships, His Word gives principles.

When we lead people, His Word gives principles.

When we use influence, His Word gives principles.

When we make decisions, His Word gives principles.

The King's standard must be greater than our personal preference.

### 2. The King's Standard Is Faithfulness

We have already seen that faithfulness is required of a steward.

Paul wrote:

> “A person who is put in charge as a manager must be faithful.”  
> — 1 Corinthians 4:2

Faithfulness means that the steward can be trusted to carry out his responsibility consistently.

It does not mean perfection.

It means remaining loyal to the King's trust.

A faithful steward may make mistakes.

A faithful steward may experience failure.

A faithful steward may need correction.

But a faithful steward does not deliberately abandon the King's instructions.

When corrected, he returns.

When he fails, he repents.

When he is weak, he seeks God's help.

Faithfulness is not the absence of weakness.

**It is continued loyalty to the King.**

### 3. The King's Standard Is Righteousness

Stewardship must never be separated from righteousness.

A person cannot claim to be a faithful steward while deliberately using God's trust for dishonest purposes.

Proverbs says:

> “The Lord detests people who use dishonest scales, but he is pleased with accurate weights.”  
> — Proverbs 11:1

God cares about how business is conducted.

He cares about honesty.

He cares about fairness.

He cares about how the powerful treat the vulnerable.

He cares about how resources are obtained and used.

Therefore, Kingdom stewardship cannot be built on deception.

The steward must be willing to lose an opportunity rather than gain something through unrighteousness.

The King is more important than the profit.

### 4. The King's Standard Is Love

Jesus taught that the greatest commandments involve loving God and loving others.

> “You must love the Lord your God with all your heart, all your soul, and all your mind.”  
> — Matthew 22:37

And:

> “Love your neighbor as yourself.”  
> — Matthew 22:39

This means our stewardship cannot become purely transactional.

People are not merely resources.

Employees are not merely labor.

Followers are not merely numbers.

Customers are not merely sources of income.

Friends are not merely connections.

Family members are not merely responsibilities.

Every person has value because every person is created by God.

Therefore, influence must be used to serve rather than exploit.

Resources should become opportunities to bless.

Leadership should become an opportunity to serve.

Knowledge should become an opportunity to help.

**The King's resources should reflect the King's heart.**

### 5. The King's Standard Is Justice

God cares about justice.

Micah gives a clear picture:

> “No, O people, the Lord has told you what is good, and this is what he requires: to do what is right, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God.”  
> — Micah 6:8

This is especially important when a steward has authority.

Power can easily create entitlement.

But Kingdom leadership is different.

Jesus taught His disciples that worldly rulers often exercise authority over people, but among His followers:

> “Whoever wants to be a leader among you must be your servant.”  
> — Matthew 20:26

Authority in the Kingdom is not permission to dominate.

It is responsibility to serve.

The greater the authority, the greater the responsibility to use it rightly.

### 6. The King's Standard Is Wisdom

Good intentions are not always enough.

A steward must learn to manage wisely.

Proverbs says:

> “Wisdom is more valuable than rubies; nothing you desire can compare with it.”  
> — Proverbs 8:11

Wisdom helps us understand how to use what has been entrusted.

Having money does not automatically mean knowing how to manage money.

Having authority does not automatically mean knowing how to lead.

Having an opportunity does not automatically mean knowing how to use it.

Having knowledge does not automatically mean knowing when to speak.

Therefore, a faithful steward should pursue wisdom.

We pray.

We study God's Word.

We seek godly counsel.

We learn from experience.

We examine our decisions.

We remain teachable.

A steward who refuses to learn may waste what the King has entrusted.

### 7. The King's Standard Is Obedience

Ultimately, stewardship comes down to obedience.

Jesus asked:

> “Why do you keep calling me ‘Lord, Lord!’ when you don’t do what I say?”  
> — Luke 6:46

Calling Jesus Lord means recognizing His authority.

And recognizing His authority should affect how we live.

The steward cannot say:

**“This belongs to the King.”**

and then manage it as though the King has no authority.

If He is King, His Word matters.

His values matter.

His commands matter.

His purposes matter.

The faithful steward therefore learns to ask:

**“Lord, what do You want me to do with what You have entrusted to me?”**

That question changes everything.

## Kingdom Principle

**The King's standard—not the steward's preference—must govern how the steward manages what has been entrusted.**

The King's Word gives direction.

The King's character gives the pattern.

The King's commands give boundaries.

The King's heart gives the purpose.

Therefore, faithful stewardship means managing God's resources according to God's ways.

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

**Am I managing what God entrusted according to His standard or according to my own?**

Do I seek His Word before making important decisions?

Am I honest when honesty costs me something?

Do I treat people as valuable or merely useful?

Do I use authority to serve or to control?

Do I pursue wisdom before making decisions?

Am I willing to obey when God's standard conflicts with my preference?

These questions help us see whether we are truly functioning as stewards.

## Faithful Response

King, You are the Owner, and I am Your steward.

Teach me to manage everything You entrust to me according to Your standard.

Let Your Word guide my decisions.

Let righteousness govern my actions.

Let love govern my relationships.

Let justice govern my leadership.

Let wisdom govern my choices.

Let obedience govern my life.

When my preference conflicts with Your will, help me choose Your way.

When I do not know what to do, teach me to seek Your wisdom.

And when You place something in my hands, remind me:

**It belongs to You, and it must be handled in a way that honors You.**

I do not want merely to be a productive steward.

I want to be a **faithful steward according to the King's standard.**

## Bible Foundation

- Psalm 119:105 — God's Word guides the believer's path.
- 1 Corinthians 4:2 — Stewards are required to be faithful.
- Proverbs 11:1 — God values honesty and rejects dishonest practices.
- Matthew 22:37–39 — Love for God and others is central to God's commands.
- Micah 6:8 — God calls His people to righteousness, mercy, and humility.
- Matthew 20:25–28 — Kingdom leadership is expressed through service.
- Proverbs 8:11 — Wisdom is more valuable than material wealth.
- Luke 6:46 — Calling Jesus Lord must be accompanied by obedience.
