# PART II — THE STEWARD

## Chapter 9 — The Steward's Mindset

Stewardship begins with a change in perspective.

Before our hands can manage what the King entrusts, our minds must understand who we are.

We are not owners.

**We are stewards.**

An owner asks:

**“What do I want to do with what I have?”**

A steward asks:

**“What does the King want me to do with what He has entrusted?”**

That difference may seem small, but it changes everything.

It changes how we view money.

It changes how we view time.

It changes how we view our abilities.

It changes how we view opportunities.

It changes how we view authority.

It changes how we view relationships.

Most importantly, it changes how we view ourselves.

### 1. From Ownership to Stewardship

The world often teaches us to think in terms of possession.

“My money.”

“My business.”

“My position.”

“My talent.”

“My success.”

“My influence.”

But Scripture gives us a different perspective.

> “The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it.”  
> — Psalm 24:1

If everything belongs to the Lord, then even the things we call “ours” are ultimately received from Him.

We may possess something legally in human society, but before God, we remain stewards.

This does not mean we should neglect responsible ownership in ordinary life.

It means we recognize the **ultimate Owner**.

The house may be entrusted to us.

The business may be entrusted to us.

The salary may be entrusted to us.

The ability may be entrusted to us.

The opportunity may be entrusted to us.

But ultimately:

**The King owns.  
The steward manages.**

### 2. From Entitlement to Gratitude

An owner mindset can easily produce entitlement.

A person begins to think:

“I deserve this.”

“I earned this.”

“This is mine.”

“I can do whatever I want.”

But a steward remembers where everything came from.

Paul asked:

> “What do you have that God hasn’t given you?”  
> — 1 Corinthians 4:7

That question turns entitlement into gratitude.

Instead of saying:

**“Why don't I have more?”**

the steward learns to say:

**“Thank You for what You have entrusted to me.”**

Gratitude does not mean we stop growing.

It means we grow without forgetting the Giver.

We can pursue greater responsibility while remaining thankful for what is already in our hands.

### 3. From Consumption to Purpose

An owner mindset can ask:

**“How much can I get?”**

A steward asks:

**“What purpose does this serve?”**

Money becomes more than something to spend.

Time becomes more than something to consume.

Talent becomes more than something to display.

Influence becomes more than something to enjoy.

Resources become tools for Kingdom purposes.

Jesus taught:

> “Use your worldly resources to benefit others and make friends.”  
> — Luke 16:9

The principle is not that every resource must be given away.

The principle is that resources should be used with **purpose**.

A steward does not simply consume what is entrusted.

A steward seeks to multiply its usefulness.

### 4. From Independence to Dependence

Stewardship requires humility because the steward cannot operate independently of the King.

Jesus said:

> “Apart from me you can do nothing.”  
> — John 15:5

This does not mean we become passive.

It means our activity must remain connected to God.

We plan, but we seek His direction.

We work, but we recognize His provision.

We develop our abilities, but we remember their source.

We make decisions, but we seek His wisdom.

The steward works diligently while remaining dependent on the King.

**Dependence is not weakness.**

It is recognition of the true Source.

### 5. From Self-Promotion to Kingdom Purpose

Influence can easily change the heart.

A person may begin serving God but eventually start building a personal name.

Jesus warned against doing good simply to receive recognition from people.

> “Don’t do your good deeds publicly, to be admired by others.”  
> — Matthew 6:1

The steward asks:

**“Does this glorify the King?”**

rather than:

**“Will this make people notice me?”**

This is especially important when the King entrusts leadership or influence.

The more visible the steward becomes, the more carefully the steward must guard the heart.

The platform is not the purpose.

The position is not the purpose.

The influence is not the purpose.

**The King is the purpose.**

### 6. From Possession to Responsibility

When we think like owners, we naturally focus on our rights.

When we think like stewards, we focus on our responsibilities.

An owner may ask:

**“What am I entitled to?”**

A steward asks:

**“What am I responsible for?”**

This is one reason stewardship produces maturity.

Instead of constantly asking what we can receive, we begin asking what we can faithfully carry.

Instead of asking who should serve us, we ask whom we can serve.

Instead of asking what we can gain from a position, we ask what good we can accomplish through it.

Jesus demonstrated this perfectly.

He said:

> “For even the Son of Man came not to be served but to serve others.”  
> — Mark 10:45

Kingdom leadership is therefore not centered on privilege.

It is centered on responsibility.

### 7. The Steward's Daily Question

A steward's mindset can be summarized in one daily question:

**“King, what do You want me to do with what You have placed in my hands today?”**

That question can apply to everything.

With money:

**“How should I manage this?”**

With time:

**“How should I use today?”**

With talent:

**“How can I develop and use this for Your glory?”**

With opportunity:

**“How can I respond faithfully?”**

With influence:

**“How can I serve others?”**

With relationships:

**“How can I love and honor these people?”**

With authority:

**“How can I lead through service?”**

The steward does not need to know everything about tomorrow.

The steward needs to be faithful with today's assignment.

## Kingdom Principle

**A steward's mindset begins when we stop viewing life primarily through the question of “What is mine?” and begin viewing it through the question of “What has the King entrusted to me?”**

This changes entitlement into gratitude.

Consumption into purpose.

Independence into dependence.

Self-promotion into Kingdom purpose.

Possession into responsibility.

The external circumstances may not change immediately.

But the heart of the steward changes.

And when the mindset changes, stewardship begins to change with it.

## Heart Check

Ask yourself:

**Am I living more like an owner or like a steward?**

When I receive something, do I immediately think about what I can get from it?

Or do I ask what purpose the King may have for it?

When I receive more, do I become entitled?

Or more grateful?

When I receive influence, do I build my own name?

Or do I use it to serve?

When I receive responsibility, do I focus on my rights?

Or on my responsibility?

The answers reveal the mindset behind our stewardship.

## Faithful Response

King, change the way I think.

Teach me to see everything through the eyes of a steward.

When I am tempted to say, “This is mine,” remind me that everything ultimately belongs to You.

When I become entitled, teach me gratitude.

When I become focused on consumption, remind me of purpose.

When I become independent, draw me back to dependence on You.

When I seek recognition, turn my heart back toward Your glory.

When I focus on my rights, remind me of my responsibility.

I want my life to reflect the mindset of a faithful steward.

So today I ask:

**King, what have You placed in my hands, and how can I faithfully use it for You?**

## Bible Foundation

- Psalm 24:1 — Everything ultimately belongs to the Lord.
- 1 Corinthians 4:7 — Everything we have is received from God.
- Luke 16:9 — Resources can be used purposefully for the benefit of others.
- John 15:5 — Fruitful living depends on remaining connected to Christ.
- Matthew 6:1 — Kingdom service should not be driven by the desire for human recognition.
- Mark 10:45 — Jesus models servant-hearted leadership.
- 1 Corinthians 4:2 — The essential requirement of a steward is faithfulness.
