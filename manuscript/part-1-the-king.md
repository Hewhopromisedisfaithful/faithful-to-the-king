# PART I — THE KING

## Chapter 1 — Everything Belongs to the King

Before we can understand stewardship, we must first understand ownership.

A steward can only understand his responsibility when he understands who the true owner is.

The Bible begins with a truth that changes the way we see everything:

> “The earth is the Lord’s, and everything in it. The world and all its people belong to him.”
>
> — Psalm 24:1

Everything begins with God.

The earth belongs to Him.

Its resources belong to Him.

Our lives belong to Him.

Our abilities, opportunities, possessions, and influence ultimately come from Him.

We may hold things in our hands, but holding something does not necessarily mean owning it.

That is the foundation of biblical stewardship.

### The King Is the Owner

In an ancient kingdom, a king could entrust land, wealth, authority, or responsibility to a servant.

The servant could manage it.

The servant could make decisions concerning it.

The servant could even have authority over other people because of what the king had entrusted to him.

But there was one thing he could never forget:

**He was managing what belonged to the king.**

The same principle helps us understand our relationship with God.

God is not merely someone who gives us things.

He is the rightful Owner of everything.

David recognized this when he prayed:

> “Everything we have has come from you, and we give you only what you first gave us!”
>
> — 1 Chronicles 29:14

This changes our perspective.

We do not begin with the question:

**“What belongs to me?”**

We begin with:

**“What has the King entrusted to me?”**

### The King Owns Everything

The Bible repeatedly reminds us that God owns what we often consider ours.

Psalm 24:1 declares that the earth and everything in it belong to the Lord.

Haggai 2:8 says:

> “The silver is mine, and the gold is mine.”

And 1 Chronicles 29:11 declares that everything in heaven and earth belongs to God.

These passages establish a simple but powerful truth:

**God is the Owner.**

Our possessions may have our name attached to them.

Our salary may be deposited into our account.

Our house may carry our name on its documents.

Our talents may feel like personal abilities.

Our time may feel completely under our control.

But from a Kingdom perspective, everything ultimately comes from the King.

### We Are Stewards, Not Owners

A steward is someone entrusted with the management of something that belongs to another.

This distinction is important.

An owner asks:

**“What do I want to do with what is mine?”**

A steward asks:

**“What does the owner want me to do with what He entrusted to me?”**

That is the difference between an earthly mindset and a Kingdom mindset.

When we believe everything belongs to us, we naturally become focused on possession.

But when we understand that everything belongs to God, we begin to think about responsibility.

Our money becomes a responsibility.

Our time becomes a responsibility.

Our talents become a responsibility.

Our opportunities become a responsibility.

Our relationships become a responsibility.

Our influence becomes a responsibility.

Even leadership becomes a responsibility.

The question changes from:

**“How much do I have?”**

to:

**“How faithfully am I managing what God has placed in my hands?”**

### The King Does Not Entrust Everything Equally

Not every person receives the same amount.

One person may receive greater financial resources.

Another may receive greater talent.

Another may receive a wider influence.

Another may receive a position of leadership.

Another may have only a small opportunity that seems insignificant to everyone else.

But the Kingdom principle is not that everyone receives the same thing.

The principle is that **everyone is responsible for what has been entrusted to them.**

Jesus illustrated this in the Parable of the Talents.

One servant received five talents.

Another received two.

Another received one.

They did not receive equal amounts.

But each servant was expected to respond faithfully to what had been placed in his hands.

This teaches us something important:

**God's expectation is connected to what He has entrusted.**

We should not spend our lives comparing our assignment with someone else's assignment.

The five-talent servant was not responsible for the two talents given to another servant.

The two-talent servant was not responsible for the five.

Each one had his own stewardship.

### Faithfulness, Not Comparison

Comparison can distract a steward from his assignment.

We may look at another person's money and think we should have more.

We may look at another person's talent and wish we had theirs.

We may look at another person's opportunities and wonder why our own seem smaller.

But the Kingdom does not begin with comparison.

It begins with faithfulness.

Jesus said:

> “If you are faithful in little things, you will be faithful in large ones.”
>
> — Luke 16:10

Small things matter in the Kingdom.

A small amount of money can be managed faithfully.

A small opportunity can be used faithfully.

A small responsibility can be carried faithfully.

A small influence can be used faithfully.

What appears small in the eyes of people may still be significant in the eyes of the King.

### The Widow's Two Coins

Jesus once watched people giving their offerings at the Temple.

Many wealthy people gave large amounts.

Then a poor widow came and gave two small coins.

From an earthly perspective, her gift appeared insignificant.

But Jesus noticed something others might have overlooked.

Her offering revealed the condition of her heart.

Her two coins were small in value, but her faithfulness was not small.

This reminds us that stewardship is not ultimately measured by how much we possess.

It is measured by how faithfully we respond to what we have been given.

The King sees beyond the size of the gift.

He sees the heart of the steward.

### What Has the King Entrusted to You?

Perhaps the King has entrusted you with money.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with a family.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with a talent.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with a business.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with a ministry.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with leadership.

Perhaps He has entrusted you with influence.

Perhaps what He has given you seems very small.

Whatever it is, the important question is not whether it looks impressive.

The important question is:

**Am I being faithful with it?**

Paul wrote:

> “Now, a person who is put in charge as a manager must be faithful.”
>
> — 1 Corinthians 4:2

Notice what Scripture emphasizes.

It does not say that the manager must be famous.

It does not say that the manager must be powerful.

It does not say that the manager must possess the most resources.

It says:

**He must be faithful.**

That is the heart of Kingdom stewardship.

### A Kingdom Mindset

When we understand that God owns everything, our entire perspective begins to change.

We stop saying:

**“This is mine.”**

And begin asking:

**“How can I honor God with this?”**

We stop asking:

**“How much can I keep?”**

And begin asking:

**“How can I faithfully manage what He has entrusted?”**

We stop comparing our portion with someone else's.

And we begin faithfully serving with our own assignment.

We stop seeing responsibility as merely a burden.

And we begin seeing it as a trust from the King.

Because stewardship is not simply about managing possessions.

**It is about honoring the One who entrusted them to us.**

## Bible Foundation

The central teaching of this chapter is supported by these passages:

- **Psalm 24:1** — Everything in the earth ultimately belongs to the Lord.
- **1 Chronicles 29:11–14** — God owns greatness, power, wealth, and everything in heaven and earth.
- **Haggai 2:8** — Silver and gold belong to God.
- **Matthew 25:14–30** — The Parable of the Talents teaches responsibility and faithfulness with what the Master entrusts.
- **Luke 16:10** — Faithfulness in small things matters.
- **1 Corinthians 4:2** — Those entrusted with responsibility are required to be faithful.

---

## Kingdom Principle

**The King owns everything.  
The King entrusts.  
The steward manages.  
The steward must remain faithful.**
